### `V32-DEC-001` — Which assistant modes are in the first release?

- **Question:** Customer assistant, professional assistant, or both?
- **Alternatives:** (a) customer only; (b) professional only; (c) both.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) customer only in the first release, professional second.**
- **Reasoning:** `bc_use_ai_assistant` already exists and is already granted to the `customer`
  role; no professional capability exists at all. Customer mode reads Journey defaults and the
  public catalogue. Professional mode reads settlement and analytics facts and is therefore the
  strictest tenant-isolation surface in the platform — `V3_SECURITY_MODEL.md` §4 names AI as
  the domain most likely to be asked, in natural language, for another tenant's numbers.
  Shipping the smaller blast radius first is not a scope cut; it is sequencing the adversarial
  test surface.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — (a) customer only.** No professional-AI capability is created and
  `bc_use_professional_ai` does not exist. Professional mode is DEFERRED to a later phase with
  its own decision, its own table keyed on the party, and its own adversarial test set.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A
- **Consequence of deferral:** the phase cannot decide whether to create
  `bc_use_professional_ai`, and the context-port list in `V32-DEC-005` is undefined.

### `V32-DEC-002` — Conversation cardinality

- **Question:** One persistent thread per user, unbounded conversation history, or bounded
  sessions?
- **Alternatives:** (a) one thread per user, V2's `UNIQUE(user_id)` shape; (b) unbounded
  multiple conversations; (c) bounded sessions — multiple conversations, each closed after an
  inactivity horizon, with a per-user cap on retained conversations.
- **Recommendation:** **(c) bounded sessions.**
- **Reasoning:** (a) is `GAP-12`, which `V3_MIGRATION_MATRIX.md` says to revisit rather than
  re-adopt; its real defect is that an accumulating context blob is an unbounded prompt, an
  unbounded cost, an unbounded retention obligation, and an unbounded injection surface,
  because text typed six months ago is still replayed to the provider today. (b) is the most
  familiar product shape and the most expensive privacy commitment: every message is
  subject-authored free text that ADR-027 requires to be exported on request and destroyed on
  erasure, at unbounded volume. (c) keeps findable history while making retention, cost, and
  prompt size finite by construction rather than by a sweep somebody has to remember to build.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — (c) bounded sessions**, with these numbers binding:
  multiple conversations per customer; a session closes after **24 hours** of inactivity;
  conversation history is retained **30 days**; at most **20** retained conversations per
  customer; when the cap is reached the **oldest closed** conversation and its messages may be
  destroyed before a new session is created; an **active** session is never silently evicted —
  if the cap cannot be satisfied without touching one, the request is refused; a closed session
  is never reopened, and continuing produces a new session.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A (this is the schema)
- **Consequence of deferral:** no `ai` schema can be written. Choosing later means a migration
  that changes a unique key on a live conversation table — the exact regression risk V2's own
  professional-AI migration refused to take.

### `V32-DEC-003` — Conversation naming, history, archive, delete

- **Question:** May a user name, archive, or delete a conversation, and what does delete mean?
- **Alternatives:** (a) none of it — history is read-only; (b) delete only; (c) name, archive,
  and delete.
- **Recommendation:** **(b) delete only**, with delete meaning immediate destruction of the
  messages, not a hidden flag.
- **Reasoning:** naming is meaningless under `V32-DEC-002`(a) and low-value under (c), where an
  auto-derived first-message label is enough. A delete control is different in kind: it is the
  user's only in-product way to withdraw free text they regret typing, and refusing it while
  offering a full-account erasure route is a poor trade. A soft-delete flag would leave the
  text in the table and make the privacy claim false.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — (b) delete only.** Users may permanently delete individual
  conversations, and deletion destroys the messages immediately. **No** user naming, **no**
  archive, and **no** soft-delete presented as deletion.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A frontend and the export contract
- **Consequence of deferral:** ship (a) and add later; additive, low cost.

### `V32-DEC-004` — Supported and prohibited first-release outcomes

- **Question:** What may the assistant produce?
- **Alternatives:** (a) answer plus re-verified recommendations only; (b) plus draft text the
  user sends themselves; (c) plus pre-filled action links that carry the user to a normal,
  independently authorized flow.
- **Recommendation:** **(a), with (c) explicitly out of the first release.**
- **Reasoning:** (c) is not a mutation and is tempting for exactly that reason, but a
  pre-filled booking link is a recommendation that has already chosen a slot, and getting it
  wrong costs a customer a real appointment. (a) is the outcome V2 shipped and the one the
  output-validation rule can fully cover.
- **Prohibited, `FIXED`, not an alternative:** creating, changing, or cancelling a booking;
  moving or refunding money; changing identity, privacy, consent, or moderation state; writing
  a review; mutating any business record. `V3_SECURITY_MODEL.md` §5 and `V3.2_PRODUCT_ROADMAP.md`
  §11 both state this, and every such mutation in V3 already requires a session-resolved,
  capability-gated, audit-enforced user action.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — (a) answers plus re-verified public professional and service
  recommendations only.** (c) is explicitly out. Prohibited and structural, not merely
  unimplemented: AI-initiated mutation of any kind; booking creation, change, or cancellation;
  preselected booking slots; payment or refund actions; identity, privacy, consent, moderation,
  review, CRM, business, or financial mutation; and action execution disguised as a
  recommendation.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A
- **Consequence of deferral:** the output schema and the validation layer have no target.

### `V32-DEC-005` — The allow-listed context source list

- **Question:** Exactly which domains may contribute context, and through which typed method?
- **Alternatives:** (a) Journey only; (b) Journey plus provider and search summaries;
  (c) (b) plus analytics and financial for professional mode.
- **Recommendation:** **(b) for customer mode, (c) for professional mode** — with the exact
  ports named in `V3.2_PHASE_0_DISCOVERY.md` §7.2 and nothing else.
- **Reasoning:** each named method is already single-party by construction:
  `inferAiDefaults(userId)` has no string field, `MetricsService.forProvider` has no
  multi-party overload, and `MyFinanceService` resolves the party from the session and accepts
  no party argument. Adding a source means adding a typed port, which is a reviewable act.
- **Excluded, `FIXED`, not an alternative:** Journey `notes` and goal titles; review comments
  and professional replies; moderator reasons; CRM notes; every human chat message;
  verification evidence; direct identifiers.
- **Owner:** OWNER (to ratify the list) / ENGINEERING (to enforce it structurally)
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — the customer allow-list is ratified as exactly four sources:**
  string-free Journey inference and defaults; public professional summaries; public service
  summaries; approved public search summaries. Structurally excluded, with no port able to
  return them: Journey notes and free-text goal titles; review comments and professional
  replies; CRM notes; internal chat messages; moderation reasons; verification evidence; direct
  identifiers; financial and professional analytics; and arbitrary database queries. The
  professional-mode row of the §7.2 table is **not** ratified and does not apply — see
  `V32-DEC-001`.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A
- **Consequence of deferral:** context assembly has no boundary, which is the single failure the
  whole AI security model exists to prevent.

### `V32-DEC-006` — Consent to use the assistant

- **Question:** Does using the assistant require an explicit, recorded consent, or does the
  capability grant plus in-product disclosure suffice?
- **Alternatives:** (a) capability plus disclosure; (b) a one-time recorded acceptance;
  (c) a versioned consent record with withdrawal, pulling V3.3-E's consent audit trail forward.
- **Recommendation:** **(b)**, as the smallest thing that produces a record.
- **Reasoning:** (a) leaves no evidence that anyone was told anything, which is a weak position
  if the question is ever asked. (c) is a real feature the catalog schedules at V3.3-E, and
  pulling it forward for one surface would build a consent system for a single consumer.
- **Owner:** OWNER + LEGAL
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — (b) a one-time recorded acceptance**, required before the first
  assistant use. This phase implements the evidence record and the enforcement contract only.
  **No final Persian legal or disclosure copy is authorized in this backend phase**, and legal
  review of the customer-facing disclosure remains OPEN before public release. The
  platform-wide versioned consent system stays scheduled at V3.3-E and must not be built here.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A first customer-facing release
- **Consequence of deferral:** ships without a consent record; retrofitting one later means
  re-prompting every existing user.

### `V32-DEC-007` — AI conversation retention, deletion, export, and subject-data disposition

- **Question:** How long are AI messages kept, what does account erasure do to them, and what
  appears in an export?
- **Alternatives:** (a) `subject_data`, destroyed on erasure, exported in full including
  assistant replies; (b) `subject_data`, destroyed on erasure, export limited to the user's own
  messages; (c) a fixed retention period with a sweep, plus either export shape.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) plus a retention period from `V32-DEC-002`'s session horizon.**
- **Reasoning:** unlike chat, an AI conversation has no counterpart with an interest, so
  ADR-027's default applies cleanly. The assistant's replies are not the subject's words but
  they are entirely about the subject and are meaningless without them, so excluding them
  produces an export nobody can read.
- **Owner:** OWNER + LEGAL
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — (a) with the retention period from `V32-DEC-002`.** AI conversations
  are `subject_data`. Export returns the **complete readable conversation**, customer messages
  and assistant replies alike. Account erasure destroys all AI conversations, messages,
  recommendation records, and consent linkage. Retention is **30 days**. Individual conversation
  deletion is immediate and permanent. **No counterpart-retention exception exists** for AI
  conversations.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A — the boot check will not pass without a disposition
- **Consequence of deferral:** the application cannot start with an `ai` schema present.

### `V32-DEC-008` — Quotas, rate limits, and the cost envelope

- **Question:** How many assistant messages may one user send per day, and what total spend is
  authorized?
- **Alternatives:** (a) a per-user daily message cap only; (b) a cap plus a platform-wide daily
  spend ceiling that disables the real provider and falls back honestly when reached;
  (c) unmetered.
- **Recommendation:** **(b)**, with the counter incremented in the same transaction as the
  message insert.
- **Reasoning:** (c) is an unbounded bill on an external meter. The transactional counter avoids
  the read-then-write race that `GAP-04` records in V2's campaign caps, and it avoids depending
  on the unresolved `THROTTLE-STORE` topology, because a PostgreSQL row is shared across
  instances and an in-memory throttler is not. Reaching the ceiling must produce the honest
  fallback state, never a silent degradation the user cannot see.
- **Owner:** OWNER (the numbers) / ENGINEERING (the mechanism)
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — the sandbox quota is (a) with the mechanism from (b).** Binding
  numbers: **20 accepted customer messages per user per Tehran calendar day**, enforced in
  PostgreSQL and incremented atomically in the same transaction as the accepted message.
  A read-then-write quota is prohibited. The deterministic fallback has zero external cost and
  **still obeys the quota**. Usage and provider mode are recorded honestly.
  **STILL OPEN:** the platform-wide monetary spend ceiling is DEFERRED until a real provider and
  a pricing model are selected, and **a real provider must not be enabled while it remains
  undecided.**
- **Blocks:** V3.2-A external gate
- **Consequence of deferral:** the sandbox milestone still lands; a real provider cannot be
  enabled without a spend ceiling.

### `V32-DEC-009` — Support and operator access to AI conversation content

- **Question:** May a privileged operator read a user's AI conversation?
- **Alternatives:** (a) no content access at all — counts, health, and cost only; (b) content
  access on a user-initiated support request, time-bounded and audited; (c) a moderation queue.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) in the first release.**
- **Reasoning:** an AI thread can contain a customer's stated beauty and health concerns.
  `V3.2_PLUS_CAPABILITY_CATALOG.md` schedules least-privilege support access at V3.3-E and keeps
  impersonation `RETIRED`. Granting a broad read now, before that framework exists, is the
  larger commitment.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-29 — (a) no content access.** No support or operator access to AI
  conversation content exists in the first release. Operators see aggregate health, counts, and
  cost only. There is **no** content-reading admin route, **no** impersonation, and **no**
  moderation queue for AI conversations.
- **Blocks:** nothing if (a); V3.2-A if (b) or (c)
- **Consequence of deferral:** (a) applies by default, which is the safe direction.

### `V32-DEC-010` — First-release participant matrix

- **Question:** Which pairings may hold a conversation?
- **Alternatives:** (a) customer to professional only; (b) customer to professional and
  customer to business; (c) (b) plus a named staff member; (d) (c) plus staff groups and
  platform support.
- **Recommendation:** **(b).**
- **Reasoning:** (c) exposes an individual employee's identity to customers and needs the staff
  role matrix that is itself unresolved at V3.3-C. (d) additionally needs per-participant read
  state, group membership lifecycle, and a leave/remove model — none of which any repository
  evidence asks for. (b) covers the two counterparties the booking model already knows about.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (b) customer↔professional and customer↔business**, with the
  counterparty **derived, never chosen**. Named-staff, group, and platform-support conversations
  are OUT. The counterparty is resolved **exclusively from the immutable historical seller-party
  snapshot on the booking's commerce order** (`commerce.orders.seller_party_type/seller_party_id`
  where `source_type='booking'`), recorded on the conversation at creation and **never moved** if
  the professional later changes salon. **There is no fallback to current `business_staff`
  affiliation**: a missing historical order or snapshot **fails closed**. A business conversation
  belongs to the business, not to a staff user; for this milestone **inbox access is the business
  owner and active managers only** — ordinary `staff`, including the booked practitioner when
  their role is only `staff`, get no automatic access, and broader or practitioner-specific
  permissions are DEFERRED to the V3.3-C role matrix. Read state is a monotonic watermark on the
  participant/access row.
  **Product acknowledgement recorded:** where the historical seller party is a salon, the
  conversation belongs to the salon and may be read by its owner or active managers, and not
  necessarily by the practitioner who delivered the service.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-B (this is the schema)
- **Consequence of deferral:** no conversation table can be written, and choosing group chat
  later on a two-party schema means re-modelling read state (see `V3.2_PHASE_0_DISCOVERY.md`
  `F-07`).

### `V32-DEC-011` — Is a booking required to start a conversation?

- **Question:** May a customer message a professional before any booking exists?
- **Alternatives:** (a) a real booking relationship is required; (b) pre-booking messaging is
  allowed, with blocking and reporting shipped alongside it; (c) pre-booking messaging allowed
  with a per-recipient daily cap on new conversations, plus (b)'s controls.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) for the first release.**
- **Reasoning:** this is the single highest-risk product decision in V3.2-B, and the repository
  contains direct evidence of the failure mode: V2's `start_or_get` rejected only
  self-conversation, so any logged-in holder of `bc_send_message` could open a thread with any
  user id — a harassment and user-enumeration surface with no counterweight. Requiring a booking
  costs nothing, because `booking` already knows the relationship. The commercial argument for
  (b) is real — pre-booking questions plainly drive bookings — which is why this is the owner's
  call and not engineering's.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (a) a proven real booking relationship is required**, with a
  security correction to the cancelled case. Qualifying: `confirmed`, `completed`, `no_show`, and
  `cancelled` **only when the append-only `booking.booking_history` proves the booking previously
  transitioned through `confirmed`**. A booking cancelled directly from `pending` **never**
  qualifies. `pending` and `expired` never qualify. A refund does not remove eligibility once
  prior confirmation is proven. Either legitimate participant may initiate. Multiple qualifying
  bookings collapse into **one** conversation per immutable historical counterparty. Eligibility
  is **re-evaluated inside the send transaction**; an existing conversation row is never evidence
  of eligibility. Missing, foreign, and ineligible targets stay indistinguishable.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-B
- **Consequence of deferral:** if (b) is later chosen and shipped without blocking and
  reporting, the platform reproduces V2's defect knowingly.

### `V32-DEC-012` — Post-booking access duration

- **Question:** For how long after a booking may participants read, and for how long may they
  send?
- **Alternatives:** (a) both indefinite; (b) read indefinite, send disabled a fixed period after
  the last booking ends; (c) both expire.
- **Recommendation:** **(b).**
- **Reasoning:** read and write are different questions. A customer looking up what a
  professional told them about aftercare a year later is a legitimate need; an open write
  channel to a stranger a year after a single appointment is the pre-booking risk arriving by a
  slower route. (c) destroys a record both parties may need.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (b) read stays available** until retention or account erasure
  removes the content; **sending closes 90 days** after `MAX(booking.slot_end)` across qualifying
  bookings with that immutable counterparty. Absolute **UTC** instant arithmetic, no calendar
  boundary. A future or newer qualifying booking reopens sending. The window is **recomputed per
  send** and the authorization result is never cached on the conversation row.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-B eligibility logic
- **Consequence of deferral:** (a) applies by default, which is the least safe direction.

### `V32-DEC-013` — Chat retention and what account erasure does to the counterpart's copy

- **Question:** How long are messages kept, and when one participant erases their account, what
  happens to the other participant's copy?
- **Alternatives:** (a) retain both copies indefinitely, export only the subject's own messages
  — V2's answer; (b) destroy the subject's authored messages, leaving the counterpart's side
  intact but one-sided; (c) destroy the entire conversation for both parties; (d) any of these
  plus a fixed retention period and a sweep.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) plus a retention period**, i.e. (a)+(d).
- **Reasoning:** V2 chose (a) explicitly, reasoning that the counterpart professional has a
  legitimate ongoing interest, and applied the same reasoning to CRM notes and to the privacy
  policy's own wording. ADR-027 §3 reaches the same place for review ratings: a record that is
  a fact about the *other* party survives. (b) produces a conversation that reads as though one
  side never spoke, which is worse than either extreme. (c) destroys a professional's business
  record because an unrelated person closed their account.
- **Owner:** OWNER + LEGAL
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — the ADR-027-CONSISTENT option, NOT the proposed exception.**
  Prose authored by the erased subject is **destroyed**. Only a neutral structural placeholder
  remains where sequence and thread coherence require one, containing **no original body, no
  excerpt, no searchable text and nothing reconstructable**. Messages authored by the surviving
  counterparty are preserved. The erased identity is tombstoned as «کاربر حذف‌شده». Retention
  stays **24 months** from `chat.conversations.last_message_at`, swept by **hard delete and
  cascade** of the whole expired conversation. Export contains only the subject's own
  **still-existing** authored messages plus permitted conversation metadata. Blocks and
  quota/throttle counters belonging to the erased subject are destroyed. Moderation reports may
  retain the minimum decision record with the reporter tombstoned, consistent with existing
  moderation and audit policy. **No claim is made that retained prose is anonymised, because
  subject-authored prose is not retained.** This choice deliberately avoids introducing an
  exception to ADR-027. Approved legal disclosure copy remains an external **release** gate and
  does **not** block this deterministic backend implementation.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-B — ADR-027 requires the disposition and a stated reason before boot
- **Consequence of deferral:** the application cannot start with a `chat` schema present, and
  engineering would otherwise have to invent the legal reasoning to make the boot check pass.

### `V32-DEC-014` — Blocking, reporting, and the moderation queue

- **Question:** Which of blocking, reporting, and a moderation queue ship in the first release?
- **Alternatives:** (a) none; (b) reporting only; (c) blocking and reporting; (d) blocking,
  reporting, and a moderation queue with a new `bc_moderate_chat` capability.
- **Recommendation:** **(d) if `V32-DEC-011` allows pre-booking messaging; (c) if it does not.**
- **Reasoning:** `media.abuse_reports` plus its report, queue, and decide routes already exist
  under `bc_moderate_media` and are the model to mirror rather than reinvent. A new capability
  rather than a widened one, because reviewing a private conversation and taking down a public
  portfolio image are different privileges.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (d) blocking, reporting, and the moderation queue ship
  together.** Reporting-without-a-queue is **rejected** as an option. Blocking is stored
  directionally but **disables sending for both sides**; the blocked party is **not told who
  blocked them**; history stays readable; only the blocker may unblock. Report reasons:
  `harassment`, `spam`, `scam_or_fraud`, `explicit`, `personal_data`, `off_platform_payment`,
  `other`. At most **5** new reports per reporter per rolling 24 hours, and **one open report per

  messages. An upheld report may warn the sender, permanently close the conversation for sending,
  or restrict the sender's platform-wide chat sending.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-B scope
- **Consequence of deferral:** (a) applies, leaving a messaging product with no abuse response.

### `V32-DEC-015` — Privileged support access to chat content

- **Alternatives:** (a) reported threads only, audited; (b) any conversation on a stated

### `V32-DEC-017` — Referral expiry and reversal on cancellation or refund

- **Question:** Does a pending referral expire, and is a reward reversed when the qualifying
  booking is cancelled or refunded?
- **Alternatives:** (a) no expiry, no reversal — V2's behaviour; (b) expiry only; (c) reversal
  only; (d) both.
- **Recommendation:** **(d).**
- **Reasoning:** V2 had neither, and the reversal half is the sharper gap: a booking that
  completed and was then refunded left the reward standing, which is a free-points loop for
  anyone willing to book and cancel. `OrderRefunded` already exists and is already consumed, so
  the trigger is available. Expiry bounds an otherwise permanent pending state. Note the
  adjacency to the still-open loyalty clawback question on hidden reviews — both are "an award
  survives the thing that earned it being undone" and would benefit from one answer.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (d) both expiry and reversal.**
  **Expiry:** a pending referral expires **90 days** after attribution. Time is an **absolute UTC
  duration** measured through an injected clock, so the boundary is testable rather than waited
  for — chat's discipline, not the AI quota's calendar-day one.
  **Reversal:** only a **FULL refund of the qualifying booking's commerce order** reverses the
  rewards. A **partial** refund does **not** reverse. A `duplicate_charge` correction **never**
  reverses — the order was legitimately paid once and the service happened. There is **no time
  limit** on reversal. Redelivery of the refund event must be idempotent.
  **Ledger semantics, binding and shared with `V32-DEC-032`:** a reversal is a **new negative row
  under a distinct reason**, never a mutation of the original — the ledger stays append-only. A
  clawback **may drive the spendable balance negative** and the balance is **never clamped at
  zero**, because clamping converts "book, get referred, spend the points, refund" into a working
  exploit. A clawback **does not reduce lifetime earned and does not demote a tier**, matching the
  platform's existing and deliberate rule that redemption never demotes. The consequence is
  accepted knowingly: a reversed referral leaves the referee tier-qualified on points they no
  longer hold.
  **Correction to the dependency ledger:** Phase F blocker 15 (cancellation and refund policy)
  decides **when a refund is granted**. It does **not** block referral from reacting to a full
  refund that has already been authoritatively recorded. The reversal story is **buildable, not
  externally blocked**, and must not carry `gate:external` or `status:blocked`.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-C
- **Consequence of deferral:** a known abuse loop ships.

### `V32-DEC-018` — Qualifying event

- **Question:** What qualifies a referral?
- **Alternatives:** (a) first `BookingCompleted`; (b) first completed booking or first paid
  non-booking order, whichever comes first — V2's rule; (c) registration.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) for the first release**, widening to (b) if a non-booking order type
  ever exists in V3.
- **Reasoning:** (c) is explicitly refused by V2's own design note and by the roadmap. (b) is
  the correct long-term rule but V3 has no shop-order equivalent today, so it would add an
  unreachable branch.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (a) the referee's first `BookingCompleted`, and nothing
  else.** Registration never qualifies. `BookingConfirmed` never qualifies — the service is still
  in the future. **`OrderPaid` never qualifies**, and this is the sharper refusal: money moves

  honestly cover.

### `V32-DEC-019` — Duplicate-account and device abuse policy

- **Question:** Beyond self-referral and once-per-referee, what abuse controls apply?
- **Alternatives:** (a) database constraints only; (b) plus a per-referrer cap on rewarded
  referrals per period; (c) plus device or network fingerprinting.
- **Recommendation:** **(b).**
- **Reasoning:** self-referral prevention and once-per-referee are already database constraints
  rather than policy, and are strong. (c) introduces a tracking capability with its own privacy
  and consent consequences that nothing in the repository has approved. (b) bounds the payout
  without collecting anything new.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (b) database constraints plus a per-referrer cap, and
  explicitly NOT (c).**

  **Enforced by the database, not by policy.** Self-referral is **unrepresentable**; a person may
  be attributed **once, ever**; **one referral code per owner**; **one reward per referral per
  side**; qualification is **compare-and-swap guarded and replay-safe**, because the outbox is
  at-least-once by design and redelivery is the steady state rather than an exception.

  **The cap.** **10 qualified referrals per referrer per Tehran calendar month.** **No lifetime
  cap.** Enforced by a **PostgreSQL transactional counter** — a single conditional
  `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE … WHERE count < limit RETURNING` — never a read-then-write and
  never the in-memory HTTP throttler, whose effective limit multiplies by instance count while
  `THROTTLE-STORE` is unresolved. A read-then-write cap is `GAP-04` reproduced knowingly.

  **Owner correction to the engineering packet — the two sides are capped independently.** When
  the referrer reaches the cap: the referral **still qualifies and is recorded**; the **referrer**
  side is marked `capped` and receives **no** referrer reward; the **referee** reward remains
  **independently eligible** and is paid. Both grants must **not** be skipped merely because the
  inviter reached their cap. An invited customer must never lose their own approved reward because
  of somebody else's activity.

  **Signals not collected.** **No device, IP, browser, or fingerprint identity signal is collected
  for referral in this milestone.** (c) introduces a tracking capability with its own privacy and
  consent consequences that nothing in the repository has approved, and the durable material does
  not exist anyway: `identity.otp_requests.request_ip` belongs to the OTP flow rather than the
  account and is destroyed on erasure, and `refresh_tokens.user_agent` / `device_label` are
  client-supplied, per-session, and likewise destroyed.

  **Accepted gap, recorded rather than papered over.** Account erasure rewrites `users.phone` to a
  tombstone alias, so the original number becomes registrable again after the 168-hour
  (**7-day**) erasure grace window. Erase-then-re-register produces a genuinely new `user_id` with
  a genuinely new `created_at`, and **nothing detects it**. The monthly referrer cap is the
  bounded-exposure control, and this gap is accepted knowingly rather than claimed closed.

  **No manual-review queue, no appeal workflow, and no override route** is built. A capped or
  refused outcome may be recorded as an **enum only** on the referral row. Any future review
  surface is a privileged capability with mandatory audit coverage and is a separate decision.

  **Claim-route throttle: 10 attempts per authenticated caller per hour.** Every refusal on the
  claim route — unknown code, revoked code, the caller's own code, already attributed, account too
  old, already booked — returns **one indistinguishable response**, so the route is neither a code
  oracle nor an account oracle.

  **Attribution is immutable.** No route, service method, or admin path rewrites the referrer, the
  code, or the attribution instant; the once-per-referee unique constraint makes a second
  attribution **unwritable** rather than merely unimplemented — the same discipline chat applies to
  its immutable counterparty.

  **Referral subject-data dispositions, ratified here rather than left to implementation.** These
  are an explicit owner retention decision, not an engineering assumption:

  | Table | Disposition | Reason |
  |---|---|---|
  | `referral.referral_codes` | `subject_data` — **deleted/revoked** on the owner's erasure | An ownerless code must not remain claimable. |
  | `referral.referrals` | `retained`, with the **erased side's identity tombstoned** | The relationship record is what explains a **retained** loyalty ledger entry the other party still holds. Destroying it would leave a points row with no explanation. |
  | `referral.reward_grants` | `retained` | The audit explanation for retained loyalty rows. Retaining the ledger row while destroying the grant that explains it would be incoherent. |
  | `referral.referrer_counters` | `subject_data` — **deleted** on erasure | A rate-limit counter about a person who no longer exists, exactly as `chat.send_counters` is treated. |

  **Export shapes, binding.** A **referrer's** export may contain their **own** code and their own
  referral facts, and **no referee identity**. A **referee's** export contains their own referral
  fact and **never the referrer's bearer code**. No export reveals another party's phone, display
  name, or referral credential.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-C reward path
- **Consequence of deferral:** (a) applies; the exposure is bounded by the per-referral value.

### `V32-DEC-020` — Wishlist saveable targets and owning module

- **Question:** What may be saved, and which module owns the table?
- **Alternatives (targets):** (a) professionals only; (b) professionals and service offerings;
  (c) (b) plus businesses; (d) (c) plus portfolio items.
- **Alternatives (owner):** (i) `provider`, as the Phase 0 blueprint proposes; (ii) a small
  `wishlist` module and schema; (iii) `journey`.
- **Recommendation:** **(b)** for targets and **(ii) or (iii)** for the owner.
- **Reasoning on targets:** professionals and service offerings are both stable, both already
  public, and both already have a summary shape the profile and search surfaces use. Portfolio
  items are the most volatile targets and make the deleted-target question sharpest for the
  least product value.
- **Reasoning on ownership:** the blueprint's `provider.wishlist_items` is defensible because
  the targets are provider entities, and wrong on the merits because the rows are about a
  **customer**: their disposition is `subject_data` keyed on `user_id`, and placing them in
  `provider` hands that module an erasure obligation for a subject it otherwise knows nothing
  about. `journey` already owns the customer's own preference data and already deletes rather
  than anonymizes on erasure, which is exactly the right treatment here.
- **Owner:** OWNER (targets) / ENGINEERING with owner ratification (module)
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (b) professionals and service offerings only, owned by
  (ii) a new `wishlist` module and `wishlist` schema.**

  **Portfolio items are OUT**, and the reason is evidence rather than taste: `uq_portfolio_media_live`
  is partial on `deleted_at IS NULL`, so a professional who removes an image and re-adds the same
  media produces a **new item id**. Saved portfolio items would go unavailable during routine
  gallery maintenance — the sharpest deleted-target behaviour for the least product value.

  **Businesses are OUT, and not merely deferred: they are not implementable.** `services/business`
  exposes **no `@Public()` route anywhere** — `GET /businesses/:id` is ownership-guarded by
  `BusinessMembershipResolver` — and businesses have no document type in the search index, which is
  keyed on `professional_id`. Saving a business would require building a public business profile
  first. That is a separate capability, scoped nowhere, and it is **not** part of V3.2-C.

  **Ownership: not `provider`, and not `journey`.** The blueprint's `provider.wishlist_items` is
  refused on the merits already stated in this entry: the rows are about a **customer**, and placing
  them in `provider` hands that module an erasure obligation for a subject it otherwise knows nothing
  about. `journey` is refused for the opposite reason — it would work, but it would grow a second
  unrelated concern inside the module that owns beauty preferences and whose context port is a
  load-bearing AI privacy boundary. A small module owning a small schema keeps both properties.

  **Authoritative identifiers:** `provider.professionals.id` and `provider.services.id`. Nothing else
  is stored about the target — see `V32-DEC-021` on snapshots.

  **Duplicate saving is database-unrepresentable** through `UNIQUE (user_id, target_type, target_id)`.
  That constraint is also the idempotency guarantee for add: an `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`
  returns the same result whether or not the item was already saved, which is stronger than an
  application check.

  **Privacy:** wishlist rows are `subject_data`, **exported for their owner** and **hard deleted** on
  account erasure — the `journey` treatment, because a saved id is single-party and referenced by
  nobody, not the anonymize-with-referential-integrity default. This is boot-blocking under ADR-027
  and must ship with the migration.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-C wishlist
- **Consequence of deferral:** the schema cannot be written.

### `V32-DEC-021` — Deleted-target behaviour, and multiple lists

- **Question:** What does a saved entry do when its target is deleted, hidden, or suspended?
  And may a customer keep more than one list?
- **Alternatives (deleted target):** (a) the entry survives and renders as unavailable; (b) the
  entry is removed silently; (c) the entry is removed and the customer is notified.
- **Alternatives (multiple lists):** (i) one list; (ii) named collections.
- **Recommendation:** **(a)** and **(i)**.
- **Reasoning:** (b) is the option a customer reads as a bug — a saved professional that
  vanished with no trace. (c) sends a notification about a third party's status change, which
  leaks that status. On lists: named collections bring naming, ordering, and sharing questions,
  and nothing in the repository asks for them; one list is additive to expand later.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (a) the entry survives as a neutral tombstone, and (i) one
  list only.**

  **The question had to be re-phrased before it could be answered.** "Hidden" and "unpublished" do
  not correspond to any column: `provider.services` has no publish flag at all, and the only
  lifecycle on either table is `deleted_at` plus `provider.professionals.verification_status`.

  **The unavailable predicate, binding:** a saved target is unavailable when it is **soft-deleted**,
  or when its **owning professional is `suspended` or `revoked`**. A target whose professional is
  merely `unverified`, `pending`, or `rejected` is **NOT** unavailable, because
  `SearchService.searchProviders` filters only `is_deleted = false` and `verifiedOnly` is an optional
  caller filter — ordinary discovery still exposes those professionals, and a stricter wishlist rule
  would make the saved list contradict the search page the customer saved from. This deliberately
  differs from the AI assistant's `verified`-only re-verification rule (ADR-030 T3), because a
  recommendation is the platform vouching and a wishlist is the customer's own choice.

  **One neutral state.** The rendered state is a single `unavailable` value with **no reason**.
  Deleted, suspended, and revoked are **never** exposed as distinguishable causes, and no
  `verificationStatus` or `deletedAt` field crosses the contract. This is a security property, not a
  presentation preference: distinguishing them would turn every customer's wishlist into a live feed
  of moderation and verification decisions about named third parties.

  **Availability is computed per read and never cached on the row**, so a suspension that is later
  lifted restores the item with no write.

  **No notification of any kind** when a target becomes unavailable. Option (c) is refused because it
  discloses a third party's status change to somebody with no right to it.

  **Tombstone retention: indefinite**, until the customer removes it. No sweep — a sweep would delete
  a saved item nobody asked to remove.

  **Bounds:** at most **500** saved items per customer, enforced inside the insert transaction rather
  than by a preceding count. List pagination is keyset, default **20**, maximum **50**.

  **One list. No named collections**, no folders, no sharing, no public wishlists. Additive later.

  **No display snapshots.** No `display_name`, price, or image is copied onto the saved row.
  `provider` and `search` remain authoritative for public target data, the summary shapes already
  exist, and a snapshot would be a stale second copy of public data plus a subject-data claim over a
  third party's prose — bought to render exactly the name the neutral-tombstone rule forbids showing.

  **The wishlist produces no domain event, no outbox table, no notification, no analytics fact, no
  popularity count, no ranking signal, and no recommendation input.** `wishlist` is therefore **not**
  added to `ServiceName`. No consumer exists; `analytics.events` could not accept the fact without
  widening `ck_analytics_events_subject_normalized`; and a ranking signal would need an index
  mapping-version bump and a full reindex. Adding an event later is additive; shipping one nothing
  reads is not.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-C wishlist behaviour
- **Consequence of deferral:** low risk; (a) and (i) are safe defaults.

### `V32-DEC-033` — Referral share channel

**Created 2026-08-30**, and the creation is itself worth recording. This question was live from the
moment V3.2-C was scoped — GitHub issue #7 has asked it since the backlog was adopted — but the
register never carried a card for it. Its only repository record was the `REFERRAL-SHARE-CHANNEL`
row in `V3.2_EXTERNAL_DEPENDENCY_LEDGER.md` §3.6, which states an unblock condition rather than
offering alternatives, an owner, and a status. A dependency row is not a decision. **Nothing was
renumbered to make room for this entry; it is appended.**

- **Question:** How does a customer share an invite in the first milestone, without the platform
  silently acquiring an SMS, email, push, or social-network dependency?
- **Alternatives:** (a) in-product code only; (b) code plus a copyable invite link; (c) (b) plus
  native OS/browser share; (d) platform-sent SMS or email invites.
- **Recommendation:** **(c), with (d) externally gated.**
- **Reasoning:** (d) is the only option that needs a vendor, and the dependency ledger already
  records that referral is vendor-independent precisely when the platform does not send the invite
  itself. Native share needs no SDK, credential, or infrastructure — it is a browser API — so it does
  not belong in the gated column for dependency reasons. It belongs there for a **scope** reason,
  which is stated below rather than glossed.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — (c).** Binding:
  - **Copy the referral code** and **copy an invite link** are the approved first-milestone
    channels, and both are **unconditional fallbacks** that are always present.
  - **Native OS/browser share is approved as CONTRACT AND DESIGN ONLY.** This milestone builds no
    production frontend, so nothing ships that calls `navigator.share`. What ships is the share
    payload shape in the contract package — a title, a fixed-template text, and the url — and a
    design specification carrying the capability check and the copy fallback. The share text is
    assembled from a fixed template plus the code; it is not free text, is not stored, and contains
    no personal data.
  - **Invite link format: `{origin}/invite/{code}`.** It carries the code and nothing else — no user
    id, no phone, no display name, no signature.
  - **The invite link has no independent expiry.** Its validity follows the code and the referral
    lifecycle. A separate link expiry would add a third clock, a signing key, and a "this link
    expired but your code still works" state nobody can explain.
  - **No milestone artefact may state or imply that the platform sent an invitation.** The referrer
    sends it. A cancelled or aborted native share is a **user choice, not an error**, and must leave
    the copy controls untouched and show no failure message.
  - **No social SDK, no social provider, no share-tracking pixel.**
  - **Externally gated and explicitly not delivered:** platform-sent SMS (dependency ledger row 6),
    platform-sent email (row 7), push (V3.3-B / V3.4-A), and every social-network share API.
  - **Approved legal and disclosure copy for referral terms remains a PUBLIC-RELEASE gate** and does
    **not** block this backend milestone — the same boundary the owner set for V3.2-B.

  **Not a share channel: the referral event and notification boundary.** Recorded here because the
  engineering packet listed it inside the share-channel table, and the owner separated them; a
  lifecycle notification tells somebody what happened to their own referral, which is a different
  thing from a mechanism for reaching a person who does not yet have an account.
  - **Approved events, when implemented: `ReferralQualified` v1 and `ReferralReversed` v1, and
    nothing else.** **`ReferralAttributed` is NOT defined and NOT emitted.** It has no consumer, so
    it is YAGNI, and defining it would contradict the same no-consumer-no-event rule that keeps the
    wishlist eventless.
  - Both payloads carry **only** required identifiers, closed enums, integer point values, and
    instants. **No referral code, phone, display name, or free prose** in any event payload,
    notification payload, analytics dimension, metric label, or log line. A referral code is a bearer
    credential for attribution and never leaves the authenticated read route.
  - **Referral notifications are in-app only** in the first milestone, under the existing
    **opt-outable** `referral` category — which is deliberately absent from `MANDATORY_CATEGORIES`
    and stays that way. Only the **qualified** and **reversed** lifecycle moments notify.
- **Blocks:** V3.2-C referral contract surface and the referral design synchronisation
- **Consequence of deferral:** engineering would invent a share mechanism, and the cheapest
  invention to reach for is the one that quietly needs a vendor.

### `V32-DEC-034` — The referral code format: alphabet and length

- **Question:** What exactly is a referral code made of — which characters, and how many?
- **Alternatives:** (a) leave it to implementation as an engineering default; (b) ratify an
  unambiguous fixed-length alphabet as a closed decision; (c) require a UUID.
- **Recommendation:** **(b).**
- **Reasoning:** the code is a **bearer credential** that is read aloud, written down, and typed
  from a screenshot, so the alphabet is a product property rather than an implementation detail —
  it decides how often a real customer mistypes their inviter's code. It is also the parameter
  that becomes expensive to change: cheap while no attribution exists, and progressively less so
  once codes have been claimed. (c) is unusable for a code a person types by hand, and shortening
  a UUID reintroduces this decision with worse entropy per character. (a) is what `ADR-035` §3
  explicitly declined to do silently.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-31 — (b), ratified at the values already implemented.**

  **The ratified format.** These are the binding values. They are also exactly what Story #11
  shipped, so ratification changed no constant, no migration, and no already-issued code:

  | Parameter | Ratified value |
  |---|---|
  | `REFERRAL_CODE_ALPHABET` | `123456789ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTVWXYZ` |
  | `REFERRAL_CODE_LENGTH` | `10` |

  **Alphabet — 31 characters, uppercase only.** `0`, `I`, `L`, `O`, and `U` are **excluded**.
  This is Crockford Base32 — which already drops `I`, `L`, `O` (misread) and `U` (accidental
  obscenity) — with `0` additionally removed, because with `O` absent it is the remaining glyph a
  reader most often supplies from memory. `1` is **kept**: with both `I` and `L` gone there is
  nothing left for it to be confused with. Uppercase only, so a code read aloud has one spelling.

  **Length — 10 characters.** 31^10 = **819,628,286,980,801** distinct codes, ≈ **49.5 bits**.

  **Ten, not eight, and the reason is a boundary rather than a preference.** The claim-route
  throttle in `V32-DEC-019` — **10 attempts per authenticated caller per hour** — belongs to
  **Story #27 — Referral attribution claim lifecycle**, which owns `POST /api/v1/me/referral/claim`
  and enforces it **in PostgreSQL**, never the in-memory HTTP throttler whose effective limit
  multiplies by instance count while `THROTTLE-STORE` is unresolved. Story #11 generates codes
  **before any of that exists**, so the format must stand on its own:

  | Guess rate | Exhaustive search | Expected success (midpoint) |
  |---|---|---|
  | **10 per hour** — the Story #27 throttle | ≈ **9.35 billion years** | ≈ **4.68 billion years** |
  | **10,000 per second** — wholly unthrottled | ≈ **2,598 years** | ≈ **1,299 years** |

  Eight characters (≈39.6 bits) would be defensible **only** with the throttle in place and would
  leave no margin if a later decision relaxed it. Ten is the first length that is safe without it.

  **This supersedes no earlier entry and reverses nothing.** It fills a gap `V32-DEC-033` and
  `V32-DEC-019` left open rather than revisiting either. `ADR-035` §3 is the implementation record
  and now cites this entry instead of flagging the parameter as unratified.

  **Changing this is a new owner decision.** Not an implementation choice: a different alphabet or
  length means a new entry here, a constant in `@beauclick/referral-contract`, a `VARCHAR` width in
  one migration, and regenerating every code already issued.
- **Blocks:** nothing further — Story #11 is implemented and this entry ratifies it as built.
- **Consequence of deferral:** the format would have stayed an engineering default inside an ADR,
  which is precisely the shape `ADR-035` §3 refused to let it keep.

### `V32-DEC-035` — The referral cap's calendar: Solar Hijri, not Gregorian

- **Question:** `V32-DEC-019` caps a referrer at **10 qualified referrals per Tehran calendar
  month**. Which calendar's month is that — the **Gregorian** month evaluated in `Asia/Tehran`, or
  the **Solar Hijri (Jalali/Persian)** month Iran actually uses?
- **Alternatives:** (a) Gregorian month, evaluated in `Asia/Tehran`; (b) **Solar Hijri (Jalali)
  month**, beginning at 00:00 `Asia/Tehran` on the first day of the Jalali month; (c) a rolling
  30-day window, abandoning the calendar framing entirely.
- **Recommendation:** **(b).**
- **Reasoning:** the cap is a **promise to a person about their own month**, and the month an
  Iranian customer lives in is Farvardin, Ordibehesht, Khordad — not January, February, March. The
  two windows are **not** a rounding difference: a Jalali month begins around the 21st of a
  Gregorian one, so the two answers to "when does my allowance reset" differ by roughly three
  weeks, every month, forever.

  The platform already takes this position everywhere a date is shown to a customer.
  `packages/persian-utils/src/format.ts` states that the platform "uses the Jalali (Solar Hijri)
  calendar, **never Gregorian**", and `VERSION_2_ARCHITECTURE_PLAN.md` records the audit that made
  it a permanent engineering standard after finding V2 had been rendering "Persian *digit glyphs*
  on a Gregorian calendar". A payout window measured in Gregorian months would have been the same
  defect in a place nobody looks.

  **The `ai` precedent did not settle it, and this is why the question had to be asked.**
  `tehranCalendarDay` buckets the AI quota by Gregorian day in Tehran, which reads like a
  precedent — but for a **day** the two calendars are the *same window* and only the label
  differs. A day boundary is a day boundary in any calendar. The question a **month** asks was
  therefore never exercised, and the implementation inherited an interpretation nobody had chosen.

  (c) was refused because it is not what `V32-DEC-019` says. A rolling window is a defensible
  policy, but it is a *different* policy, and adopting it here would be re-deciding the cap under
  cover of clarifying it.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-09-01 — (b) the Solar Hijri (Jalali) calendar month.**

  **The binding parameters.** These are the whole decision; nothing else about the cap changes:

  | Parameter | Ratified value |
  |---|---|
  | `REFERRAL_CAP_PER_REFERRER` | **10** — unchanged from `V32-DEC-019` |
  | `REFERRAL_CAP_CALENDAR` | **`solar_hijri`** (Jalali / Persian) |
  | `REFERRAL_CAP_TIME_ZONE` | **`Asia/Tehran`** |
  | `PERIOD_KEY` | **Jalali `YYYY-MM`**, ASCII digits, zero-padded month |

  **Each period begins at 00:00 `Asia/Tehran` on the first day of the Jalali month**, and **not**
  at 00:00 UTC. Tehran is UTC+03:30 and Iran abolished DST in 2022, so a period boundary currently
  falls at **20:30 UTC on the preceding day** — an offset that must be *derived from the IANA
  database at the instant in question* rather than hardcoded, because a future reinstatement of DST
  would move it by an hour.

  **A Gregorian month evaluated in Tehran is explicitly NOT the policy.** It was the implemented
  behaviour between Story #12's merge and this entry, and it is now wrong rather than merely
  imprecise.

  **Three separable concerns, and conflating any two of them is how this goes wrong:**

  1. **Instants are stored and compared in UTC.** `qualified_at`, `attributed_at` and `expires_at`
     are `TIMESTAMPTZ` and none of them acquires a calendar.
  2. **The timezone converts an instant to a wall clock.** `Asia/Tehran`, from the IANA database.
  3. **The calendar converts that wall-clock date to a Jalali year and month.** Pure arithmetic on
     a Gregorian y/m/d, with no timezone knowledge of its own.

  The period key is the output of (3) applied to the output of (2) applied to (1). An
  implementation that skipped (2) would bucket by UTC and move every late-evening Tehran
  qualification into the wrong month; one that skipped (3) is the Gregorian behaviour this entry
  replaces.

  **No reward value changes.** Both remain **0** (`V32-DEC-016`), and this entry is deliberately
  closed **before** either becomes non-zero — which is the only moment at which the calendar can be
  changed for free. Once a non-zero reward has been paid under one calendar, changing it re-cuts a
  window people were already paid against.

  **Nothing else about the cap is reopened.** The limit of 10, the per-referrer scope, the absence
  of a lifetime cap, the transactional single-statement enforcement, the independence of the two
  reward sides at the cap, and the refusal of device/IP/fingerprint signals are all `V32-DEC-019`
  and are unchanged.

  **No data migration is required, and that is a fact rather than a convenience.**
  `referral.referrer_counters` stores the period as a `VARCHAR(7)` matching
  `^[0-9]{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])$`, which a Jalali key such as `1405-06` satisfies unchanged. And no
  row can exist under the old interpretation: the table was created by the migration merged as
  `597c88d` on 2026-09-01, **no release tag contains it**, the CD pipeline has never run — its own
  header records that it "cannot deploy anything, because there is nowhere to deploy to" while
  `HOSTING` is unresolved — and the only database that has ever held the table is a local
  development scratch database, verified empty. There is therefore no cutover, no mixed-calendar
  period, and no user data to rewrite.
- **Blocks:** nothing. It corrects the interpretation of `V32-DEC-019` before the parameter it
  governs can have any financial effect.
- **Consequence of deferral:** the cap would have kept a calendar nobody chose, and the cost of
  changing it would have risen from zero to "re-cutting a window customers were paid against" the
  moment a reward value became non-zero.

### `V32-DEC-036` — Does a reversal return the referrer's monthly cap slot?

- **Question:** `V32-DEC-019` caps a referrer at **10 qualified referrals per Solar Hijri month**
  (`V32-DEC-035`). When a referral is later **reversed** because the qualifying booking's order was
  fully refunded (`V32-DEC-017`), does the referrer get that slot back for the remainder of the
  month, or does it stay spent?
- **Alternatives:** (a) the slot **stays spent** — the cap counts *qualifications processed*;
  (b) the slot is **returned** — the cap counts *rewards that stuck*; (c) the slot is returned only
  when the reversal falls in the **same** cap period as the qualification.
- **Recommendation:** none offered. Both (a) and (b) are defensible readings of a cap that was
  written before reversal existed, and choosing between them is a business judgement about how
  tightly payouts should be bounded, not an engineering one. The owner chose **(a)**.
- **Reasoning:** `ADR-037` raised this in its own words — *"a counter it must decrement or not — a
  decision that ADR deliberately leaves open"* — and no closed decision answers it. `V32-DEC-016`,
  `-017`, `-018`, `-019`, `-032` and `-035` are all silent.

  **(a)** bounds payouts more tightly and is the simpler rule to explain: ten attempts a month,
  whatever becomes of them. Its cost is that a referrer whose invitee's order is refunded through no
  fault of their own loses a slot they never earned anything from.

  **(b)** is fairer to the referrer and matches how the cap reads to a customer — *"ten rewards a
  month"*. Its cost is a wider abuse surface: with a returned slot, a referrer colluding with
  invitees who book and refund can cycle the same slot indefinitely, which is close to the loop
  `V32-DEC-017` was written to close.

  **(c)** avoids the cycling by bounding the return to the current period, and pays for it with a
  rule nobody can state in one sentence.

  **This was not blocking and was not urgent.** Both reward values are **0**, so no slot has ever
  been worth anything. The question became live at the same moment `V32-DEC-035` did — before the
  first non-zero reward value — and it is closed here, ahead of that.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-09-01 — (a) the slot STAYS SPENT.**

  **The cap counts qualifications the platform successfully processed, not rewards that survived a
  refund.** The binding parameters:

  | Parameter | Ratified value |
  |---|---|
  | On reversal, `referral.referrer_counters` | **unchanged** |
  | Decrement | **not permitted** |
  | Deletion of the counter row | **not permitted** |
  | Recomputation from `referrals` or `reward_grants` | **not permitted** |
  | Cross-period adjustment | **not permitted** |
  | Reversal in a **later** Jalali month | **same rule** — nothing is written, in either period |

  **The reason is the abuse boundary, and it is the same one `V32-DEC-017` was written to defend.**
  Returning the slot makes qualification/refund **cycling** possible: a referrer colluding with
  invitees who book and refund could reuse one slot indefinitely, and the monthly cap — which
  `V32-DEC-019` calls the *bounded-exposure control* for an abuse gap it accepts knowingly — would
  bound nothing at all. (a) keeps the bound real at the cost of a referrer occasionally losing a
  slot they earned nothing from, and the owner accepts that cost knowingly.

  **The rule does not vary by period, and that clause is load-bearing.** A refund that lands in a
  **later** Jalali month must not decrement the earlier period's counter and must not create or
  adjust a row in the current one. Option (c) — returning the slot only within the same period — is
  refused: it would make a referrer's remaining allowance depend on *when the refund happened*
  rather than on what they did, and no customer could state the rule.

  **The implementation already matched this before the decision was taken**, which is why closing
  it changed no production line: V3.2-C Story #28 shipped without touching
  `referral.referrer_counters` at all, as the **null action** rather than a quiet adoption of (a).
  What this entry adds is the ratification, and a **real-PostgreSQL test that proves the counter is
  untouched** — a byte-identical row including PostgreSQL's own `xmin`, in both the same and a later
  Jalali month — where before there was only a source-text assertion that the reversal service never
  names the table. A structural check proves the code does not mention a table; it cannot prove the
  database row survived.

  `xmin` rather than `updated_at`, and a mutation probe is why: `updated_at` is a TypeORM
  `@UpdateDateColumn` set by application code, and the cap counter is written by raw SQL, so a raw
  `UPDATE` leaves it untouched and an assertion on it would pass while the row was being rewritten.
  `xmin` changes on **any** write.
- **Blocks:** nothing. It is closed **before either referral reward value becomes non-zero**, on the
  same reasoning `V32-DEC-035` was closed before then.
- **Consequence of deferral:** n/a — decided. Had it been deferred past the first non-zero value, an
  unratified reading would have been governing a real payout bound — which is exactly the situation
  `V32-DEC-035` was created to correct.

## D. V3.2-D — CRM and delegation

### `V32-DEC-022` — Who may read and write CRM data: professional versus business

- **Question:** Is CRM data scoped to the party (professional or business) or to the individual
  author?
- **Alternatives:** (a) party-scoped, with the author recorded for display and audit — V2's
  shape; (b) author-scoped; (c) party-scoped with per-data-class rules.
- **Recommendation:** **(c)**, defaulting to (a).
- **Reasoning:** (a) is the right base, and it matches every other ownership boundary in V3.
  (c) is needed because the roadmap already requires financial and AI-conversation data to keep
  their stricter existing boundaries — those must not inherit CRM's broader default.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** OPEN — BLOCKING
- **Blocks:** V3.2-D
- **Consequence of deferral:** the access model is undefined, and a staff fallback would be
  chosen by whichever controller was written first.

### `V32-DEC-023` — Staff role matrix

- **Question:** Which staff roles may see and do what?
- **Alternatives:** (a) owner only; (b) owner and manager; (c) a full owner, manager, staff
  capability matrix.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) for the first release.**
- **Reasoning:** `business.business_staff` exists; a capability matrix over it does not, and
  `V3.2_PLUS_CAPABILITY_CATALOG.md` schedules multi-role staff access at V3.3-C. Building the
  matrix inside V3.2-D would settle a V3.3-C question as a side effect of a CRM phase.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** OPEN — BLOCKING
- **Blocks:** V3.2-D, and shared with V3.3-C
- **Consequence of deferral:** the phase cannot decide who other than the owner may open the
  CRM at all.

### `V32-DEC-024` — Independent-professional delegation

- **Question:** May a solo professional grant another person access to their CRM, bookings, or
  analytics?
- **Alternatives:** (a) no; (b) yes, with a fixed scope and revocation; (c) yes, with a
  configurable scope.
- **Recommendation:** **(a) for the first release.**
- **Reasoning:** delegation is a real product need with no evidence of urgency, and it widens
  every tenant boundary at once. Financial and AI scopes must stay stricter under any answer.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** OPEN
- **Blocks:** V3.2-D delegation scope only
- **Consequence of deferral:** (a) applies; additive later.

### `V32-DEC-025` — CRM note retention, export, erasure, and consent

- **Question:** A CRM note is written *about* a customer *by* a professional. Whose subject data
  is it, what does erasure do, and is the customer told the notes exist?
- **Alternatives:** (a) `retained` on customer erasure, exported to the professional only —
  V2's shape; (b) `subject_data` for the customer, destroyed on their erasure; (c) `retained`
  but with the note text destroyed and the relationship kept.
- **Recommendation:** **(c).**
- **Reasoning:** the note's *existence* is a fact about the professional's business; its *text*
  is free text about an identifiable person, and ADR-027's stated model is "destroy the
  identifying link and the free text; leave rows that are nothing but ids". (a) keeps a named
  customer's described characteristics after they asked to be forgotten. (b) deletes a
  professional's business record wholesale. Consent — whether the customer is told notes exist —
  is a separate half of this entry and has no recommended answer, because it is a disclosure and
  legal question.
- **Owner:** OWNER + LEGAL
- **Status:** OPEN — BLOCKING
- **Blocks:** V3.2-D — ADR-027 requires the disposition before boot
- **Consequence of deferral:** the application cannot start with a CRM table present.

## E. V3.2-E — B2B quotes and campaigns: owner decision cards

Both entries below require an explicit **yes or no** before any engineering begins.
`V3.2_PRODUCT_ROADMAP.md` §7 states this, and the historical existence of a V2 implementation
is **not** evidence of V3 approval.

### `V32-DEC-026` — B2B quotes: build now, or defer?

| Field | Statement |
|---|---|
| **User or business outcome** | A business buyer requests pricing for a bulk or contracted purchase; the platform issues a quote with a validity window; acceptance converts it into a normal order and payment. |
| **Minimum viable scope** | B2B account with an approval status; price tiers; a quote with a status lifecycle and an expiry; acceptance converting to an order through `createOrder`; cancellation. |
| **Pricing and commerce integration** | Tier pricing is a `PricingRule` added to the single existing chain — never a second total. This is the specific defect V2 had: `PRODUCT_GAP_REGISTER.md` records that B2B quote orders were a pricing path no discount could reach, because they fired no hook. |
| **Authorization** | A new B2B capability, plus party-scoped ownership. Quote acceptance is a money-committing action and must be audit-enforced. |
| **Financial and tax implications** | Real. A B2B order is an invoice-bearing transaction in a way a consumer booking may not be. Tax and fiscal invoicing are `PRODUCT + EXTERNAL` at V3.4-B and are **not** implied by approving quotes. |
| **Concurrency requirements** | Tier and quote acceptance must be transactional against the catalogue price at acceptance time, so a price change mid-negotiation cannot be raced. |
| **Reason to build now** | A V2 implementation exists, so the domain is understood; the pricing chain is designed to accept the rule. |
| **Reason to defer** | No V3 evidence that a B2B buyer exists. Every consumer-facing V3.2 capability serves a user the platform already has; this one serves a customer segment the platform has not yet demonstrated. |
| **Recommendation** | **Defer** until an owner states the B2B requirement is real. |
| **Owner** | OWNER |
| **Status** | OPEN — BLOCKING (V3.2-E may not begin) |
| **Consequence of deferral** | None to any other phase. V3.2-E is independent. |

### `V32-DEC-027` — Campaigns and promotions: build now, or defer?

| Field | Statement |
|---|---|
| **User or business outcome** | The platform or a professional runs a time-boxed discount with eligibility rules, a budget, and usage caps. |
| **Minimum viable scope** | Campaign with a window and a status; eligibility; total and per-customer usage caps; a `PricingRule`; usage release on cancellation or refund; audit. |
| **Pricing and commerce integration** | One rule in the existing chain. `apps/api/src/pricing/membership-discount.rule.ts` already anticipates a campaign rule in its ordering comment, and the immutable `subtotalToman` base already guarantees a membership discount and a campaign discount do not compound. |
| **Authorization** | Platform capability to create and edit; audit-enforced. |
| **Financial and tax implications** | A discount changes a real total and therefore a real commission. The refund path already exists and is event-driven; campaign usage release must ride it. |
| **Concurrency requirements** | **The known failure.** V2's `record_usage()` checked the cap well before the insert, so concurrent checkouts overshot both the total and the per-customer cap — recorded in the code as `GAP-04`. The V3 answer must be a database-enforced or transactional decrement, never a read-then-write quota. `waitlist`'s atomic CAS discipline and ADR-024 are the local precedent. |
| **Reason to build now** | The pricing chain is ready for it and the marketing value is immediate and obvious. |
| **Reason to defer** | No campaign has been designed, budgeted, or requested; the eligibility and stacking rules are unwritten; and a discount engine with an unbounded budget is a direct financial exposure. |
| **Recommendation** | **Defer**, but ahead of B2B if either is approved — the integration is smaller and the outcome is nearer. |
| **Owner** | OWNER |
| **Status** | OPEN — BLOCKING (V3.2-E may not begin) |
| **Consequence of deferral** | None to any other phase. |

### `V32-DEC-028` — Payout policy

- **Question:** On what schedule, under what approval threshold, to what beneficiaries, and
  with what failure and reversal handling does money leave the platform?
- **Alternatives:** (a) manual payout with the ledger as the record — today's behaviour;
  (b) operator-approved batch payout; (c) automated payout above a threshold with operator
  approval below or above it.
- **Recommendation:** **(b)** as the first automated step.
- **Reasoning:** the settlement foundation is append-only and idempotent and already records
  batches and items. The gap is not the ledger; it is banking rails, beneficiary and KYC rules,
  and an approval policy — none of which engineering may invent. `V3.2_PRODUCT_ROADMAP.md` §8

- **Status:** OPEN — BLOCKING, and additionally `EXTERNAL`
- **Blocks:** V3.2-F payout
- **Consequence of deferral:** payouts stay manual, which is a correct and honest state.

### `V32-DEC-029` — Calendar sync direction and source of truth

- **Question:** Which providers, one-way or two-way, and which side wins a conflict?

- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** OPEN — BLOCKING, and additionally `EXTERNAL`
- **Blocks:** V3.2-F calendar

### `V32-DEC-032` — Loyalty points clawback on a hidden review

- **Question:** Does hiding an abusive review reverse the points it earned?
- **V3.2 consequence:** it is the same question as referral reversal (`V32-DEC-017`) — an award
  surviving the undoing of the thing that earned it — and the ledger had no reversal path built
  for either. One answer governs both.
- **Owner:** OWNER
- **Status:** **DECIDED 2026-08-30 — yes, with exactly the ledger semantics `V32-DEC-017` sets.**
  A hidden review's points are clawed back by a **new negative row under a distinct reason**, never
  by mutating the original entry, so the ledger stays append-only. The clawback **may drive the
  spendable balance negative** and the balance is **never clamped at zero**. The clawback **does not
  reduce lifetime earned and does not demote a tier**, matching the platform's existing rule that
  redemption never demotes.

  Closed here rather than deferred so that one ledger does not acquire two different answers to one
  question. **This entry carries no Story Points**: the decision effort is already represented in
  GitHub issue #6 and in the V3.2-C decision packet, and its GitHub closure record is a separate
  `type:decision` issue in milestone V3.2-C, created and closed unpointed for that reason.

  **This closure does not schedule the implementation.** The review-clawback path is not V3.2-C
  scope and no story exists for it; what is settled is the semantics any future implementation must
  use.
- **Blocks:** nothing further; it no longer blocks V3.2-C reward reversal design
- **Consequence of deferral:** n/a — decided.
