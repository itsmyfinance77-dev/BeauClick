## Purpose

This packet closes the product values that engineering must not invent. It
applies the owner's three connected directions as one commercial model:

1. customers are not charged a BeauClick booking fee; seller subscriptions are
   the primary recurring revenue;
2. booking collection supports pay at venue, online deposit with venue balance,
   and full online payment;
3. cancellation, late cancellation, no-show, provider fault, reschedule,
   dispute and seller settlement are evaluated from the immutable policy the
   customer accepted.

ADR-039 and the v1 contract already preserve these extension points. This
packet does not authorise a gateway, payout rail, production money movement or
legal claim. It separates decisions so one later change does not require
rewriting Booking, Payment, Commerce and Financial together.

## Current repository facts

- Booking is professional/slot keyed and already emits lifecycle facts.
- The current checkout collects one full order total.
- Payment verifies the exact amount captured by a provider.
- Booking cancellation currently causes the remaining captured amount to be
  refunded; `no_show` has no financial consequence.
- Financial currently applies a 1,500-basis-point commission configuration to
  platform-observed money and has no seller-pending/disputed availability
  schedule.
- No seller subscription, plan entitlement, booking allowance, deposit
  schedule or production payout adapter exists.
- The commercial-policy foundation is intentionally not composed into the API
  and has no production provider.

## Recommendation summary

| Decision | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| revenue | customer booking free; seller subscription primary | predictable B2B revenue without reducing customer conversion |
| packaging | seller chooses an admin-defined plan or buys a custom credit quantity | supports both predictable bundles and pay-for-needed-capacity |
| allowance | plan-included credits and custom purchases share one ledger but retain their source | 200 may be configured or entered, never a system constant |
| overage | warn, then seller explicitly buys more credits; never break existing bookings | supports changing demand without a hidden charge or operational lockout |
| collection | all three v1 modes, selected only within platform-approved bounds | supports cash-heavy and online sellers without lying about collected money |
| deposit | seller chooses fixed or percentage inside platform bounds | commercial flexibility with consistent customer protection |
| customer cancellation | full deposit refund before cutoff; approved retention after cutoff | clear, predictable consequence |
| no-show | retain the approved deposit amount after slot end only | compensates reserved capacity without charging an uncollected balance |
| provider/platform fault | 100% refund of platform-collected service money | the customer did not cause the failure |
| dispute | hold seller availability while the dispute is open | prevents payout/refund races |
| settlement | pending, disputed, available, settled, refunded and reversed are append-only facts | honest accounting and replay safety |
| venue balance | seller-reported only; excluded from platform-collected revenue | BeauClick has no authoritative collection fact |

## Decisions requiring approval

### `V33-DEC-009` — Admin-defined plans and selectable booking credits

**Structure ratified 2026-09-02.** The binding structural wording is in the
decision register. The blanks below remain open under #46.

**Owner clarification, 2026-09-02.** BeauClick has two purchase paths. A seller
may select one of the subscription plans defined by an administrator, or enter
a desired booking-credit quantity and buy that quantity directly. A plan holder
may also buy custom additional credits later. No allowance, including 200, is a
permanent system constant.

**Recommendation.** Use a combined plan-plus-top-up model:

- every seller has a minimal base workspace; paid capability is then acquired
  through a plan, a custom credit purchase, or both;
- an administrator creates an immutable plan version with public name, price,
  billing term, included booking credits, staff seats, locations and feature
  entitlements such as AI, SMS or analytics;
- a seller may select one active plan version; its included credits are granted
  once per subscription term;
- instead of a paid plan, a seller may purchase a positive integer custom
  booking-credit quantity within platform-approved minimum and maximum bounds;
- a plan holder may use the same custom purchase path as a top-up;
- a plan may include 200 credits if the administrator configures that value;
  changing it creates a new plan version and affects only future purchases or
  renewals;
- top-up checkout may offer quick presets such as 50, 100, 200 or 500, plus an
  allowed custom quantity; presets are presentation only;
- a versioned top-up price schedule maps quantity to unit price or volume tier;
- each grant records its source as `plan_included` or `custom_purchase`; each
  purchase snapshots quantity, unit/tier price, total, currency, schedule
  version and term, so later price changes affect only later purchases;
- professional and business subscribers use the same quota mechanism; plan,
  vertical and identity type do not change the meaning of one booking credit.

This keeps pricing flexible without making price a mutable field on a live
subscription or scattering plan names through Booking.

**Values to approve:** minimal base-workspace capabilities `________________`;
initial plan versions and their prices/included credits/
seats/locations/capabilities `________________`; minimum top-up quantity `____`;
maximum self-service quantity `____`; top-up price tiers `________________`;
whether unused included and top-up credits expire, carry forward, or both under
a stated cap `________________`; annual prepaid option and discount `________`.

**Recommendation on billing term.** Use an anniversary-based term measured
from the subscription start instant, not a Gregorian or Solar Hijri calendar
month. This avoids changing allowance merely because a calendar month has a
different length. Display dates may still use the Solar Hijri calendar.

### `V33-DEC-010` — Allowance consumption, return and overage

**Structure ratified 2026-09-02**, with one correction to the recommendation
below: customer-cancellation and no-show treatment is versioned policy rather
than a fixed rule, so no cutoff or retained outcome is hardcoded and the exact
values remain open under #46. The binding structural wording is in the decision
register.

**Recommendation.** Consume one unit atomically when a booking first becomes
`confirmed`, keyed idempotently by booking id. `pending` and `expired` consume
nothing. `completed`, customer-cancelled, late-cancelled and `no_show` keep the
unit consumed because capacity was reserved. Provider/platform cancellation
returns one unit exactly once. Reschedule transfers the same unit and never
consumes a second one.

At 80% and 100%, notify authorised seller roles. Reaching zero credits must not
make existing bookings unreadable or unmanageable. New confirmation after the
limit follows one approved action:

- **Recommended:** seller explicitly buys any permitted additional quantity at
  the then-current snapshotted price; no silent charge;
- alternative: allow a defined grace quantity, then block new confirmation;
- rejected: silently bill an unspecified overage or cancel existing bookings.

**Values to approve:** minimum top-up quantity `____`; optional grace quantity
`____`; subscription payment grace period `____` days.

### `V33-DEC-011` — Allowed collection modes by seller and service

**Recommendation.** Platform policy determines which modes a plan/vertical may
use; an entitled seller chooses a default, and may narrow it per service.
Customers never override the seller's accepted policy.

- `pay_at_venue`: BeauClick collects zero service money;
- `deposit_online_balance_at_venue`: BeauClick collects only the deposit and
  displays the balance payable to the seller;
- `full_payment_online`: BeauClick collects the full service price.

The rollout flag, entitlement, seller policy and operational kill switch must
all pass independently. Disabling online collection falls back only to a mode
the seller explicitly enabled; it never silently changes an accepted booking.

### `V33-DEC-014` — Dispute window and moderation authority

**Recommendation.** A customer may dispute a late-cancel/no-show retention for

platform-held amount from pending to disputed. It cannot change booking history
or charge the uncollected venue balance.

Moderators may uphold customer refund, uphold seller retention, or request
evidence; every read and decision is capability-gated and audited. No moderator
may edit payment, booking or message history.

## Story #58 credit-accounting split — ratified 2026-09-06 (`V33-DEC-025`)

**Structure and contract only. No commercial value was approved by it** — no included
quantity, grace, expiry, overage, cutoff, retention rule, no-show penalty, notification

The readiness audit found that booking-credit enforcement cannot be switched on: the
seeded `D-7` plan version confers zero credits, every seller is placed on it, and no

`#58b` (**3 SP**), the later explicit switch to global fail-closed enforcement, blocked
until #46 ratifies a production-positive grant source and a rollout treatment.

The ratification also corrected three stale claims: **#57 is not a structural
prerequisite**; the entitlement seam must cover **both** confirmation paths rather than
only the zero-collectible one; and the `business` and `administrator` cancellation
returns the issue described are unreachable in the current booking actor vocabulary.
Customer-cancellation return and retention remain open under #46. The full card is

## Story #57 custom-purchase split — ratified 2026-09-06 (`V33-DEC-026`)

**Structure, contract and security only. No commercial value was approved by it** —
no price, tier, minimum or maximum purchasable quantity, UI preset, quote-validity

Story #57 asks for a seller to buy a booking-credit quantity at a price fixed when
they buy. Half of that is buildable today and half is not, and the half that is not

UNIQUE (subscription_id, source, period_index)` would allow a seller **exactly one**

purchase and its price snapshot and **writes no grant of any kind**, and #99 (`#40c-2`)

Refund and clawback of purchased credits, credit expiry, quote validity, tax and
invoice treatment and revenue recognition all remain **open under #46**. Real
collection remains **blocked by #47**. The full card is `V33-DEC-026` in the decision

## Booking-credit purchase-schedule selection — ratified 2026-09-06 (`V33-DEC-027`)

**Structure, contract and security only. No commercial value was approved by it** —
no schedule key, price, tier, quantity bound, preset, allowance or expiry.

A post-ratification code audit found one question `V33-DEC-026` had not answered.
It ratified that a seller submits **only a quantity** and that the server prices it
from the administrator's catalogue — but not **which** catalogue row. Four facts
make that unanswerable as the schema stands: the resolver requires a schedule key,
the seller may not supply one, `commercial.price_schedules` puts no uniqueness on
`purpose` so several `booking_credit` schedules are legal, and the anti-overlap
exclusion constraint is **key-scoped**, so two different keys may each hold a
published version active at the same instant. Nothing on the plan or the
subscription selects one — `plan_versions.price_schedule_version_id` is what the
**plan itself** costs.

Every way of inventing an answer at implementation time would have broken something
already ratified: a client-supplied key is a client-chosen price; first-row or
lexicographic selection makes the price a function of row order or naming; a
hard-coded or environment key is a commercial value in code, which `V33-DEC-009`
forbids; and a rule that only one credit schedule may ever exist would quietly
answer a #46 question about per-plan pricing.

So the **administrator** chooses credit pricing per **immutable plan version**,
through a nullable, stable schedule-key reference whose target purpose the database
enforces. A subscription **snapshots that key at activation**, so later plan edits,
supersession or affiliation changes cannot redirect its pricing source. Each request
resolves the version of that key **active at the request instant**, inside the
caller's transaction, and snapshots that exact version, tier and price on the
purchase. Binding the **key** rather than a version is the load-bearing choice: an
administrator can reprice credits forward without migrating a single subscription,
and every historical purchase keeps the price it was actually offered.

**Nothing existing is backfilled.** `D-7` and every current subscription keep a null
binding, and Story #57 therefore **ships safely unavailable** — a purchase becomes
possible only after an administrator creates a credit schedule, publishes a version
of it, and publishes a plan version carrying its key. That is the same shape `#58a`
shipped in: a complete mechanism, dormant until somebody with the authority
configures it, rather than a mechanism with a default nobody decided.

Story #57 stays one story and is re-estimated **8 → 13 SP** for the binding, the
snapshot propagation, the administrator surface and their database and adversarial
proofs. #99 stays 5 SP and blocked on #47. The full card is `V33-DEC-027` in the
decision register.

The load-bearing rulings, in the shortest honest form:

1. Every commercial parameter is an administrator-managed **immutable version** with
   non-overlapping effective windows, a one-way `draft -> published -> retired`
   lifecycle, privileged publication with live revocation, and mandatory transactional
   audit. **No commercial number may survive as a code constant, fallback, seed or
   environment variable.**
2. That makes `FinancialConfig.DEFAULT_COMMISSION_RATE_BP = 1500` and
   `FINANCIAL_COMMISSION_RATE_BP` a **verified contradiction**. #43 replaces them with
   a versioned commission policy; until one is published, a new commission-bearing
   ledger write **fails closed** rather than defaulting to 15%. Existing ledger rows
   keep their snapshotted rate.
3. Every commitment — subscription, quote, purchase, booking, collection, refund or
   retention decision, ledger allocation, settlement — **snapshots** the effective
   policy version and the exact money or quantity, and nothing downstream re-reads
   live policy.
4. **Ordinary administrator publication is not retroactive.** The activation instant
   is database-authoritative and never earlier than publication. A genuinely
   historical import needs a separate system/migration path and its own audit record.
5. **Collection modes stay exactly three.** Fixed and percentage deposits are
   calculation rules producing `deposit_online_balance_at_venue`, not new modes.
6. **Deposit retention is a cancellation/no-show outcome, not a collection mode.** It
   belongs to #42, can never exceed money actually collected, and cannot activate
   before Legal approval and approved Persian copy.
7. **Missing configuration fails closed** for money movement, penalty, expiry,
   commission, paid activation and global enforcement. Today's safe behaviour — no
   retention, full refund of the remaining collected amount — stands.
8. **#43 owns the pending-funds model, which does not exist yet.** Only invariants are
   ratified: availability follows an explicit policy event, a reserve is never
   negative, a reversal never exceeds the applicable collected or settled fact, and
   settlement plus later refund or clawback stay reconcilable.
9. **#95's premise is corrected** — see the correction below.
10. **Configurability grants no legal or external authority.** Publishing a policy
    changes what the platform records, not what a bank does.

Optional four-eyes approval of a publication is recorded as a **named future
decision**, deliberately not ruled on and not turned into an issue here.

**ADR-048 is required before the first schema or executable implementation**, and was
deliberately not written alongside the decision. The full card is `V33-DEC-028` in the
decision register.

### Correction to the `V33-DEC-025` premise recorded above

The sentence in the `V33-DEC-025` section that says `#58b` is *"blocked until #46
ratifies a production-positive grant source and a rollout treatment"* is stale in one
half, and `V33-DEC-028` Ruling 10 corrects it.

A production-positive grant source is **already structurally reachable**:
`plan_versions.included_booking_credits` is administrator-supplied, `NOT NULL` and has
no default; `ck_seller_subscriptions_zero_price` constrains the plan *price*, not its
entitlement; and the grant service writes the subscription's snapshotted quantity. An
administrator may therefore publish a **zero-price plan version carrying a positive
allowance** today, through the existing admin plane.

What #95 was actually waiting for is the **rollout treatment**, which is now ruled:
no synthetic hard-coded backfill; existing sellers on the seeded `D-7` stay
legacy-exempt and selectively enforced until explicitly transitioned to an
administrator-published positive entitlement; the activation command must fail closed
while any eligible active seller remains unintentionally legacy-exempt, must offer a
non-mutating preview and count, and must retain a persistent audited emergency kill
switch; and only once every eligible seller is explicitly governed may global
enforcement be activated atomically. Engineering must not invent an allowance to
satisfy this.

## Story #83 decomposition and the collection-only contract — ratified 2026-09-06 (`V33-DEC-029`)

**Structure, contract and security only. No commercial value was approved by it, and it is
not Legal approval.** No collection mode was enabled and no deposit percentage, fixed
amount, bound, rounding rule, base choice, cutoff, retention amount, dispute window,
commission rate, settlement delay, tax treatment or word of customer-facing copy was
chosen.

The `V33-DEC-028` readiness audit found that **#83 could not populate its own contract
honestly**, and the finding is a fact about the code rather than a preference:

- `BookingCommercialTermsV1` **requires** six values owned by other issues — cancellation
  cutoff, late-cancellation and no-show retention, reschedule action and dispute window
  (**#42**), and settlement delay (**#43**) — plus a mandatory `customerPolicyCopyVersion`
  for which no approved Persian copy exists.
- `PercentageDepositTerms` carries **no calculation base**; the rate is applied to whatever
  total the caller passes.
- `ck_ops_policy_reference` refuses to record a policy key and version **without** an
  acceptance instant that would be false today.
- One hard-coded writer serves every order, so a fail-closed path on merge would have
  stopped **every booking on the platform**.

So #83 splits **13 → 21 SP**: **`#41d-1`** (#83, 13 SP) publishes versioned collection
policy and changes no order behaviour; **`#41d-2`** (8 SP) adds seller-party assignment and
the immutable order snapshot.

The load-bearing rulings, in short:

1. The foundation introduces a **collection-only** terms and snapshot contract — mode,
   deposit rule, calculation base, contract version, key/version, resolution instant — and
   **no field is filled with a zero, placeholder, default or invented version identifier**.
   #42 and #43 own their own later contracts.
2. **Acceptance is never fabricated.** `policy_accepted_at` keeps meaning actual customer
   acceptance of approved copy, stays nullable, and **neither child populates it**.
   `ck_ops_policy_reference` becomes key/version all-or-none, a positive version when
   present, and independently nullable acceptance. Existing rows stay byte-identical.
3. The **percentage base is required policy data with no default**, over exactly
   `{service_subtotal, service_total}` — both amounts exist authoritatively at order
   creation. BigInt floor division, the min/max clamps and the service-total clamp remain
   binding. **Neither value is chosen.**
4. **Assignment is seller-party-level only**, keyed to the party the order snapshots,
   mutated only by live ownership through an opaque `workspaceRef`. Staff affiliation
   grants nothing. **Per-service override is deferred behind #44**, because a service is
   owned by a professional while the booking is sold by the business — an ownership
   mismatch no precedence rule repairs.
5. An assignment binds a **stable key**; order creation resolves the version active at the
   **database clock instant** inside the same transaction. Later publication or retirement
   never reinterprets an existing order.
6. Ordinary publication is **database-authoritative and non-retroactive**.
7. Rollout is an explicit **dark launch**: an unenrolled legacy party stays on the named
   full-online / null-policy path with a stated exit; an **enrolled** party resolves or
   **fails closed**, with no post-lookup fallback and no seed policy. This clarifies
   `V33-DEC-028` Ruling 8 without weakening it.
8. The database-backed resolver is the **one production source**; the in-memory registry
   survives only for contract and sandbox tests behind a narrow port.
9. `#41d-2` adds one **new non-privileged** capability, `bc_manage_own_collection_policy`,
   rather than widening `bc_manage_own_subscription`. A capability is never ownership.
10. Every failure collapses to **one non-enumerating public refusal**.

**Boundaries unchanged.** #42 keeps cancellation, no-show, dispute, retention and copy with
`gate:legal`; #43 keeps commission, pending funds and settlement with `gate:external`; #47
and #99 stay blocked; #95 is untouched. Neither child populates acceptance, retains a
deposit, calls a provider, settles funds or globally enables booking-credit enforcement.

**ADR-048 must be committed alone, before any schema, contract or executable code** for
either child. The full card is `V33-DEC-029` in the decision register.

## What can proceed before final closure

- contract and deterministic sandbox tests;
- plan/policy version persistence with production activation false;
- authorization, privacy, audit, idempotency and concurrency infrastructure;
- operator readiness reporting that says external/legal gates remain false;
- design exploration explicitly labelled non-production and decision-gated.

The following may not proceed as production-enabled behaviour:

- charging a subscription price;
- collecting or retaining a real deposit;
- marking seller money available or settled;
- applying a commission/acquisition fee;
- displaying unapproved legal copy;
- calling a real gateway, bank or payout provider.
