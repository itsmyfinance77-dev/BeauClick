## `V33-DEC-001` — Revenue model

**Decision.** Customers pay no BeauClick fee to discover, compare, book or
manage appointments. Seller subscriptions are the primary predictable revenue.
Optional acquisition fees, payment processing, premium AI/SMS/analytics,
advertising, retail and B2B revenue are separate policies and entitlements.

**Constraint.** BeauClick may not present commission on venue-collected money as
authoritative without an authoritative collection fact. Full-service commission
is therefore not the default for deposit-online/balance-at-venue.

## `V33-DEC-002` — Actor and workspace model

**Decision.** A person has one identity and may enter multiple workspaces. Roles
such as salon manager, receptionist, practitioner, finance operator, inventory
operator and B2B sales are scoped memberships. Salon, laser/beauty clinic,
maison, retail, wholesale, academy, mobile team and multi-location organisation
are vertical classifications.

## `V33-DEC-003` — Collection modes

**Decision.** The closed v1 modes are `pay_at_venue`,
`deposit_online_balance_at_venue` and `full_payment_online`.

For deposit mode, the receipt always distinguishes:

- full disclosed service price;
- amount collected by BeauClick;
- balance payable directly to the seller.

## `V33-DEC-004` — Immutable policy history

**Decision.** A business edits future policy by creating a new version. Every
booking stores a snapshot of the exact terms and copy version it accepted.
Historical outcomes never read live business configuration.

## `V33-DEC-005` — Domain responsibility

**Decision.** Booking owns lifecycle facts. Commercial Policy evaluates those
facts against a snapshot. Commerce owns price and collection schedule. Payment
executes collection/refund. Financial owns pending/available/settled allocation.
Notification communicates the result. No domain reaches into another's tables.

## `V33-DEC-006` — Fulfilment remains professional-keyed

**Decision.** A business is the seller/workspace, not the booking resource.
Professional and slot remain the booking concurrency keys. Locations, devices,
rooms and aggregate business calendars are additive resource/permission work.

## `V33-DEC-007` — Four independent controls

**Decision.** Rollout flag, plan entitlement, business policy and operational
kill switch are evaluated separately and fail closed. None substitutes for
authorization or audit.

## `V33-DEC-008` — Open production parameters

**Status:** OPEN under #46 and #47.

## `V33-DEC-009` — Plan catalogue and booking-credit pricing

**Structure:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-02 after review of the
Story #40 readiness packet. **Values:** OPEN under #46.

The owner ratified the structure below. It is binding on implementation. It
fixes no price, allowance, bound, term or legal wording, and it activates
nothing in production.

### Plan versions and lifecycle

- Seller plans are immutable, versioned records.
- The version lifecycle is `draft -> published -> retired`.
- A retired version can neither be reactivated nor edited.
- Restoring earlier terms requires a new version, never an edit.
- Activation windows for the same plan key must not overlap.
- Existing subscriptions, purchases, grants and bookings retain their original
  snapshots; retiring a version prevents new selection and purchase but changes
  no existing grant.

### Subscriber party

- The subscriber party is `professional | business`.
- The subscriber party is always resolved server-side.
- A client-supplied professional, business, owner or subscriber id is never
  trusted.

### Base workspace

- The base workspace is `D-7`: an automatically assigned, published, zero-price
  plan version.
- Every seller therefore has one explicit subscription history rather than an
  implicit fallback.

### Administrator capability

Administrators may define plans, billing terms, included booking credits, seats,
locations, capability sets, credit quantities, quantity tiers, price schedules,
activation dates and UI presets.

### Seller purchase paths

- A seller may select an active admin-defined plan, or enter a desired positive
  booking-credit quantity.
- A plan holder may use the same custom purchase path for top-ups.
- Plan-included and custom-purchase grants share one balance but retain their
  source.

### Pricing and snapshots

- Pricing uses immutable versioned tiers; a flat price is a one-tier schedule.
- Every purchase snapshots quantity, resolved tier, integer unit price, integer
  total, currency, schedule version, subscriber party and effective instant.
- Currency is IRT only unless a later owner decision widens it.

### Production safety

- No allowance, including 200, may exist as a code constant, default, fallback
  or seed.
- An unconfigured plan or price schedule refuses safely rather than falling back.
- No production price is active by default.
- No recurring billing and no external provider is part of Story #40.

## `V33-DEC-010` — Booking-credit consumption, return and overage

**Structure:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-02. **Values:** OPEN
under #46.

### Consumption

- Consume exactly one booking credit when a booking first enters `confirmed`.
- Consumption is keyed by booking id.
- Retries, replays and concurrent duplicate confirmations consume nothing extra.
- Pending or expired holds consume nothing.
- Rescheduling does not consume another credit.
- A booking confirmed in term N and rescheduled into term N+1 remains charged to
  term N.

### Return

- A return is a separate immutable and idempotent fact.
- The original consumption is never deleted or rewritten.
- Credits return to the subscriber party snapshotted on the consumption row; the
  seller's current affiliation is never re-resolved during a return.
- Provider, business, administrator or platform cancellation returns the credit.

### Customer cancellation and no-show

Treatment is versioned policy, not a constant:

- an early-cancellation return may be enabled by an approved policy;
- late-cancellation/no-show retention may be enabled by an approved policy;
- there is no hardcoded cutoff and no hardcoded retained outcome;
- exact values remain open under #46.

### Zero balance and overage

- Existing bookings remain readable and manageable at zero balance.
- Only a new qualifying confirmation is refused at zero balance.
- No negative balance, no hidden charge and no automatic overage.
- Grace quantity defaults to zero and is disabled until configured.

### Grant durability

- Already-issued grants are not retroactively changed by upgrade, downgrade or
  cancellation.
- Prepaid/custom credits do not expire by default while Legal has not approved
  expiry. The model may carry a nullable expiry, but production expiry cannot be
  activated without Legal approval.
- Refund of a credit purchase remains a separate open Product/Legal decision.
- Revenue recognition of unused credits remains an Accounting decision.

## `V33-DEC-001` correction — free booking is not a zero service price

**Recorded 2026-09-02** as part of the same owner review, because the Story #40
readiness packet's finding `C1` was interpreted too broadly.

The defect it reports is real. [`checkout.service.ts`](../../../v3/apps/api/src/checkout/checkout.service.ts)
initiates a payment and returns a gateway redirect only when the order total is
positive, and the only production confirmation path is the payment callback. A
genuinely zero-total order therefore receives no redirect and stays `pending`.

But `V33-DEC-001` does **not** mean the service price is zero. It means the
customer pays no separate BeauClick booking fee. Under `pay_at_venue` the full
service price may be non-zero while:

```text
platformCollectibleNow = 0
```

Consequently:

- a temporary architecture predicated only on `totalToman === 0` is **not**
  approved, and no `#40-pre` story is created;
- the proper no-online-collection confirmation path belongs to Story #41, which
  must distinguish the full service price, the amount collectible by BeauClick
  now, and the balance payable at the venue;
- Story #40's final booking integration (#58, `#40d`) depends on that Story #41
  capability;
- the zero-total-order defect remains explicitly visible in Story #41's
  acceptance criteria. No code is changed by this ratification.

## Security, privacy and boundary decisions

**Recorded 2026-09-02** alongside the `V33-DEC-009`/`V33-DEC-010` ratification.

  administrator plan and price-schedule mutations.

  the separate Financial DataSource.
- Seller credit accounting remains an entitlement ledger, not a money ledger.
- No commercial events and no `ServiceName` member are approved here, because no
  consumer has been named.

## `V33-DEC-018` — Seller subscription lifecycle structure

**Structure:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-03 after review of the
Story #56 readiness packet. **Values:** OPEN under #46.

The owner ratified the structure below. It is binding on implementation. It
fixes no price, allowance, seat count, billing term, expiry period or legal
wording, and it activates nothing in production.

### Story split

- Issue #56 becomes **`#56a` — Subscription Foundation**, 8 SP, with **no
  seller-facing route of any kind**.
- Exactly one new issue is created: **#69 (`#56b`) — Seller Subscription
  Surface**, 5 SP. No second replacement for #56 exists.
- Paid activation belongs to neither and has no issue yet.

### Subscriber ownership

- The subscriber party is resolved from **ownership only**, server-side, once,
  at subscription creation.
- `subscriber_party_type` and `subscriber_party_id` are snapshotted onto the
  row. Ownership is never re-resolved from current staff affiliation.
- Affiliated staff may never mutate the employing business's subscription.

This is a deliberate divergence from `ProviderBackedFinancialPartyResolver`,
whose staff-affiliation branch is correct for earnings and wrong for
subscriptions. It restates for subscriptions what `V33-DEC-010` already fixed
for returns: the seller's current affiliation is never re-resolved.

### Affiliation collision

- An independent professional holding an active personal subscription who later
  joins a business **keeps that subscription, which continues independently**.
  It is not superseded, transferred, cancelled or made dormant.
- While that professional acts as staff for a salon booking, #58 consumes from
  the **snapshotted business party**, never from the professional's personal
  subscription.
- If the professional later becomes independent again, their personal
  subscription remains available.
- This ruling holds **while subscriptions are zero-price and non-recurring**.
  Any paid-renewal consequence of holding two subscriptions remains open under
  #46/#47 and must not be invented.

### Base workspace

- Existing sellers receive `D-7` through a **migration backfill**.
- New sellers receive it through **one idempotent lazy-ensure method**.
- One active subscription per subscriber party, enforced by a **partial unique
  index** rather than by application code.
- No event and no scheduler. Neither has a named consumer or an approved
  trigger.

### Paid activation

- **Only zero-price plan versions may activate**, enforced structurally in
  PostgreSQL.
- A non-zero selection is **refused outright**. No pending payment intent, no
  dormant paid subscription, no fake checkout, no sandbox shortcut, no partial
  activation.
- Removing the zero-price restriction requires a later **visible migration**
  after #46 and #47 close.
- Paid activation is part of neither `#56a` nor `#56b`.

### Lifecycle

- `active -> superseded | cancelled`.
- Upgrade and downgrade create a **new immutable snapshot** and supersede the
  prior active row in one transaction.
- Cancellation activates or ensures the base workspace, so every seller always
  holds exactly one active subscription.
- Previously issued grants are immutable.

### Grants

- Issued **exactly once at activation**, from the subscription snapshot.
- The grant is written **even when the quantity is zero**, so the activation
  fact stays auditable rather than being inferred from an absent row.
- Deduplicated structurally by `(subscription_id, source, period_index)`.
- No recurring grant generation while no publishable plan version carries an
  approved billing term.
- Expiry columns may exist, but **no production path may write an expiry**
  until the relevant legal/product decision closes.

### Authorization

- New **non-privileged** capability `bc_manage_own_subscription`, granted to the
  `professional` and `business` roles.
- Reading the caller's own subscription requires authentication and ownership.
- Mutation requires authentication, ownership and the capability.
- It is **not** added to `PRIVILEGED_CAPABILITIES`: that list means authority
  over other people's data, and a self-service capability would dilute a meaning
  that is currently exact.
- No staff-affiliation branch may satisfy ownership.

### Audit

- `admin.admin_audit_log` is reused as the **platform** audit trail. No second
  audit engine is built.
- Recorded: automatic assignment, migration backfill, seller selection and
  cancellation, activation, supersession, and grant issuance.
- Reasons are **closed server-generated values**. DTOs must not accept a
  free-text audit reason — user-controlled prose must not reach a table the
  application cannot UPDATE.
- Automatic and migration actors use `actor_label`, never a fabricated human.
- This widening of the audit-log charter is deliberate and must be documented in
  the Story #56 ADR.

### Privacy

- The subscription and grant tables are ADR-027 **`retained`** with explicit
  structural reasons.
- Neither may be claimed `no_subject_data`.
- No new erasure behaviour outside the approved retained disposition.

### Consequence recorded at ratification

ADR-041 §6 stated that `#40b` "must not ship before #46 closes the base-workspace
definition". This decision **supersedes that constraint** for #56 (`#56a`) and #69 (`#56b`).
The reasoning it guarded remains sound and is narrowed rather than dismissed:
nothing consumes booking credits until #58, so a base workspace conferring zero
entitlements changes no seller's capabilities today, and the zero-quantity grant
above makes that an explicit auditable fact instead of an accident.

One consequence survives and is recorded here rather than discovered later:
**sellers backfilled onto the seeded `D-7` version stay on it when #46 publishes
a new `D-7` version.** Assignment happens once per party, and neither `#56a` nor
`#56b` contains a mechanism to move an existing subscription to a newer base
version. Doing so will require its own decision and its own visible migration,
because it changes what a seller already holds.

## `V33-DEC-019` — Seller subscription surface contract

**Contract:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-03 after review of the
Story #69 readiness packet. **Values:** OPEN under #46.

The owner ratified the contract below. It is binding on implementation. It fixes
no price, allowance, seat count, billing term or legal wording, and it activates
nothing in production.

### Why the singular contract was rejected

One user may own both a professional and a business workspace:
`provider.professionals.owner_id` and `business.businesses.owner_id` are
independent unique indexes. A singular `GET /me/subscription` therefore has no
implementable answer for such a caller, and every way of producing one is
forbidden.

The API must **never** select a party by first-in-array, business-first,
professional-first, staff affiliation, array order or plan type. The failure is
silent in both directions: the caller is shown one workspace without being told
another exists, and a mutation changes a workspace the caller did not name.

### Approved route family

```text
POST /api/v1/me/subscriptions/initialization
GET  /api/v1/me/subscriptions
GET  /api/v1/me/subscriptions/:workspaceRef/history
POST /api/v1/me/subscriptions/:workspaceRef/selection
POST /api/v1/me/subscriptions/:workspaceRef/cancellation
GET  /api/v1/me/commercial-plans
```

**Namespace verified, no correction required.** The existing `v1/me/*`
namespaces are `ai`, `analytics`, `availability`, `finance`, `journey`,
`loyalty`, `notifications`, `referral` and `wishlist`; the three controllers on
bare `v1/me` declare only literal sub-paths (`provider`, `reviews`, and
identity's root `@Get()`). No `v1/me/:param` wildcard exists that could capture
these paths — the `:id` routes in `provider.controller.ts` belong to
`v1/providers`. `subscriptions` and `commercial-plans` are both free.

### Initialization is an explicit command, not a hidden mutation

- GET routes are **read-only**. They create no subscription, grant or audit row.
- Initialization is an authenticated `POST`, accepts **no request body**, and
  rejects unknown query or body fields.
- It idempotently ensures the base workspace for **every** seller workspace the
  caller owns, and returns the resulting collection.
- Replay writes nothing and audits nothing.
- Concurrent initialization stays single by database invariant
  (`uq_seller_subscriptions_one_active_per_party`), not by application check.

**This amends `V33-DEC-018`'s "idempotent lazy ensure on first commercial
access".** The mechanism is unchanged — the same idempotent ensure, the same
index — but its trigger moves out of a read and into a named command. A `GET`
that writes makes a retried or prefetched read create audit rows, and no `GET`
route in this repository has ever written.

### `workspaceRef`

- An **opaque routing reference issued only by the server**. It is not a
  `professionalId`, `businessId`, `partyId`, `subscriptionId`, `ownerId` or
  `userId`, and must not expose or encode a raw database identity.
- Clients treat it as an uninterpreted string and must never construct one.

Binding implementation constraints for the execution turn:

- derive it with a **domain-separated HMAC** under a dedicated
  `WORKSPACE_REFERENCE_HMAC_SECRET`;
- the authenticated input includes the owner and the owned party identity;
- return a **fixed-length base64url** value under a closed length and character
  contract;
- on every request, enumerate the caller's **currently owned** workspaces,
  recompute their references, and match with **constant-time comparison**;
- never query a party directly from a caller-supplied reference;
- `workspaceRef` is **not authorization** — live ownership is reverified on
  every request;
- a reference belonging to another caller, a malformed reference and a
  nonexistent reference produce **byte-identical** refusals;
- rotation invalidates outstanding references; clients recover by repeating
  initialization or the collection read, and this must be documented.

**Single-key only.** The repository has no established current/previous-key
convention — `OTP_HMAC_SECRET` is the naming precedent and it is single-key — so
#69 implements one key and documents recovery by re-listing. No key-management
subsystem is invented here.

### Ownership and capability

- Reading requires authentication and **live** ownership.
- Mutation requires authentication, live ownership and
  `bc_manage_own_subscription`.
- Staff affiliation never establishes ownership.
- A user owning both workspaces sees **two entries**, each with its own
  `workspaceRef`. Mutating one leaves the other **byte-identical**.
- Story #44 retains ownership of the future active-session workspace model.

`bc_manage_own_subscription` remains **non-privileged** and is not added to
`PRIVILEGED_CAPABILITIES`.

**The "live capability revocation" acceptance criterion is removed as false.**
`CapabilityGuard` returns early for any capability outside
`PRIVILEGED_CAPABILITIES`, so this one is read from the access token and never
re-checked; a test asserting database-backed live revocation would be asserting
something the guard does not do. What **is** live is ownership, revalidated
inside every request. Capability changes take effect on the existing
access-token lifecycle. Immediate operational suspension, if ever required,
needs its own explicit design and is not invented here.

### Plans

- Authenticated and seller-facing.
- Returns only **published and currently active/selectable** versions. No
  drafts, no retired versions, no internal audit fields, no unpublished
  schedules.
- Price and entitlement values are read from the immutable published version.
- A non-zero plan may be **visible** where the ratified catalogue contract
  permits, but **selection still refuses** it with `paid_activation_unavailable`.
  No payment intent and no dormant subscription is created.

### Idempotency

- Selecting the currently active plan version returns
  `selection_already_applied` and writes no subscription, grant or audit row.
- **Cancelling while the base workspace is already active is an idempotent
  no-op.** It must not cancel the base workspace and recreate another one.
- Replay and concurrency must not create duplicate successor subscriptions.

### The `#56a` concurrency defect, recorded

`SellerSubscriptionService.selectPlanVersion` skips the compare-and-swap when no
active subscription exists: `if (current)` guards the supersede, so two
concurrent first selections both reach a bare insert and
`uq_seller_subscriptions_one_active_per_party` refuses one as an **untranslated
`QueryFailedError`** — an HTTP 500 rather than
`subscription_changed_concurrently`.

#69 repairs it by ensuring the base subscription **transactionally before**
selection, so a row always exists to contend over and the compare-and-swap
always arbitrates. **No schema change is authorized**; if implementation proves
the existing constraints insufficient it stops for review rather than adding
one.

### Refusal vocabulary

The closed vocabulary is preserved. Only states safe for the **authenticated
owner** to know may be distinguished.

Cross-owner, malformed, missing and ineligible workspace references collapse to
**one byte-identical refusal**. `seller_not_eligible` must not reveal whether
another workspace exists.

These may remain distinct, because each describes an outcome the caller can act
on rather than the existence of anything they do not own:

- `plan_version_not_selectable`
- `paid_activation_unavailable`
- `subscription_changed_concurrently`
- `commercial_not_configured`
- `selection_already_applied`

### Audit

- Fixed server-generated reasons only; **no free-text reason** is accepted by
  any DTO or internal API.
- **One** audit record per successful mutation.
- **No** audit row for a refusal, a validation failure, an idempotent replay or
  a no-op cancellation.
- Subscription, grant and audit writes share the **same transaction manager**.

### Privacy

- No response exposes a raw party, owner or audit identity.
- No cross-workspace history: history is scoped by authenticated ownership plus
  one `workspaceRef`.
- No aggregate that leaks another party.
- Forged references remain non-enumerating.

finished, and CI's real-PostgreSQL job then found three exact-set failures in

correct, but CI acted as the first full-suite run.

**CI must not be the first full-suite execution.**

`/api/v1/me/finance` resolves a single party business-first, so a dual owner

## `V33-DEC-020` — Finance read authorization and the dual-owner surface

**Security and contract:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-04 after
review of the Dual-Owner Finance Audit. Binding on the implementation of #72.
It moves no money, changes no ledger row and settles no commercial value.

### The mistake this decision names

`/api/v1/me/finance` decides *who may read* a financial record by asking *whose
money it is*. Those are different questions, and `ProviderBackedFinancialPartyResolver`
answers both with one lookup.

Beneficiary resolution follows an active `business_staff` affiliation, which is
correct for attribution — ADR-023 §3, and a professional joining a salon
genuinely redirects their earnings. Applied to a read, the same answer hands an
employee the employer's books.

Two defects follow from the single boundary, and they are one bug:

1. **A dual owner is resolved business-first.** `provider.professionals.owner_id`
   and `business.businesses.owner_id` are independent unique indexes, so one
   user may own both. The professional workspace's receivables, settlements and
   ledger are then reachable through no route, and nothing in the response says
   another owned workspace exists.
2. **An affiliated staff professional reads the employing business's financial
   position** — receivable, settlement, outstanding, ledger and every aggregate
   derived from them, including colleagues' and the owner's earnings.

The second is the more serious and was not in the filed issue. Both are read-surface
defects: every row in `financial.*` is correct, correctly attributed and intact.
Nothing was lost and nothing was written wrongly.

### Ruling 1 — staff affiliation is not financial ownership

An affiliated professional must never gain access to the employing business's
receivable, settlement, outstanding balance, ledger, finance summary, or any
aggregate derived from them.

`business-authorization.pg-spec.ts` currently asserts that an affiliated
professional's `mySummary` returns the employer's party. **That case asserts a
security defect and must be corrected during implementation**, not preserved as
a regression guard.

### Ruling 2 — the product consequence is accepted

After correction, an affiliated professional's finance screen shows only their
own professional party's records. That result may legitimately be empty or zero,
because their earnings genuinely belong to the business.

**The server must not fall back to the employer's business finance to avoid an
empty screen.** An empty screen that is true is not a defect.

Manager or staff access to business finance, if it is ever wanted, requires an
explicit scoped role/capability design — likely coordinated with Story #44. It
must never be inferred from a `business_staff` row.

### Ruling 3 — one bug, both defects

Issue #72 covers both. They share one incorrect party-selection boundary and a
fix for either alone would leave that boundary in place. **The issue must not be
split.**

### Ruling 4 — story points

#72 moves from **5 to 8**. The label predated the second defect and the
module-boundary constraint on reusing `workspaceRef`. It stays `status:proposed`.

### Ruling 5 — three concepts, separated structurally

| Concept | Question | Follows affiliation |
|---|---|---|
| Financial beneficiary / seller party | whose money is this? | **yes** — unchanged, ADR-023 §3 |
| Workspace ownership | which workspaces does this user own? | **no** |
| Permission to read finance | who may read these records? | **no** — must use ownership |

Finance reads use **live ownership only**. The rules:

- a business owner may read the owned business's finance;
- a professional owner may read the owned professional's finance;
- a dual owner may read each **separately**;
- staff affiliation grants no finance read access;
- changing affiliation never rewrites historical financial ownership — the
  party is written onto the ledger row and the ledger is append-only;
- **no route accepts a raw owner, user, professional, business or party id.**

### Ruling 6 — approved additive route family

```text
GET /api/v1/me/finance/workspaces
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/summary
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/outstanding-orders
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/settlements
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/orders/:orderId/ledger
```

Requirements:

- `/workspaces` lists every finance workspace the caller **currently owns**, and
  an empty array — never a `404` — when they own none;
- a `workspaceRef` selects exactly one workspace;
- each workspace's financial data is returned **separately**;
- professional and business totals are **never merged** into one figure;
- every SQL query stays party-scoped at the predicate, never a filter applied to
  already-loaded mixed rows;
- **no direct lookup from a caller-supplied reference** — references are matched
  against recomputed candidates for live-owned parties, never used in a `WHERE`.

### Ruling 7 — the four singular routes stay, corrected

```text
GET /api/v1/me/finance/summary
GET /api/v1/me/finance/outstanding-orders
GET /api/v1/me/finance/settlements
GET /api/v1/me/finance/orders/:orderId/ledger
```

Their party resolution changes from beneficiary to ownership. Behaviour by
caller:

| Owned finance workspaces | Response |
|---|---|
| exactly one | the existing successful shape, byte-for-byte where feasible |
| zero | the existing non-enumerating not-found/not-yours refusal |
| more than one | `409` with the new closed code `finance_workspace_selection_required` |

**The server must never silently select** the first, business-first,
professional-first or affiliation-derived party. That is the defect, and a
compatibility layer that preserved it would preserve the bug.

**Compatibility and deprecation boundary.** The singular routes are retained by
this decision and **are not removed in #72**. The in-repository web client
migrates to the workspace-aware routes in the same pull request, so no user
meets the `409`; it exists for clients this repository cannot see. Retiring the
singular routes is a separate decision, not taken here, and requires evidence
that no client depends on them.

### Ruling 8 — `workspaceRef` is reused exactly, and extracted

#72 reuses Story #69's reference with **no observable change**: the same
`WORKSPACE_REFERENCE_HMAC_SECRET`, the same domain prefix, the same
43-character base64url output, the same length-prefixed HMAC construction, the
same owner/party inputs, the same constant-time ownership resolution. **No new
secret, no changed output, no rotation.** Existing #69 references must remain
byte-identical after the extraction, and a test must prove it.

The primitive moves into a `scope:shared` library or equivalent
domain-neutral boundary, because `@nx/enforce-module-boundaries` restricts
`scope:financial` to `scope:shared` alone — importing `commercial-policy` from
`financial` is a lint error, and duplicating the construction is forbidden.

The shared primitive:

- queries **no** database;
- imports **neither** commercial-policy **nor** financial;
- accepts **already-resolved** owned parties as an argument;
- **never performs authorization itself.**

`commercial-policy` and `financial` each retain responsibility for enumerating
their caller's live-owned parties. The reference is a routing handle that is
matched, never a credential that is believed.

### Ruling 9 — `bc_view_own_finance` is not enforced in #72

The audit established that the capability exists, is granted to the
`professional` and `business` roles in the database, and is enforced on no
route — while `AccountResolverService` normally assigns only the default
`customer` role, and no complete production role-grant lifecycle makes the
capability reliably present in a seller's access token.

**Enforcing it now would lock legitimate sellers out of their own finance.**

For #72 the security boundary is exactly three things:

1. an authenticated caller;
2. live ownership;
3. workspace-scoped SQL predicates.

No documentation, comment, test or response may claim `bc_view_own_finance`
enforcement or live revocation for this surface.

### Ruling 10 — the role lifecycle gap is a separate bug

Recorded as **#75**, in V3.3. It is not implemented as part of #72.

The gap is wider than finance: the seller capabilities exist and are granted to
the `professional` and `business` roles, but account resolution assigns only the
default `customer` role and no self-service path grants either seller role. Any
future story that enforces an existing seller capability would deny legitimate
sellers on the day it ships. A fix must tie role assignment to authoritative
ownership or verification, define revocation and token-invalidation semantics
explicitly, and grant no employer authority from a `business_staff` row.

### Ruling 11 — no migration

#72 requires no schema migration. It must not alter financial ledger tables,
rewrite historical financial parties, create workspace persistence, change
subscriptions or grants, create a new capability, or move or recalculate money.

### Ruling 12 — one refusal

For the workspace-aware routes these are **byte-identical**: malformed, random,
foreign, stale, no-longer-owned and nonexistent `workspaceRef`.

No response may reveal whether the HMAC was valid, whether the party exists,
whether another user owns it, or another workspace's balance, row count or
history.

### Ruling 13 — financial correctness

- balances remain calculated from the existing financial ledger;
- ledger data is **not** copied or rewritten;
- professional and business totals never merge;
- pagination cursors are scoped to the `workspaceRef` and its party, so a cursor
  stolen from one workspace is invalid for another;
- currency and settlement states keep their existing semantics;
- all filtering happens **in SQL**, never after loading mixed rows into memory.

### Ruling 14 — no write of any kind

#72 contains read routes only. It writes no admin audit row, financial ledger
row, settlement row, subscription or grant row, event or notification.

### Ruling 15 — the #69 execution gate carries forward

1. Targeted fast tests.
2. Targeted real-PostgreSQL tests.
3. **Complete** fast suite.
4. **Complete** real-PostgreSQL suite with zero skipped.
5. Typecheck, lint, build.
6. Clean isolated migration and idempotency.
7. Role verification.
8. Restore rehearsal.
9. Production image and container.

## `V33-DEC-021` — Seller role and capability lifecycle

**Security and contract:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-04 after review
of the Seller Role and Capability Lifecycle readiness audit. Binding on the
implementation of #75. It moves no money, changes no ledger row, grants no
administrative authority and activates nothing in production.

### Ruling 1 — #75 is an active defect, not a latent one

Story #69 enforces `bc_manage_own_subscription` on three mutations —
initialization, plan selection and cancellation — while production account
creation grants only the default `customer` role and no self-service path grants
`professional` or `business`.

A genuine seller who owns a professional profile or a business is therefore
refused `403` on all three, permanently, until an administrator manually grants
them a role. Because the capability is deliberately non-privileged there is no
live database re-check to consult, and because no production path writes the
role there would be nothing for one to find. The seller also never receives the
base `D-7` workspace, since initialization is its only trigger.

**The absence of a subscription screen in the in-repository web client does not
make this latent.** The routes are mounted unconditionally, behind no rollout
flag, entitlement gate or kill switch. The defect is on the deployed API and is
reachable by any client.

#75's "Impact today — No user-visible breakage" is superseded by this ruling.

### Ruling 2 — the professional trigger is ownership, not verification

The `professional` role is granted **atomically when an authenticated user
successfully creates and owns a professional profile.**

The role represents ownership of a professional workspace. Onboarding and
profile management may legitimately be required *before* verification, so gating
the role on verification would require the role to earn the role.

Verification keeps every job it already has: it governs public visibility and
every verification-dependent operation, and those predicates are unchanged. The
role alone never bypasses a route-level ownership or state check.

The role is **not** granted by:

- submitting verification evidence;
- searching or viewing a professional;
- joining a business as staff or manager;
- any caller-supplied role or owner field.

### Ruling 3 — the business trigger is ownership

The `business` role is granted **atomically when an authenticated user
successfully creates and owns a business.**

It is never derived from `business_staff`, from a manager or staff affiliation,
from a professional affiliation, from a client-supplied role, or from a
client-supplied business or owner id.

### Ruling 4 — roles are additive and `customer` is never removed

Valid combinations are `customer`; `customer` + `professional`; `customer` +
`business`; and `customer` + `professional` + `business`.
`provider.professionals.owner_id` and `business.businesses.owner_id` are
independent unique indexes, so the dual owner is real and both roles are
granted.

**No automatic path may remove the default `customer` role.**
`bc_book_service`, `bc_use_ai_assistant` and `bc_view_own_orders` belong to
`customer` alone, so a grant that replaced roles rather than adding them would
strip every seller of booking, the assistant and their own order history.

### Ruling 5 — a role is never sufficient authorization

Every seller operation retains all of:

- authentication;
- the required capability where the route declares one;
- **live ownership** of the target professional or business;
- any relevant verification, publication or lifecycle predicate.

A role granted without ownership confers no self-scoped authority: the ownership
resolver enumerates no party and the operation finds nothing to act on.

**Ownership checks must not be weakened to make a role test pass.** A test that
requires a loosened predicate is testing the wrong thing.

### Ruling 6 — staff affiliation grants no global role

A `business_staff` row grants no global `business` role. This holds for
`manager` exactly as it holds for `staff` — the same boundary `V33-DEC-020`
Ruling 1 drew for finance, and it must not be reintroduced here by a role grant.

A staff professional keeps their independently owned `professional` role.
Joining or leaving a business grants and revokes nothing.

**Story #44 retains ownership of business-scoped staff roles and permissions.**
#75 introduces no business-scoped capability, no staff-derived role and no new
`business_staff.role` value.

### Ruling 7 — backfill is idempotent and reads ownership only

Existing eligible owners are backfilled. Eligibility comes from authoritative
ownership and nothing else:

| Fact | Grant |
|---|---|
| non-deleted professional row owned by the user | `professional` |
| non-deleted business row owned by the user | `business` |
| ownership of both | **both** |

The backfill must preserve `customer`; must never read `business_staff`; must
never grant `business` to a staff-only user; must not duplicate `user_roles`;
must remain safe under concurrent application activity; must rely on the
existing unique constraints rather than an application-level check; and must
record audit evidence through the established migration/system mechanism —
without inventing a second audit engine.

The exact deleted, suspended and verification predicates are **verified against
the live schema before implementation**. No suspension state is invented: the
audit found `suspended` and `revoked` present in the verification state machine
with no caller, and an unreachable state must not acquire speculative behaviour
here.

### Ruling 8 — ownership and role commit together

New ownership and its matching role assignment commit in **one transaction**,
through a narrow composition-root port that accepts and uses the caller's
transaction manager.

Forbidden: opening another pool connection; emitting an event to grant the role
later; a committed ownership row without its required role; a role row surviving
a failed ownership transaction.

There is no eventual window in which ownership exists and the role does not. If
atomicity proves impossible it is **reported as a finding**, never hidden behind
an event.

### Ruling 9 — grant timing follows the existing token lifecycle

The role row persists immediately. An already-issued access token is not
rewritten. The new role and its capabilities become effective at the caller's
**next successful access-token issuance**, through refresh or login.

Forbidden: claiming immediate capability activation in an existing JWT; adding
database-live checks for every non-privileged capability; invalidating all
sessions; minting tokens from a professional or business domain service.

Clients use the ordinary auth refresh lifecycle before invoking newly
capability-gated seller operations. If a narrow frontend change is required to
trigger that existing refresh flow, it is identified during implementation and
kept minimal.

### Ruling 10 — no new revocation machinery

#75 creates none. The security that already exists is stated rather than
extended:

- **loss or deletion of ownership blocks self-scoped access immediately**,
  through the live ownership predicate, even while a stale token still carries
  the role;
- manual removal of a non-privileged role becomes effective in newly issued
  access tokens, per the existing lifecycle;
- privileged capabilities keep their existing live re-check;
- verification status continues to be checked by the operations that require it.

**No document, comment, test or response may claim live revocation for a
non-privileged capability.** Lifecycle cases that cannot occur in current
production code — professional or business deletion, ownership transfer,
suspension, verification revocation — remain explicit non-goals rather than
speculative implementations.

### Ruling 11 — no role provenance in #75

No provenance column and no assignment-source table.

Live ownership is the real authority, so a manually granted seller role without
ownership grants no access. The defect needs deterministic owner-role creation
and a backfill, not a multi-source authorization model, and #44 will define
future scoped staff authority. Provenance becomes necessary only if multiple
independent grant sources must coexist or carry different revocation semantics.

Recorded as a standing constraint:

- **no code may begin treating the global seller role alone as ownership;**
- any future non-ownership-scoped use of these roles requires a new decision;
- a future provenance model must be additive and migration-backed.

### Ruling 12 — the administrator seed anomaly is recorded, not changed

`administrator` carries `bc_manage_own_profile`, seeded by
`20260824100001_create_roles_and_capabilities.sql` and mirrored in
`CAPABILITIES_BY_ROLE`. It is harmless **only** because ownership remains
mandatory on every route that could use it.

#75 neither expands nor removes administrator capabilities unless the audit
proves an immediate violation. It adds one regression test: an administrator
holding no professional ownership cannot use that capability to read or mutate
another professional's profile.

### Ruling 13 — ADR-023 is amended, not reversed

ADR-023's "`owner` is not a `business_staff` role" section states that V3 never
grants these identity roles dynamically. That statement is superseded to exactly
this extent, using the repository's amendment convention and preserving the
original text:

- beneficiary resolution **may** follow affiliation (ADR-023 §3, unchanged);
- seller workspace authorization follows **ownership**;
- owner role assignment follows **ownership creation**;
- `business_staff` never grants a `business` role.

Nothing else in ADR-023 changes. Its financial-party model, its consent
structure and its one-active-affiliation invariant all stand.

### Ruling 14 — story boundary

**#75 includes:** the professional owner role grant; the business owner role
grant; the existing-owner backfill; transaction-safe composition ports; accurate
token-timing documentation; adversarial role and ownership tests; and any
minimal existing-client refresh adaptation required for reachability.

## `V33-DEC-023` — Zero-collectible order lifecycle and confirmation contract

**Contract and security:** RATIFIED by the product owner on 2026-09-05 after
review of the Story #81 readiness packet. Binding on Story #81 and constraining
#58, #82 and #83.

**Traces to:** Story #81 (`#41b`). **Constrains:** #58 (`#40d`), #82 (`#41c`),
#83 (`#41d`).

**It closes the public vocabulary `V33-DEC-022` Ruling 6 left open, and nothing
else.** It fixes no price, deposit, percentage, bound, rounding value, cutoff,
retention rule, term or legal wording; it enables no collection mode; it
approves no gateway, provider, settlement or payout; and it claims no Legal,
Accounting or external approval. Every value named in `V33-DEC-011`–
`V33-DEC-017` stays open under #46, and real collection stays blocked by #47.

**The open ruling was a false binary, and this card records that.**
`V33-DEC-022` Ruling 6 framed the choice as *a new `OrderStatus` value **with** a
new named-consumer event* versus *staying `pending`*. Those are two independent
questions. The readiness audit confirmed against the tree that `BookingConfirmed`
already has named consumers and a sufficient payload, so a new status does **not**
require a new event. The ratified answer takes the status and refuses the event.

### Ruling 1 — the status exists, and it is named for what it means

Add the explicit `OrderStatus` value **`online_collection_not_required`** for an
order whose authoritative schedule has `platformCollectibleNowToman === 0`.

It means exactly one thing: **BeauClick is not collecting money online now.** It
does **not** mean paid, free service, settled, completed, waived, forgiven or
written off. A venue balance may still be owed to the seller in full, and that
balance is not BeauClick's money and not BeauClick's fact.

The trigger is the schedule's collectible-now amount, never `totalToman`. The
2026-09-02 `V33-DEC-001` correction already ruled that a zero-collectible order
may carry a non-zero service price, and `CheckoutService` currently branches on
`order.totalToman > 0` — which is the defect, not the contract.

### Ruling 2 — the legal lifecycle, and what stays unreachable

    pending --> online_collection_not_required --> cancelled

- `paid` remains reachable **only** through verified payment. It is not reachable
  from `online_collection_not_required` and must never be reused to mean "no
  collection required": `commerce.orders`' own contract states that `paid` means
  a gateway confirmed money actually moved, and the `OrderPaid` event contract
  depends on that meaning.
- **No transition returns to `pending`.** A confirmed zero-collectible order does
  not become payable again.
- Keeping the order `pending` after its booking is confirmed is rejected
  precisely because `pending` is live: `CheckoutService.retryPayment()` treats
  `pending` as "still owes money", and payment retry and reconciliation would
  stay armed against an order nobody will ever pay online.

### Ruling 3 — the transaction, and its order (H-a)

In the confirmation transaction, in this order:

1. transition the order `pending -> online_collection_not_required` by
   compare-and-swap;
2. run the **mandatory** entitlement hook;
3. confirm the booking.

All three commit together or none does; any failure rolls back the whole
transaction.

**This is recommendation H-a, and the reason is lock order, not aesthetics.** The
gateway callback path mutates in the relative order *payment facts -> order ->
booking* (`applyVerification`, then `markPaid`, then `bookings.confirm`). The
zero-collectible path has **no payment facts** — creating any would violate
Ruling 5 — so H-a means its remaining two mutations keep that same relative
order: **order, then booking.** Two paths that touch the same two aggregates in
opposite orders are a lock-order inversion available to any future concurrent
workload; matching the established order removes it before it can be written.

Nothing here pretends payment rows exist on this path. Step 1 of the callback's
sequence is absent, not emulated.

### Ruling 4 — idempotency and concurrency

A retry is idempotent and returns the authoritative existing result rather than
erroring.

Concurrent attempts converge through **database compare-and-swap guarantees**,
not an application-level check-then-act. Exactly one of each may commit:

- one order transition;
- one booking confirmation and one booking history fact;
- one entitlement-hook effect;
- one `BookingConfirmed` outbox event.

### Ruling 5 — no fabricated money facts

When collectible-now is zero, the platform creates **no** payment intent, gateway
attempt, `PaymentSucceeded`, `OrderPaid`, settlement reference, receivable,
refund, or any other money-moved fact.

`CheckoutService.checkout()` today creates an intent for **every** order and only
withholds `initiate()`. Intent creation must move **inside** the
positive-collectible branch during implementation. An intent for an amount
BeauClick will never ask for is a payment-domain row asserting something untrue.

### Ruling 6 — no new event, and no new `ServiceName`

**`BookingConfirmed` remains the single domain fact** for "this slot is now
booked", and `booking.confirmed_at` is the authoritative timestamp. Add no
duplicate order-side timestamp column for the same moment.

Emit no new commerce event and add no `ServiceName` member. The audit verified
against the tree that `BookingConfirmed` already carries `bookingId`,
`professionalId`, `customerId` and `confirmedAt`, and already has named,
registered consumers. A new status is a **state** the platform can be in; an
event is a **fact** something happened. The fact has not changed and already has
a contract, so adding an event would create a second name for one occurrence and
oblige every consumer to learn both.

### Ruling 7 — the checkout response keeps its shape

The checkout HTTP response preserves its established shape and returns
`payment: { intentId: null, redirectUrl: null }` alongside the confirmed booking
and the order in `online_collection_not_required`.

This requires making the corresponding TypeScript and browser-contract field
**nullable**. It must not be done by omitting the key and it must not be done by
inventing a sentinel intent id: a fake identifier is indistinguishable from a
real one at every call site that receives it, and an omitted key silently
becomes `undefined` in a client that was compiled against a required field.

### Ruling 8 — the entitlement hook is mandatory by construction

The composition-root entitlement hook is **mandatory and non-optional**. It:

- accepts the caller's `EntityManager` and commits atomically with the order
  transition and booking confirmation;
- is keyed by **booking id only**;
- accepts **no** client-supplied owner, party, subject or quantity.

An `@Optional()` dependency could silently omit a money or entitlement effect,
which is the whole reason this seam is being built now rather than with #58.
Story #81 ships an explicit **no-op binding**; **#58 later replaces that binding**
with real booking-credit consumption. #81 does not implement consumption.

Keying by booking id only is a security ruling, not an ergonomic one: a
client-supplied party or quantity on an entitlement seam is a grant of somebody
else's credits chosen by the caller.

### Ruling 9 — failure creates no refund

A failing hook, or a booking that is unavailable or expired, rolls back the order
transition and the hook and confirms nothing. **No refund is created, because no
money moved.**

This is the deliberate difference from the gateway path, where a real charge
against an unconfirmable booking must stand and be refunded afterwards. Here
there is nothing to give back, and issuing a refund would be a fabricated money
fact under Ruling 5.

### Ruling 10 — cancellation and expiry must distinguish never-collected orders

Consumers that act on cancellation and expiry must tell a **never-collected**
order from a **paid** one.

- `OrderService.cancel()` is currently `pending`-only by SQL predicate and must
  be widened to permit `online_collection_not_required -> cancelled`.
- `BookingCancelledRefundHandler` currently branches on `status === 'pending'`
  alone and sends **everything else** to `remainingRefundable(order)`, which is
  `totalToman - refundedTotalToman`. A zero-collectible order has a positive
  `totalToman` (the venue balance), so the handler would attempt a real refund of
  money BeauClick never collected. It must treat the new status explicitly as
  never-collected.
- `BookingExpiredOrderHandler` reaches the same `cancel()` and inherits the same
  widening.

**No refund and no provider call is allowed for `online_collection_not_required`.**

### Ruling 11 — what this decision does not enable

It enables no deposit or full-online collection policy, no price, no percentage
base, no rounding value, no seller choice, no provider, no settlement and no
production activation. Commercial and legal **values** stay under #46; real
collection and settlement stay under #47. `V33-DEC-011` still controls which
collection modes may be enabled and is still open.

### Ruling 12 — Story #81 sizing and its ADR gate

Story #81 remains **8 SP**. Its implementation requires **ADR-044** — allocated
after re-checking the ADR directory for the next unused number — committed
**before** any schema, migration or executable code. ADR-044 is not created by
this governance pass.

## `V33-DEC-024` — Deposit capture, order lifecycle, refund ceiling and collected-money accounting

**Accounting, contract and security:** RATIFIED by the product owner on
2026-09-05 under an explicit delegation of the outstanding product decisions,
after review of the Story #82 readiness audit. Binding on Story #82 and
constraining #83, #46 and #47 — it closes none of them.

**Traces to:** Story #82 (`#41c`). **Constrains:** #83 (`#41d`), #46, #47.

### The problem, in one sentence

`total_toman`, the payment intent's amount, the ledger receivable and the refund
ceiling are today **one number wearing four names**, and deposit mode separates
them.

They coincide only because `full_payment_online` is the one collection mode that
has ever run. The readiness audit confirmed each against the tree:
`CheckoutService` passes `order.totalToman` as the intent amount;
`OrderService.markPaid()` emits `OrderPaid.totalToman = order.totalToman`;
`OrderPaidLedgerHandler` posts that same figure as `netAmountToman`; and
`recordRefundWithin`, `remainingRefundable` and `ck_orders_refund_within_total`
all cap refunds at the service total. Under a deposit each of those would
describe money BeauClick never collected.

### What the audit found that changed the answer

Two facts, neither of them in the original framing, decided two of the rulings
below.

**The outbox relay dispatches by event NAME only.** Handlers are indexed by
`eventType`; `DomainEventHandler.eventVersion` is declared and never read by the
relay, while `parseEnvelope` throws on a version mismatch. Every contract in the
catalogue is `version: 1`, so versioning has never been exercised. An
`OrderPaid v2` would therefore be delivered to the v1 loyalty handler and throw
on every delivery — a poison outbox row retried for ever. Making the relay
version-aware is real work in shared event infrastructure and is **outside #82**.

**The gateway-verified amount already exists as a durable fact.**
`payment.payment_attempts.verified_amount_toman` records what the gateway said it
captured, `NOT NULL` whenever the attempt succeeded, with replay already guarded
by `uq_payment_attempts_provider_reference`. The collected amount does not need
inventing; it needs projecting.

### Alternatives evaluated and rejected

| Considered | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| Reinterpret `OrderPaid v1`'s `totalToman` as "collected" | `V33-DEC-022` Ruling 7 forbids it, and the field is *named* `totalToman` — every consumer would keep reading a field whose meaning had silently moved |
| Add fields to `OrderPaid v1` | Additive fields do not repair a wrong meaning on an existing field; a consumer ignoring them still reads `totalToman` as captured |
| `OrderPaid v2` | Correct in principle, but poisons v1 consumers under the current relay (above). The prerequisite relay work is outside this story |
| Reuse `paid` with changed semantics | `paid` is documented as whole-order capture in the entity, the migration and the event contract, and both `retryPayment` and the refund predicate key off it. Redefining it moves four meanings at once |
| A separate collection-state column beside `status` | Two lifecycle columns on one row invite disagreement and need their own cross-column CHECK — more machinery than the question needs |
| `deposit_collected` as the state name | Names the commercial arrangement, which is #83's to define, and is wrong for any future non-deposit partial capture |
| Derive the refund ceiling from the payment schema at refund time | The truth is there, but a cross-schema read cannot be a PostgreSQL CHECK, so an enforced invariant would decay into a service convention |
| A dedicated capture table in `commerce` | A second thing to keep in agreement with the payment attempt that is already authoritative, and refunds would still need a join to enforce the ceiling |
| A sandbox-only deposit selector or route | A selector that exists only outside production is still a selector in the codebase, and it contradicts the exact-set test that proves no mode is reachable |
| Defer #82 until #83 | Leaves two known money defects live behind a product/legal gate that may not close for a long time — and they are the reason #82 exists |

### Ruling 1 — one distinct verified-deposit fact

A verified positive platform collection **below** the service total emits a new
`OrderCollectionCaptured v1`.

`OrderPaid v1` keeps its existing whole-order-capture meaning and its **exact
payload**, and is emitted only when the verified collected amount equals the
service total — which is every order that exists today, so its meaning never
moves.

**Exactly one of the two commerce events is emitted for one legitimate capture,
never both.** They are mutually exclusive by construction: the emitter branches
on whether the collected amount equals the service total.

**No `OrderPaid v2`**, for the relay reason recorded above. Relay
version-awareness is a separate concern and is not #82's to take on.

Payload field names must make the mistake unrepresentable — the service total,
the platform-collected amount and the venue balance are three distinctly named
fields, and no field is called `totalToman`.

### Ruling 2 — an explicit order state, and what it does not mean

Add the `OrderStatus` value **`online_collection_completed`** for an order whose
scheduled positive online amount was verified while a venue balance remains.

It means exactly one thing: **BeauClick's scheduled online collection
completed.** It does not mean the service was fully paid, delivered, settled, or
paid at the venue.

    pending --> online_collection_completed --> partially_refunded --> refunded
                                            --> cancelled (via refund)

- Cancellation after a collection proceeds through **refund of collected money**,
  not the never-collected cancel path #81 added.
- `paid` stays reserved for verified full-online capture.
- #81's `online_collection_not_required` is unchanged, remains disjoint, and
  never becomes `paid`.
- **No captured or terminal state returns to `pending`.**

The name deliberately mirrors `online_collection_not_required` so the two read as
one family, and deliberately says nothing about the venue balance.

### Ruling 3 — the authoritative collected amount

Add **`commerce.orders.collected_total_toman`**: the commerce-owned projection of
gateway-verified money allocated to the order.

- Zero before capture; set **atomically, once**, from the verified
  intent/attempt amount.
- It **must equal** the immutable schedule's `platform_collectible_toman` —
  a capture that does not match the schedule is not a capture this order agreed
  to.
- **Never reduced by refunds**, and **never reconstructed from `total_toman`**.
- PostgreSQL enforces `0 <= refunded_total_toman <= collected_total_toman <=
  total_toman`.
- `ck_orders_refund_within_total` is **replaced** by an exact collected-based
  constraint. After a truthful backfill this is strictly tighter, so nothing that
  was legal becomes illegal.
- The refund compare-and-swap, replay guards, terminal-status calculation and
  `remainingRefundable` use the collected total **only** — never the service
  total and never the venue balance.

Historical rows are backfilled truthfully from the pre-deposit invariant:
captured and refunded legacy rows reflect the full-online capture their contract
proves, never-collected rows receive zero, and **the migration fails loudly on an
inconsistent row rather than guessing**.

### Ruling 4 — the ledger records only money BeauClick collected

The financial projection receives **only** the verified collected amount.

Seller receivable and the existing commission calculation operate on that amount
alone. `venue_balance_toman` is never a BeauClick receivable, liability,
settlement amount or revenue fact, and appears in no ledger entry, batch or
outstanding balance.

**No commission is claimed on venue-collected money, and no second rate is
invented.** Refund reversal is limited to actual collected and refunded money, at
the rate originally recorded on the entry.

**The general commercial commission basis remains open under #46.** This ruling
decides only that uncollected money may not enter the ledger; whether commission
*accrues* on the service price is a separate decision this card does not take.

### Ruling 5 — schedule-driven execution, and no mode-selection shortcut

Production logic becomes correct for **any already-valid immutable payment
schedule**. That is the whole mechanism; there is no branch that asks what mode
was chosen, because nothing may choose one yet.

#82 adds **no** public or client-supplied collection-mode selector, **no**
hard-coded deposit amount or percentage, **no** code-registered production
policy, and **no** part of #83.

Deterministic sandbox evidence plants a valid deposit order and schedule through
an internal real-PostgreSQL fixture — the database's own
`ck_ops_mode_consistent` refuses a fixture that is not a real deposit — and then
drives the **real** intent, sandbox provider, verification, callback, order,
booking, event and ledger pipeline.

Public order creation continues to write only `full_payment_online` until #83
publishes and selects policy. **The existing exact-set test stays exact and must
not be weakened to `toContain`**; it is the only guard proving no selector
exists.

### Ruling 6 — what consumers may say

- Deposit notifications display the **verified collected amount**, never the
  service total.
- Analytics records the platform-collected payment fact and **never labels a
  venue balance as BeauClick revenue or seller receivable**.
- Receipts and browser projections keep all three server-provided schedule
  amounts and the additive order state; **clients do no money arithmetic**.
- Loyalty, journey and referral behaviour does not broaden merely because a
  deposit was captured.
- Every current `OrderPaid` consumer must be explicitly classified during
  implementation as full-capture-only, an `OrderCollectionCaptured` consumer, or
  intentionally unaffected — no consumer is left to inherit a meaning by
  accident.
- No event or log payload gains legal prose, credentials, provider secrets or
  client-supplied ownership.

### Ruling 7 — refund, cancellation and retry before policy publication

- Ordinary and automatic refunds return **at most the remaining collected
  amount**. One Toman above collected is refused **even when it is below the
  service total** — which is exactly the case today's ceiling permits.
- Duplicate-charge correction stays outside order accounting and never reduces
  `collected_total_toman` or the legitimate seller receivable.
- Until #83/#46 publish an approved retention policy, a sandbox deposit
  cancellation returns the **entire remaining collected amount**. This is the
  safest reversible interim behaviour, chosen because keeping money under no
  published policy is the harder error to undo. **It is not approval of a
  permanent commercial policy.**
- No no-show retention, cancellation penalty, dispute adjudication, settlement
  timing, payout or tax rule is decided here.
- Retry keeps the existing approved failure vocabulary and is unavailable once a
  succeeded or open charge makes another attempt unsafe. **A successfully
  captured deposit is not retryable.**

### Ruling 8 — scope, sizing and sequencing

Story #82 stays **one coherent story**. Capture, state, refund ceiling, event and
ledger must become correct **together**; splitting them would temporarily ship a
money inconsistency, which is the one outcome this card exists to prevent.

Re-estimated **8 → 13 SP**, calibrated against #40, #56, #69, #72 and #81 — all 8
— because #82 adds an event contract with consumer dispositions, four
constraints, a backfill, changes to verification, refund accounting and the
financial projection, and two user-facing corrections.

**ADR-045 is mandatory before any migration or executable code**, after
re-checking that it remains the next unused number. Delivery order remains
`#41a → #41b → #41c → #41d` (#41 → #81 → #82 → #83). #57 and #58 are independent
and untouched by this closure.

### What this decision does NOT approve

No deposit amount, percentage, calculation basis, minimum, maximum, cutoff,
permanent retention rule, commission rate, dispute rule, settlement timing, tax
treatment or legal copy. No real provider, credential, payout or production
activation. `V33-DEC-011`–`V33-DEC-017` stay open under #46; real collection and
settlement stay blocked by #47; #83 keeps its own product and legal gates.

## `V33-DEC-025` — Booking-credit accounting split and confirmation-seam correction

**Structure and contract:** RATIFIED by the product owner on 2026-09-06 under an
explicit delegation of the outstanding product decisions, after review of the
Story #58 readiness audit. Binding on Story #58 and constraining #46.

**Traces to:** Story #58 (`#40d`). **Constrains:** #46. **Releases:** the #57
prerequisite claim.

### The question

Story #58 says "exactly one credit is consumed when a booking first enters
`confirmed`". Can that be switched on?

### The evidence, read from the tree

**No.** Four facts compose into a platform-wide lockout:

| Fact | Where |
|---|---|
| The seeded `D-7` plan version confers `included_booking_credits = 0` | `20260902800001:1000-1002` — *"Zero, empty, zero, zero… the absence of an entitlement, never a choice of one"* |
| Every seller is placed on it | `V33-DEC-018`'s lazy ensure onto the seeded auto-assignable base version |
| The grant written is exactly that quantity | `booking-credit-grant.service.ts:79` |
| No other positive grant source is reachable | `ck_booking_credit_grants_source` permits only `plan_included`; #57 is unimplemented; no recurring machinery exists |

**Balance is therefore zero for every seller in existence.** Fail-closed
enforcement today would refuse the first confirmation of every new booking.

Three further facts contradict #58's own text:

- **The seam covers one of two paths.** There are exactly two production callers
  of `BookingService.confirm` — `checkout.service.ts:331` (the #81
  zero-collectible path, which invokes the hook) and `checkout.service.ts:453`
  (the #82 verified-capture path, which does not). "First entering `confirmed`"
  is not satisfied by a seam the paid path never enters.
- **`booking_credit_grants` already carries the generic source vocabulary**, so
  a balance model reading immutable grant rows does not care which story wrote
  them.
- **Two of the four return actors #58 names do not exist.** The booking actor
  CHECK is `customer | professional | system | admin`
  (`booking schema:124`), and the cancel route derives its actor from
  `BookingPartyResolver.roleFor`, which returns only `customer` or
  `professional`. There is no `business` actor at all, and no production route
  produces `admin`.

### Alternatives evaluated and rejected

| Considered | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| Ship #58 whole and enforce globally | Refuses every seller's next confirmation. The story cannot be *completed* while its enforcement half depends on a #46 value that has never been set |
| Treat a zero grant as unlimited | Creates a free entitlement in code with no decision behind it — exactly what `V33-DEC-009` forbids when it says no allowance may exist as a code constant, default, fallback or seed |
| Re-estimate without splitting | Honest about size, but parks a finished, unactivatable enforcement path behind an open commercial gate for an unknown period |
| Defer #58 entirely | Leaves the seam covering one of two confirmation paths — a latent gap that widens each time a confirmation path is added |
| Block #58 behind #57 | #57 cannot produce a paid entitlement while #47 blocks a seller-payment rail. A dormant purchase record is not credit, so this waits on the same external gate one story further away |
| A mutable balance counter | Cannot be audited against its own history, and makes every concurrent confirmation contend on one row for a value that is a pure function of immutable facts |
| An asynchronous/eventual return consumer | Adds a commercial event with no independent consumer, against `V33-DEC-024`, and can lose a credit |

### Ruling 1 — split foundation from global enforcement

Issue #58 becomes **`#58a` — booking-credit accounting foundation and selective
enforcement**, re-estimated **8 → 13 SP**. Exactly one child is created,
**`#58b` — global booking-credit enforcement activation**, at **3 SP**. The
family becomes **16 SP**.

The split is **mandatory**, not stylistic: the current grant is exactly zero,
every seller receives only that grant, no positive source is production-reachable,
and immediate fail-closed enforcement would reject every new confirmation.

**Zero is never reinterpreted as unlimited.** It is an explicit absence of
entitlement.

### Ruling 2 — dormant versus exhausted, and why they are different states

`#58a` may install the complete durable accounting model and bind it to both
confirmation paths.

- For a party that has **never received any positive booking-credit grant**, the
  entitlement layer is **dormant**: confirmation proceeds and no consumption row
  is written. This is a rollout state, recorded as a distinct closed internal
  outcome (`not_configured` or equivalent).
- Once a party holds **at least one positive immutable grant**, enforcement is
  **active for that party**. Exhausting its balance to zero refuses a new first
  confirmation — it does **not** fall back to dormant.
- A zero-quantity grant never activates enforcement and never contributes
  balance.
- No code constant, seed, fallback or migration may confer a positive allowance.

**The distinction is the whole safety property.** "Never configured" and
"configured and spent" are the same number and opposite meanings; collapsing them
is precisely how a rollout state becomes a permanent free entitlement.

`#58b` is the later explicit switch to global fail-closed enforcement and cannot
become Ready until #46 ratifies a production-positive grant source and a rollout
treatment for existing sellers.

### Ruling 3 — one confirmation-wide seam

The stale "zero-collectible hook" contract is corrected. Consumption applies when
a booking **first enters `confirmed`**, whether the order collected zero, a
deposit or the full total.

`#58a` replaces the zero-only seam with **one domain-neutral confirmation
entitlement port**, bound through the composition root, invoked from **both**
production paths inside the caller's transaction. An absent binding remains a
**boot failure**, as `V33-DEC-023` Ruling 8 established.

The port accepts the caller's `EntityManager` and `bookingId` **only** — no
owner, party, subscription, grant, quantity, mode or client-derived field. No
module acquires a forbidden cross-domain import: both call sites are already in
`apps/api`, the one scope ADR-011 permits to see every domain.

### Ruling 4 — transaction and compensation semantics

Credit consumption and the first successful booking confirmation are **one
transactional outcome**: both commit or neither does.

- **Zero-collectible path:** insufficient configured balance refuses the
  confirmation and rolls back the order's zero-collection transition. No refund,
  because no money moved.
- **Verified capture path:** the authoritative payment/capture fact **remains
  recorded** when the booking cannot be confirmed for entitlement or slot
  reasons; the customer is refunded exactly the **verified collected amount**
  through the existing idempotent compensation. This is not new machinery — it is
  the path #82 already built for a booking that became unavailable after capture.
- **No consumption may survive an unconfirmed booking.** The booking row is
  locked while proven pending before the credit is written, so a later
  confirmation cannot lose a race after the consumption commits.
- No second pool connection, asynchronous event, eventual consumer or
  best-effort credit compensation.

### Ruling 5 — #57 is not a structural prerequisite

`#57` is **removed** as a prerequisite of `#58a`, which consumes generic
immutable grant rows without regard to their source.

The database CHECK truthfully permits only `plan_included` today and `#58a` must
**not** widen it early. `#57` later widens the source and writes
`custom_purchase` grants once it has an authoritative paid-purchase fact; those
rows then enter the same balance without changing the consumption or return
model. `#57` is otherwise untouched by this closure.

### Ruling 6 — immutable accounting and deterministic allocation

Balance is **derived**: immutable positive grants, minus immutable consumptions,
plus immutable returns. No mutable counter is the source of truth.

- One consumption keyed uniquely by **booking id**, snapshotting at least the
  charged subscriber party and the selected grant/subscription period. A replay
  or concurrent confirmation of the same booking returns the original outcome and
  consumes nothing extra.
- A return is a **separate immutable fact**, keyed uniquely to its consumption.
  The consumption row is never updated or deleted.
- Allocation is **oldest available eligible grant first**, with a stable id
  tie-breaker. Expiry is currently database-pinned `NULL`
  (`ck_booking_credit_grants_no_expiry`), so **no expiry priority or expiry
  behaviour is invented here**; a future expiry decision may version this rule
  visibly.
- A booking charged to a grant and period **remains charged there after
  rescheduling**, including across a future term boundary.
- Balance decisions are serialised per snapshotted seller party by a
  **transaction-scoped PostgreSQL advisory lock** taken before the conditional
  insert. A bare `INSERT … SELECT` under `READ COMMITTED` is **insufficient**:
  two transactions can each read a balance of one and both insert, because
  neither sees the other's uncommitted row. Application-memory locks are also
  insufficient. The mechanism is the one already shipped for the AI conversation
  cap (`ai-conversation.service.ts:156`).
- PostgreSQL enforces unique booking consumption, unique return, non-negative
  quantities and immutability.

### Ruling 7 — the authoritative party

The charged party is resolved from the order's immutable `seller_party_type` /
`seller_party_id` snapshot for that booking, and is **snapshotted again on the
consumption row**. Current professional affiliation is never re-resolved and no
caller or event may supply a party.

Every return credits the **snapshotted** party, even after the professional joins
or leaves a business or ownership changes. This restates for credits what
`V33-DEC-020` ruled for finance reads: live affiliation must not decide
historical money.

Missing, inconsistent or non-unique booking/order facts **fail closed** rather
than consuming another party's credits.

### Ruling 8 — the return boundary

`#58a` implements returns only for cancellation paths that are both **reachable
and already structurally authorised**: seller-side cancellation, represented by
the existing `professional` actor, and a genuine platform/system cancellation of
an already-confirmed booking.

- **No `business` actor is added.** None exists in the booking vocabulary; a
  business owner acting through the seller route is represented by
  `professional` until a separately approved role model changes it.
- **No administrator return path is claimed.** `admin` exists in the database
  vocabulary but no production cancellation route produces it.
- Customer-cancellation return and retention, no-show retention, cutoffs, grace
  quantity and notification thresholds remain **open under #46**.
- Pending expiry consumes and returns nothing. Rescheduling consumes and returns
  nothing.
- Returns are **synchronous and transactional** with the qualifying cancellation.
  No commercial event and no `ServiceName` member is added, because no
  independent consumer exists (`V33-DEC-024`).

### Ruling 9 — audit, privacy and surface boundary

Consumption and return facts are **retained business records** under ADR-027,
with export and erasure behaviour stated explicitly and no deletion that would
break the counterparty's entitlement ledger. Audit uses **closed, server-authored
actions and reasons**; customer cancellation prose is never copied into
commercial audit, events, logs or metrics.

**No new seller or customer HTTP route is part of `#58a`** — in particular, no
balance dashboard. Existing booking request and response shapes accept no owner,
party, grant, quantity, subscription or entitlement selector. Existing bookings
remain readable and manageable at zero balance; enforcement affects only a
genuinely new first confirmation.

### Ruling 10 — the activation child

`#58b` owns **only** the global fail-closed activation and its rollout proof,
after a positive production grant source is ratified. It does not redesign the
schema, the consumption algorithm or the return model.

It depends on `#58a` and **#46**. It does not depend on a real gateway unless the
positive source #46 chooses itself requires #47.

Labels: `status:blocked`, `gate:product`, `priority:p1`, `track:engineering`,
`type:story`, milestone V3.3. **`gate:legal` is deliberately not applied**: no
existing legal decision blocks the activation *contract*. What blocks it is a
product value — the included quantity — which is a `gate:product` concern. If
#46's eventual answer carries approved customer-facing copy or a legal term, the
legal gate is added then, on evidence.

### What this decision does NOT approve

No positive booking-credit quantity, price, grace, expiry, overage, cutoff,
retention rule, no-show penalty, notification threshold or legal copy. No
zero-as-unlimited fallback. No custom-purchase grant, gateway, recurring billing,
provider, payout or settlement. No customer-cancellation retention outcome. #46
and #47 keep every gate they had.

## `V33-DEC-026` — Custom booking-credit purchase: split, snapshot and paid-activation boundary

**Structure, contract and security:** RATIFIED by the product owner on 2026-09-06,
after review of the Story #57 readiness and decision-closure audit. Binding on
Story #57 and its new child, and constraining #46 and #47.

**Traces to:** Story #57 (`#40c`). **Constrains:** #46, #47. **Depends on:**
`V33-DEC-009` (the catalogue this story prices against), `V33-DEC-018`/`V33-DEC-019`
(the subscription and its `workspaceRef` surface), `V33-DEC-025` (the balance model
these credits enter).

**Corrected 2026-09-06 by `V33-DEC-027`.** This card ratified that a seller submits
only a quantity and that the server prices it from the administrator's catalogue,
and did not say WHICH schedule. A code audit found the server has no authoritative
way to choose one: several `booking_credit` schedules are legal and the anti-overlap
constraint is key-scoped. `V33-DEC-027` supplies the missing structure — an
administrator-set, nullable, stable schedule key on the immutable plan version,
snapshotted by the subscription, with the ACTIVE VERSION resolved per request — and
re-estimates #57 **8 → 13 SP**. Nothing in the rulings below is reversed; Ruling 4's
snapshot rule and Ruling 6's authorization are the ones it builds on.

### The question

Story #57 says a seller "may buy" a booking-credit quantity and have its price
fixed at the moment of purchase. Can that be built?

### The evidence, read from the tree

**Half of it can, and the half that cannot is blocked more deeply than #47.**

| Fact | Where |

| The tiered pricing engine, its exact integer arithmetic and its closed refusal vocabulary already exist and are unit-tested | `commercial-catalogue-contract.ts:329`, `:140-160` |

| **No `booking_credit` schedule exists.** The migration seeds exactly one schedule — `D-7-base-price`, `seller_plan`, bounds `[1,1]`, one tier at zero | `20260902800001:950-979` |
| An administrator can create and publish one without engineering choosing any value | `commercial-catalogue.controller.ts:97,131,172` |
| **A seller could make exactly ONE custom purchase, ever** — `uq_booking_credit_grants_once UNIQUE (subscription_id, source, period_index)` with `period_index DEFAULT 0` | `20260903800001:341` |

So the purchase **record** and its immutable price snapshot are buildable today
and independently testable — an administrator supplies the commercial numbers, so
engineering chooses none. The **paid activation** is not merely gated by #47: the
adapter it would bind does not exist.

### Alternatives evaluated and rejected

| Considered | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| Ship #57 whole | Its central verb, "buy", has no rail and no adapter. The story could not be demonstrated, let alone completed |
| Block #57 entirely until #47 | Parks finished, correct, fully testable work behind an external gate of unknown duration — the trade `V33-DEC-025` already refused for #58 |

| Write the grant when the request is created and mark it unpaid | A dormant "awaiting payment" grant is entitlement the moment any query forgets the flag. `V33-DEC-025` Ruling 2 made the same argument about dormant versus exhausted |

### Ruling 1 — split the story, and keep #57 for the rail-independent half

Issue #57 becomes **`#40c-1` — Custom booking-credit purchase record and immutable
price snapshot**, re-estimated **5 → 8 SP**, unblocked and Ready. *(Re-estimated
again to **13 SP** on 2026-09-06 by `V33-DEC-027`, which added the plan-to-schedule
binding and its snapshot propagation to this child's scope.)* Exactly one child
is created, **#99 `#40c-2` — Paid activation of a custom booking-credit purchase**, at
**5 SP**, `status:blocked`, `gate:external` on #47. The family becomes **13 SP**.

The split is **mandatory, not stylistic**: no gateway adapter exists, so the paid
half cannot be written, reviewed or demonstrated, while the record and its snapshot
can be built and proved against an administrator-published schedule today.

#57 keeps its number, following the `#56`/`#58` precedent in which the parent number
carries the shippable foundation.

### Ruling 2 — the lifecycle, and the state that must not exist

Child 1's purchase lifecycle is exactly **`awaiting_payment | abandoned`**.
**Neither state confers entitlement**, and child 1 contains **no code path that
writes a booking-credit grant at all** — an absence that is asserted by test, not
promised by a comment.

Child 2 may add exactly one state, **`paid`**, and only together with the verified
payment transition of Ruling 3.

The forbidden shape is a purchase that is treated as entitlement while unpaid. It
is prevented structurally rather than by discipline: with no writer, there is
nothing to forget a flag.

### Ruling 3 — the single transition at which credit may be granted

A `custom_purchase` grant may be written **only** in the same transaction that
records an adapter-verified authoritative payment fact.

- A quote writes nothing.
- A purchase request writes no grant.
- An unverified callback constructs no verified fact.
- A sandbox self-assertion is not an authoritative fact.

**The commercial domain cannot manufacture the verified fact.** The port accepts a
value object that only the `apps/api` payment adapter may construct, so
"no sandbox path marks a grant paid on its own say-so" is a property of the type
system and the composition root rather than a rule someone must remember.

### Ruling 4 — the snapshot is authoritative, and no quote duration is chosen

The immutable purchase snapshot is taken from the schedule version **active at the
instant the request is created**, inside the caller's transaction, following the
precedent `ADR-042 §5` set for subscriptions: a snapshot must record what the
seller was actually offered, not whatever is active later.

Later editing, retirement or supersession of that schedule version **never rewrites
a written purchase**. Verification asserts the verified amount equals the snapshot
total.

**No quote-expiry duration is selected by this decision.** The creation and
effective instants are recorded so #46 may later add a versioned expiry rule as a
visible change. Engineering must not invent one.

### Ruling 5 — idempotency, uniqueness, and the constraint that has to move

| Fact | Key |
|---|---|
| Purchase request | `UNIQUE (subscription_id, request_key)` |
| Authoritative payment | `UNIQUE (provider_key, provider_reference)` |
| Grant from a purchase | `UNIQUE (purchase_id)` |

Before any custom grant can exist, `uq_booking_credit_grants_once
UNIQUE (subscription_id, source, period_index)` is **replaced by a partial unique
index restricted to `source = 'plan_included'`**. This preserves #56's invariant
exactly — one plan-included grant per subscription and period — while allowing a
seller more than one custom purchase, which the current constraint silently forbids.

`uq_bcg_identity` and `fk_bcc_grant_identity` are **untouched**: they are what makes
`#58a`'s consumption reference a storage guarantee.

No advisory lock is required. Unlike `#58a`'s balance decision, every write here is
made idempotent by a unique constraint over data the caller supplies.

### Ruling 6 — authorization reuses what already works

The seller surface reuses **`bc_manage_own_subscription`** and the opaque
`workspaceRef`. **No new capability is created.**

Ownership is re-evaluated **per request** through the existing owned-party resolver.
**Staff affiliation is never authority** — the rule `V33-DEC-020` established for
finance reads and `V33-DEC-025` Ruling 7 restated for credits, applied here for the
third time and for the same reason: current affiliation must not decide money.

### Ruling 7 — retention, export, erasure

Purchase records and authoritative-payment records are **retained obligations**
under ADR-027. They record what the platform undertook to provide and what was
verifiably collected for it.

- **Export** returns the seller's own purchase facts — quantity, unit price, total,
  currency, state and instants — and **never the provider reference**, which is a
  reconciliation key rather than the seller's own datum.
- **Erasure** deletes nothing and anonymizes nothing here, and reports truthful
  counts saying so.
- Audit uses closed, server-authored actions with a mandatory reason, written in the
  mutation's own transaction — the correction `#58a` had to make after auditing a
  ledger through a logger whose lines survive a rollback.

### Ruling 8 — one public refusal

Every pricing cause — absent, unpublished, superseded, incomplete, out of the
schedule's own bounds, or changed concurrently — maps to **one** public code,
**`purchase_unavailable`**. Internal catalogue causes stay internal, so a caller
cannot enumerate the catalogue through error messages.

Ownership refusals keep their existing non-enumerating 404 behaviour: malformed,
foreign, stale and nonexistent `workspaceRef` remain byte-identical, as
`V33-DEC-020` Ruling 12 requires.

### Ruling 9 — the #46 and #47 boundary, stated exactly

| Ratified now (structure) | Open under #46, admin-configurable | Blocked by #47 |
|---|---|---|
| The purchase table, its snapshot fields and immutability | Every price, tier, minimum and maximum quantity, UI preset | Real seller-to-platform collection |
| Idempotency and uniqueness keys, including the partial-index replacement | Quote validity duration | Any authoritative payment fact |
| The lifecycle and the single grant transition | Refund and clawback of purchased credits | Provider adapter and merchant onboarding |
| Authorization, refusal vocabulary, privacy, audit | Credit expiry and carry-forward | Settlement and reconciliation |
| | Tax, invoice and revenue-recognition treatment | |

### Ruling 10 — non-goals

Not in either child: refund or clawback implementation; credit expiry; recurring
billing; tax or invoice treatment; revenue recognition; external provider
activation; any UI; any event with no consumer; any deployment, tag or Release.

Neither child adds a `ServiceName` member, and the commercial domain still has no
outbox.

### What this decision does NOT approve

No positive booking-credit price, tier, minimum or maximum purchasable quantity, UI
preset, quote-validity duration, refund term, expiry period, tax treatment or legal
copy. #46 and #47 keep every gate they had.

**ADR-047 is required before any schema or code** for `#40c-1`, following the
ADR-044/045/046 precedent, and is deliberately not written by this decision.

## `V33-DEC-027` — Admin-managed booking-credit purchase-schedule selection

**Structure, contract and security:** RATIFIED by the product owner on 2026-09-06,
under an explicit delegation, after a post-ratification code audit found one
structural question `V33-DEC-026` left unanswered. Binding on Story #57 and
constraining #99, #46 and the administrator catalogue surface.

**Traces to:** Story #57 (`#40c-1`). **Corrects:** `V33-DEC-026`.
**Depends on:** `V33-DEC-009` (the versioned catalogue), `V33-DEC-018` (the
subscription snapshot). **Constrains:** #99 (`#40c-2`), #46.

### The question `V33-DEC-026` did not answer

`V33-DEC-026` ratified that a seller submits **only a quantity** and that the
server resolves its price from the administrator-published catalogue. It did not
say **which** catalogue row.

Four facts, each read from the tree, compose into an unanswerable question:

| Fact | Where |
|---|---|
| The price resolver takes a schedule key as its first argument: `resolvePrice(scheduleKey, at, quantity)` | `commercial-catalogue.service.ts:597` |
| The seller may not supply one. `V33-DEC-026` forbids any owner, party, subscription, plan, schedule, tier, price, amount, currency, status or actor field on the request | `V33-DEC-026` R6, Story #57 acceptance criteria |
| **Several `booking_credit` schedules are legal.** `commercial.price_schedules` is keyed by `schedule_key` and `purpose` carries no uniqueness at all — `ck_price_schedules_purpose` only restricts the vocabulary to `seller_plan \| booking_credit` | `20260902800001:154-166` |
| **Overlap is prevented only within one key.** `ex_price_schedule_versions_no_overlap` is `EXCLUDE USING gist (schedule_key WITH =, tstzrange(...) WITH &&) WHERE lifecycle_state <> 'draft'`, so two *different* keys may each hold a published version active at the same instant | `20260902800001:312-317` |

And nothing on either side of the subscription selects one.
`plan_versions.price_schedule_version_id` is what the **plan itself costs** — its

The audit confirms the gap is live rather than theoretical: `resolvePrice` has
**only test callers** today, each passing a key the test itself created, and no
production code names a `booking_credit` schedule key at all.

**So the server has no authoritative way to select a schedule**, and every way of
inventing one at implementation time would violate the contract that was already
ratified.

### Alternatives evaluated and rejected

| Considered | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| Accept a `scheduleKey` on the seller request | Directly violates `V33-DEC-026` R6. A client-chosen price source is a client-chosen price, and the whole snapshot model exists to prevent one |
| Take the first, or the lexicographically first, active `booking_credit` schedule | Makes the price a function of row order or of an administrator's naming. Two legitimately published schedules would silently make one of them dead, and which one would change as keys are added |
| Hard-code a global schedule key, or read one from an environment variable | `V33-DEC-009` forbids a commercial value existing as a code constant, default, fallback or seed, and an environment variable is a deployment-time commercial decision made by whoever edits the file |
| Rule that exactly one `booking_credit` schedule may ever exist | Buys determinism by foreclosing per-plan pricing for ever. #46 has not decided that every plan charges the same for credits, and a decision that quietly answers a `#46` question is the shape this register exists to prevent |
| Bind an exact price-schedule **version** to the subscription | Freezes that subscription's top-up pricing at activation. An administrator could never reprice credits without migrating every existing subscription, and "migrating subscriptions" means rewriting immutable snapshots |
| A mutable, unversioned global pointer to the current credit schedule | Redirects an existing subscription's pricing source invisibly, with no version, no audit and no snapshot. It is the mutable-counter mistake `V33-DEC-025` refused, wearing a different column |

### Ruling 1 — the binding lives on the immutable plan version

The administrator chooses custom-credit pricing **per seller-plan version**,
through a **nullable, stable `bookingCreditScheduleKey`** reference. It points at
a `commercial.price_schedules` row whose `purpose` is exactly `booking_credit`.

Nullable because most plan versions will not offer custom credits, and because the
seeded `D-7` must remain honestly unconfigured. A plan version is already
immutable, so the binding inherits that immutability for free.

### Ruling 2 — the seller never names a price source

The seller supplies **quantity only**. No schedule key, schedule version, tier,
price, amount or currency may appear on any request DTO, and a body carrying one
is **rejected rather than ignored**.

The purchase service derives the key from the **seller's active subscription
snapshot**, identified by the owned `workspaceRef` — the same opaque reference and
the same per-request ownership re-evaluation `V33-DEC-026` R6 already ratified.

### Ruling 3 — the subscription snapshots the key at activation

A subscription **snapshots the nullable booking-credit schedule key when it is
activated**, beside the entitlement fields it already snapshots.

Later plan edits, plan supersession, ownership changes and affiliation changes
**cannot redirect that subscription's pricing source**. This is `V33-DEC-018`'s
rule — a subscription reads its own snapshot, never live catalogue state — applied
to one more field, for the third time in this register.

### Ruling 4 — bind the key, resolve the version at request time

The binding is to a **stable schedule key**, never to an exact schedule version.

For each request the service resolves the version of that key **published and
active at the request instant**, inside the caller's transaction, and snapshots
that exact version, tier, unit price, total, currency and instant on the purchase
row.

This is the whole point of the ruling: an administrator can version credit prices
forward without migrating a single subscription, and every historical purchase
keeps the price it was actually offered. It is also the reason a version binding
was rejected — that would have frozen each subscription's top-up price at
activation.

### Ruling 5 — different plan versions may share or differ

Two plan versions may reference the **same** schedule key or **different** ones.

That preserves per-plan credit pricing as a future administrative choice without
creating a second purchase path, and without this decision choosing whether such
pricing should exist. That choice remains #46's.

### Ruling 6 — every failure is the same public answer

A **null** binding, a **missing** schedule, a **wrong-purpose** schedule, **no
active published version**, **incomplete tiers**, an **out-of-bounds quantity**
and a **concurrent catalogue change** all fail closed as the one public
`purchase_unavailable` outcome ratified by `V33-DEC-026` R8.

**There is no fallback schedule**, and a caller cannot distinguish these causes —
so the catalogue cannot be enumerated through refusals.

### Ruling 7 — the database refuses a wrong-purpose reference

A binding to a `seller_plan` schedule must be **unwritable**, not merely rejected
by whichever code path happened to construct it.

ADR-047 chooses the narrow PostgreSQL mechanism — a composite identity carrying
`purpose`, a trigger, or an equivalent — but it **may not rely on a comment,
controller validation or service check alone**. The precedent is the one this
schema already sets: `ck_booking_credit_grants_no_expiry` and
`ex_price_schedule_versions_no_overlap` are guarantees precisely because they hold
against raw SQL, a future service and a migration that forgets.

### Ruling 8 — nothing existing is backfilled

Existing `D-7` plan versions and every existing subscription keep a **null**
binding. **They are not backfilled to a fabricated schedule.**

The consequence is stated rather than hidden: **Story #57 ships safely
unavailable.** A purchase becomes possible only after an administrator creates a
`booking_credit` schedule, publishes a version of it, and publishes a new plan
version carrying its key — three deliberate administrative acts, none of which
engineering performs.

This is the same shape `#58a` shipped in: a complete, correct mechanism that is
dormant until an administrator configures it, rather than a mechanism with a
default that nobody decided.

### Ruling 9 — no commercial value enters the code

No schedule key, price, tier, quantity bound, preset, allowance or expiry is
seeded, defaulted, hard-coded or inferred anywhere. Every commercial value stays
under #46 and inside the existing administrator catalogue.

### Ruling 10 — Story #57 is re-estimated 8 → 13 SP

Story #57 remains **one story** and is **not** split again. It is re-estimated
**8 → 13 SP**, because it now also carries the versioned plan-to-schedule binding,
its database-enforced purpose constraint, subscription snapshot propagation, the
administrator surface for setting the binding, and the adversarial proofs for all
of them — in addition to the purchase table and seller surface already in scope.

#99 (`#40c-2`) remains **5 SP** and blocked on #47. It consumes this binding and
snapshot; it does not redefine them.

### The lifecycle, end to end

1. **Administrator** creates a `booking_credit` price schedule and publishes a
   version with tiers. *(Existing admin surface. No new route needed for this
   step.)*
2. **Administrator** publishes a plan version whose `bookingCreditScheduleKey`
   names that schedule. The database refuses a `seller_plan` key here.
3. **Seller** is on a subscription that snapshotted that key at activation.
4. **Seller** requests a quantity. The service reads the key from the
   subscription snapshot, resolves the version of that key active **now**, inside
   the caller's transaction, and writes an immutable purchase snapshot of the
   exact version, tier, unit price, total, currency and instant.
5. **Administrator** later publishes a new version of the same key. New requests
   price against it; **every existing purchase is unchanged**; no subscription
   moves.
6. **Any break in that chain** — no binding, no schedule, no active version,
   incomplete tiers, out-of-bounds quantity — yields `purchase_unavailable`.

### Compatibility

Nothing existing changes state. `D-7` keeps a null binding, every current
subscription keeps a null binding, the seeded `seller_plan` schedule is untouched,
and no migration backfills anything. The only observable difference before an
administrator acts is that #57's surface exists and refuses.

### The #46 and #47 boundary

| Ratified now (structure) | Open under #46, admin-configurable | Blocked by #47 |
|---|---|---|
| Where the binding lives, and that it is nullable and versioned | Whether credit pricing differs per plan at all | Real seller-to-platform collection |
| That the subscription snapshots it at activation | Every price, tier, bound, preset and key | Any authoritative payment fact |
| Request-time resolution of the active version | Whether `D-7` should ever carry a binding | Provider adapter and onboarding |
| Database-enforced purpose, fail-closed refusals | Quote validity, refund, expiry, tax | Settlement and reconciliation |

### Non-goals

This decision creates no schedule, key, price, tier, bound, preset or allowance;
no seeded or default binding; no backfill; no new seller route beyond the one
`V33-DEC-026` already ratified; no payment, grant, order, intent, ledger entry,
commercial event or `ServiceName` member; no provider; no UI; and no deployment,
tag or Release.

### What this decision does NOT approve

No booking-credit price, tier, minimum or maximum purchasable quantity, UI preset,
quote-validity duration, refund term, expiry period, tax treatment or legal copy.
No decision about whether credit pricing *should* vary by plan. #46 and #47 keep
every gate they had.

**ADR-047 remains required before any schema or code for Story #57, and must now
cover both `V33-DEC-026` and this decision.**
