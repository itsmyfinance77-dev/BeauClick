## `V33-DEC-001` — Revenue model

**Decision.** Customers pay no BeauClick fee to discover, compare, book or
manage appointments. Seller subscriptions are the primary predictable revenue.
Optional acquisition fees, payment processing, premium AI/SMS/analytics,
advertising, retail and B2B revenue are separate policies and entitlements.

**Constraint.** BeauClick may not present commission on venue-collected money as
authoritative without an authoritative collection fact. Full-service commission
is therefore not the default for deposit-online/balance-at-venue.

## `V33-DEC-002` — Actor and workspace model

**Decision.** A person has one identity and may enter multiple workspaces. Roles
such as salon manager, receptionist, practitioner, finance operator, inventory
operator and B2B sales are scoped memberships. Salon, laser/beauty clinic,
maison, retail, wholesale, academy, mobile team and multi-location organisation
are vertical classifications.

## `V33-DEC-003` — Collection modes

**Decision.** The closed v1 modes are `pay_at_venue`,
`deposit_online_balance_at_venue` and `full_payment_online`.

For deposit mode, the receipt always distinguishes:

- full disclosed service price;
- amount collected by BeauClick;
- balance payable directly to the seller.

## `V33-DEC-004` — Immutable policy history

**Decision.** A business edits future policy by creating a new version. Every
booking stores a snapshot of the exact terms and copy version it accepted.
Historical outcomes never read live business configuration.

## `V33-DEC-005` — Domain responsibility

**Decision.** Booking owns lifecycle facts. Commercial Policy evaluates those
facts against a snapshot. Commerce owns price and collection schedule. Payment
executes collection/refund. Financial owns pending/available/settled allocation.
Notification communicates the result. No domain reaches into another's tables.

## `V33-DEC-006` — Fulfilment remains professional-keyed

**Decision.** A business is the seller/workspace, not the booking resource.
Professional and slot remain the booking concurrency keys. Locations, devices,
rooms and aggregate business calendars are additive resource/permission work.

## `V33-DEC-007` — Four independent controls

**Decision.** Rollout flag, plan entitlement, business policy and operational
kill switch are evaluated separately and fail closed. None substitutes for
authorization or audit.

## `V33-DEC-008` — Open production parameters

**Status:** OPEN under #46 and #47.

## `V33-DEC-009` — Plan catalogue and booking-credit pricing

**Structure:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-02 after review of the
Story #40 readiness packet. **Values:** OPEN under #46.

The owner ratified the structure below. It is binding on implementation. It
fixes no price, allowance, bound, term or legal wording, and it activates
nothing in production.

### Plan versions and lifecycle

- Seller plans are immutable, versioned records.
- The version lifecycle is `draft -> published -> retired`.
- A retired version can neither be reactivated nor edited.
- Restoring earlier terms requires a new version, never an edit.
- Activation windows for the same plan key must not overlap.
- Existing subscriptions, purchases, grants and bookings retain their original
  snapshots; retiring a version prevents new selection and purchase but changes
  no existing grant.

### Subscriber party

- The subscriber party is `professional | business`.
- The subscriber party is always resolved server-side.
- A client-supplied professional, business, owner or subscriber id is never
  trusted.

### Base workspace

- The base workspace is `D-7`: an automatically assigned, published, zero-price
  plan version.
- Every seller therefore has one explicit subscription history rather than an
  implicit fallback.

### Administrator capability

Administrators may define plans, billing terms, included booking credits, seats,
locations, capability sets, credit quantities, quantity tiers, price schedules,
activation dates and UI presets.

### Seller purchase paths

- A seller may select an active admin-defined plan, or enter a desired positive
  booking-credit quantity.
- A plan holder may use the same custom purchase path for top-ups.
- Plan-included and custom-purchase grants share one balance but retain their
  source.

### Pricing and snapshots

- Pricing uses immutable versioned tiers; a flat price is a one-tier schedule.
- Every purchase snapshots quantity, resolved tier, integer unit price, integer
  total, currency, schedule version, subscriber party and effective instant.
- Currency is IRT only unless a later owner decision widens it.

### Production safety

- No allowance, including 200, may exist as a code constant, default, fallback
  or seed.
- An unconfigured plan or price schedule refuses safely rather than falling back.
- No production price is active by default.
- No recurring billing and no external provider is part of Story #40.

## `V33-DEC-010` — Booking-credit consumption, return and overage

**Structure:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-02. **Values:** OPEN
under #46.

### Consumption

- Consume exactly one booking credit when a booking first enters `confirmed`.
- Consumption is keyed by booking id.
- Retries, replays and concurrent duplicate confirmations consume nothing extra.
- Pending or expired holds consume nothing.
- Rescheduling does not consume another credit.
- A booking confirmed in term N and rescheduled into term N+1 remains charged to
  term N.

### Return

- A return is a separate immutable and idempotent fact.
- The original consumption is never deleted or rewritten.
- Credits return to the subscriber party snapshotted on the consumption row; the
  seller's current affiliation is never re-resolved during a return.
- Provider, business, administrator or platform cancellation returns the credit.

### Customer cancellation and no-show

Treatment is versioned policy, not a constant:

- an early-cancellation return may be enabled by an approved policy;
- late-cancellation/no-show retention may be enabled by an approved policy;
- there is no hardcoded cutoff and no hardcoded retained outcome;
- exact values remain open under #46.

### Zero balance and overage

- Existing bookings remain readable and manageable at zero balance.
- Only a new qualifying confirmation is refused at zero balance.
- No negative balance, no hidden charge and no automatic overage.
- Grace quantity defaults to zero and is disabled until configured.

### Grant durability

- Already-issued grants are not retroactively changed by upgrade, downgrade or
  cancellation.
- Prepaid/custom credits do not expire by default while Legal has not approved
  expiry. The model may carry a nullable expiry, but production expiry cannot be
  activated without Legal approval.
- Refund of a credit purchase remains a separate open Product/Legal decision.
- Revenue recognition of unused credits remains an Accounting decision.

## `V33-DEC-001` correction — free booking is not a zero service price

**Recorded 2026-09-02** as part of the same owner review, because the Story #40
readiness packet's finding `C1` was interpreted too broadly.

The defect it reports is real. [`checkout.service.ts`](../../../v3/apps/api/src/checkout/checkout.service.ts)
initiates a payment and returns a gateway redirect only when the order total is
positive, and the only production confirmation path is the payment callback. A
genuinely zero-total order therefore receives no redirect and stays `pending`.

But `V33-DEC-001` does **not** mean the service price is zero. It means the
customer pays no separate BeauClick booking fee. Under `pay_at_venue` the full
service price may be non-zero while:

```text
platformCollectibleNow = 0
```

Consequently:

- a temporary architecture predicated only on `totalToman === 0` is **not**
  approved, and no `#40-pre` story is created;
- the proper no-online-collection confirmation path belongs to Story #41, which
  must distinguish the full service price, the amount collectible by BeauClick
  now, and the balance payable at the venue;
- Story #40's final booking integration (#58, `#40d`) depends on that Story #41
  capability;
- the zero-total-order defect remains explicitly visible in Story #41's
  acceptance criteria. No code is changed by this ratification.

## Security, privacy and boundary decisions

**Recorded 2026-09-02** alongside the `V33-DEC-009`/`V33-DEC-010` ratification.

## `V33-DEC-018` — Seller subscription lifecycle structure

**Structure:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-03 after review of the
Story #56 readiness packet. **Values:** OPEN under #46.

The owner ratified the structure below. It is binding on implementation. It
fixes no price, allowance, seat count, billing term, expiry period or legal
wording, and it activates nothing in production.

### Story split

- Issue #56 becomes **`#56a` — Subscription Foundation**, 8 SP, with **no
  seller-facing route of any kind**.
- Exactly one new issue is created: **#69 (`#56b`) — Seller Subscription
  Surface**, 5 SP. No second replacement for #56 exists.
- Paid activation belongs to neither and has no issue yet.

### Subscriber ownership

- The subscriber party is resolved from **ownership only**, server-side, once,
  at subscription creation.
- `subscriber_party_type` and `subscriber_party_id` are snapshotted onto the
  row. Ownership is never re-resolved from current staff affiliation.
- Affiliated staff may never mutate the employing business's subscription.

This is a deliberate divergence from `ProviderBackedFinancialPartyResolver`,
whose staff-affiliation branch is correct for earnings and wrong for
subscriptions. It restates for subscriptions what `V33-DEC-010` already fixed
for returns: the seller's current affiliation is never re-resolved.

### Affiliation collision

- An independent professional holding an active personal subscription who later
  joins a business **keeps that subscription, which continues independently**.
  It is not superseded, transferred, cancelled or made dormant.
- While that professional acts as staff for a salon booking, #58 consumes from
  the **snapshotted business party**, never from the professional's personal
  subscription.
- If the professional later becomes independent again, their personal
  subscription remains available.
- This ruling holds **while subscriptions are zero-price and non-recurring**.
  Any paid-renewal consequence of holding two subscriptions remains open under
  #46/#47 and must not be invented.

### Base workspace

- Existing sellers receive `D-7` through a **migration backfill**.
- New sellers receive it through **one idempotent lazy-ensure method**.
- One active subscription per subscriber party, enforced by a **partial unique
  index** rather than by application code.
- No event and no scheduler. Neither has a named consumer or an approved
  trigger.

### Paid activation

- **Only zero-price plan versions may activate**, enforced structurally in
  PostgreSQL.
- A non-zero selection is **refused outright**. No pending payment intent, no
  dormant paid subscription, no fake checkout, no sandbox shortcut, no partial
  activation.
- Removing the zero-price restriction requires a later **visible migration**
  after #46 and #47 close.
- Paid activation is part of neither `#56a` nor `#56b`.

### Lifecycle

- `active -> superseded | cancelled`.
- Upgrade and downgrade create a **new immutable snapshot** and supersede the
  prior active row in one transaction.
- Cancellation activates or ensures the base workspace, so every seller always
  holds exactly one active subscription.
- Previously issued grants are immutable.

### Grants

- Issued **exactly once at activation**, from the subscription snapshot.
- The grant is written **even when the quantity is zero**, so the activation
  fact stays auditable rather than being inferred from an absent row.
- Deduplicated structurally by `(subscription_id, source, period_index)`.
- No recurring grant generation while no publishable plan version carries an
  approved billing term.
- Expiry columns may exist, but **no production path may write an expiry**
  until the relevant legal/product decision closes.

### Authorization

- New **non-privileged** capability `bc_manage_own_subscription`, granted to the
  `professional` and `business` roles.
- Reading the caller's own subscription requires authentication and ownership.
- Mutation requires authentication, ownership and the capability.
- It is **not** added to `PRIVILEGED_CAPABILITIES`: that list means authority
  over other people's data, and a self-service capability would dilute a meaning
  that is currently exact.
- No staff-affiliation branch may satisfy ownership.

### Audit

- `admin.admin_audit_log` is reused as the **platform** audit trail. No second
  audit engine is built.
- Recorded: automatic assignment, migration backfill, seller selection and
  cancellation, activation, supersession, and grant issuance.
- Reasons are **closed server-generated values**. DTOs must not accept a
  free-text audit reason — user-controlled prose must not reach a table the
  application cannot UPDATE.
- Automatic and migration actors use `actor_label`, never a fabricated human.
- This widening of the audit-log charter is deliberate and must be documented in
  the Story #56 ADR.

### Privacy

- The subscription and grant tables are ADR-027 **`retained`** with explicit
  structural reasons.
- Neither may be claimed `no_subject_data`.
- No new erasure behaviour outside the approved retained disposition.

### Consequence recorded at ratification

ADR-041 §6 stated that `#40b` "must not ship before #46 closes the base-workspace
definition". This decision **supersedes that constraint** for #56 (`#56a`) and #69 (`#56b`).
The reasoning it guarded remains sound and is narrowed rather than dismissed:
nothing consumes booking credits until #58, so a base workspace conferring zero
entitlements changes no seller's capabilities today, and the zero-quantity grant
above makes that an explicit auditable fact instead of an accident.

One consequence survives and is recorded here rather than discovered later:
**sellers backfilled onto the seeded `D-7` version stay on it when #46 publishes
a new `D-7` version.** Assignment happens once per party, and neither `#56a` nor
`#56b` contains a mechanism to move an existing subscription to a newer base
version. Doing so will require its own decision and its own visible migration,
because it changes what a seller already holds.

## `V33-DEC-019` — Seller subscription surface contract

**Contract:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-03 after review of the
Story #69 readiness packet. **Values:** OPEN under #46.

The owner ratified the contract below. It is binding on implementation. It fixes
no price, allowance, seat count, billing term or legal wording, and it activates
nothing in production.

### Why the singular contract was rejected

One user may own both a professional and a business workspace:
`provider.professionals.owner_id` and `business.businesses.owner_id` are
independent unique indexes. A singular `GET /me/subscription` therefore has no
implementable answer for such a caller, and every way of producing one is
forbidden.

The API must **never** select a party by first-in-array, business-first,
professional-first, staff affiliation, array order or plan type. The failure is
silent in both directions: the caller is shown one workspace without being told
another exists, and a mutation changes a workspace the caller did not name.

### Approved route family

```text
POST /api/v1/me/subscriptions/initialization
GET  /api/v1/me/subscriptions
GET  /api/v1/me/subscriptions/:workspaceRef/history
POST /api/v1/me/subscriptions/:workspaceRef/selection
POST /api/v1/me/subscriptions/:workspaceRef/cancellation
GET  /api/v1/me/commercial-plans
```

**Namespace verified, no correction required.** The existing `v1/me/*`
namespaces are `ai`, `analytics`, `availability`, `finance`, `journey`,
`loyalty`, `notifications`, `referral` and `wishlist`; the three controllers on
bare `v1/me` declare only literal sub-paths (`provider`, `reviews`, and
identity's root `@Get()`). No `v1/me/:param` wildcard exists that could capture
these paths — the `:id` routes in `provider.controller.ts` belong to
`v1/providers`. `subscriptions` and `commercial-plans` are both free.

### Initialization is an explicit command, not a hidden mutation

- GET routes are **read-only**. They create no subscription, grant or audit row.
- Initialization is an authenticated `POST`, accepts **no request body**, and
  rejects unknown query or body fields.
- It idempotently ensures the base workspace for **every** seller workspace the
  caller owns, and returns the resulting collection.
- Replay writes nothing and audits nothing.
- Concurrent initialization stays single by database invariant
  (`uq_seller_subscriptions_one_active_per_party`), not by application check.

**This amends `V33-DEC-018`'s "idempotent lazy ensure on first commercial
access".** The mechanism is unchanged — the same idempotent ensure, the same
index — but its trigger moves out of a read and into a named command. A `GET`
that writes makes a retried or prefetched read create audit rows, and no `GET`
route in this repository has ever written.

### `workspaceRef`

- An **opaque routing reference issued only by the server**. It is not a
  `professionalId`, `businessId`, `partyId`, `subscriptionId`, `ownerId` or
  `userId`, and must not expose or encode a raw database identity.
- Clients treat it as an uninterpreted string and must never construct one.

Binding implementation constraints for the execution turn:

- derive it with a **domain-separated HMAC** under a dedicated
  `WORKSPACE_REFERENCE_HMAC_SECRET`;
- the authenticated input includes the owner and the owned party identity;
- return a **fixed-length base64url** value under a closed length and character
  contract;
- on every request, enumerate the caller's **currently owned** workspaces,
  recompute their references, and match with **constant-time comparison**;
- never query a party directly from a caller-supplied reference;
- `workspaceRef` is **not authorization** — live ownership is reverified on
  every request;
- a reference belonging to another caller, a malformed reference and a
  nonexistent reference produce **byte-identical** refusals;
- rotation invalidates outstanding references; clients recover by repeating
  initialization or the collection read, and this must be documented.

**Single-key only.** The repository has no established current/previous-key
convention — `OTP_HMAC_SECRET` is the naming precedent and it is single-key — so
#69 implements one key and documents recovery by re-listing. No key-management
subsystem is invented here.

### Ownership and capability

- Reading requires authentication and **live** ownership.
- Mutation requires authentication, live ownership and
  `bc_manage_own_subscription`.
- Staff affiliation never establishes ownership.
- A user owning both workspaces sees **two entries**, each with its own
  `workspaceRef`. Mutating one leaves the other **byte-identical**.
- Story #44 retains ownership of the future active-session workspace model.

`bc_manage_own_subscription` remains **non-privileged** and is not added to
`PRIVILEGED_CAPABILITIES`.

**The "live capability revocation" acceptance criterion is removed as false.**
`CapabilityGuard` returns early for any capability outside
`PRIVILEGED_CAPABILITIES`, so this one is read from the access token and never
re-checked; a test asserting database-backed live revocation would be asserting
something the guard does not do. What **is** live is ownership, revalidated
inside every request. Capability changes take effect on the existing
access-token lifecycle. Immediate operational suspension, if ever required,
needs its own explicit design and is not invented here.

### Plans

- Authenticated and seller-facing.
- Returns only **published and currently active/selectable** versions. No
  drafts, no retired versions, no internal audit fields, no unpublished
  schedules.
- Price and entitlement values are read from the immutable published version.
- A non-zero plan may be **visible** where the ratified catalogue contract
  permits, but **selection still refuses** it with `paid_activation_unavailable`.
  No payment intent and no dormant subscription is created.

### Idempotency

- Selecting the currently active plan version returns
  `selection_already_applied` and writes no subscription, grant or audit row.
- **Cancelling while the base workspace is already active is an idempotent
  no-op.** It must not cancel the base workspace and recreate another one.
- Replay and concurrency must not create duplicate successor subscriptions.

### The `#56a` concurrency defect, recorded

`SellerSubscriptionService.selectPlanVersion` skips the compare-and-swap when no
active subscription exists: `if (current)` guards the supersede, so two
concurrent first selections both reach a bare insert and
`uq_seller_subscriptions_one_active_per_party` refuses one as an **untranslated
`QueryFailedError`** — an HTTP 500 rather than
`subscription_changed_concurrently`.

#69 repairs it by ensuring the base subscription **transactionally before**
selection, so a row always exists to contend over and the compare-and-swap
always arbitrates. **No schema change is authorized**; if implementation proves
the existing constraints insufficient it stops for review rather than adding
one.

### Refusal vocabulary

The closed vocabulary is preserved. Only states safe for the **authenticated
owner** to know may be distinguished.

Cross-owner, malformed, missing and ineligible workspace references collapse to
**one byte-identical refusal**. `seller_not_eligible` must not reveal whether
another workspace exists.

These may remain distinct, because each describes an outcome the caller can act
on rather than the existence of anything they do not own:

- `plan_version_not_selectable`
- `paid_activation_unavailable`
- `subscription_changed_concurrently`
- `commercial_not_configured`
- `selection_already_applied`

### Audit

- Fixed server-generated reasons only; **no free-text reason** is accepted by
  any DTO or internal API.
- **One** audit record per successful mutation.
- **No** audit row for a refusal, a validation failure, an idempotent replay or
  a no-op cancellation.
- Subscription, grant and audit writes share the **same transaction manager**.

### Privacy

- No response exposes a raw party, owner or audit identity.
- No cross-workspace history: history is scoped by authenticated ownership plus
  one `workspaceRef`.
- No aggregate that leaks another party.
- Forged references remain non-enumerating.

## `V33-DEC-020` — Finance read authorization and the dual-owner surface

**Security and contract:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-04 after
review of the Dual-Owner Finance Audit. Binding on the implementation of #72.
It moves no money, changes no ledger row and settles no commercial value.

### The mistake this decision names

`/api/v1/me/finance` decides *who may read* a financial record by asking *whose
money it is*. Those are different questions, and `ProviderBackedFinancialPartyResolver`
answers both with one lookup.

Beneficiary resolution follows an active `business_staff` affiliation, which is
correct for attribution — ADR-023 §3, and a professional joining a salon
genuinely redirects their earnings. Applied to a read, the same answer hands an
employee the employer's books.

Two defects follow from the single boundary, and they are one bug:

1. **A dual owner is resolved business-first.** `provider.professionals.owner_id`
   and `business.businesses.owner_id` are independent unique indexes, so one
   user may own both. The professional workspace's receivables, settlements and
   ledger are then reachable through no route, and nothing in the response says
   another owned workspace exists.
2. **An affiliated staff professional reads the employing business's financial
   position** — receivable, settlement, outstanding, ledger and every aggregate
   derived from them, including colleagues' and the owner's earnings.

The second is the more serious and was not in the filed issue. Both are read-surface
defects: every row in `financial.*` is correct, correctly attributed and intact.
Nothing was lost and nothing was written wrongly.

### Ruling 1 — staff affiliation is not financial ownership

An affiliated professional must never gain access to the employing business's
receivable, settlement, outstanding balance, ledger, finance summary, or any
aggregate derived from them.

`business-authorization.pg-spec.ts` currently asserts that an affiliated
professional's `mySummary` returns the employer's party. **That case asserts a
security defect and must be corrected during implementation**, not preserved as
a regression guard.

### Ruling 2 — the product consequence is accepted

After correction, an affiliated professional's finance screen shows only their
own professional party's records. That result may legitimately be empty or zero,
because their earnings genuinely belong to the business.

**The server must not fall back to the employer's business finance to avoid an
empty screen.** An empty screen that is true is not a defect.

Manager or staff access to business finance, if it is ever wanted, requires an
explicit scoped role/capability design — likely coordinated with Story #44. It
must never be inferred from a `business_staff` row.

### Ruling 3 — one bug, both defects

Issue #72 covers both. They share one incorrect party-selection boundary and a
fix for either alone would leave that boundary in place. **The issue must not be
split.**

### Ruling 4 — story points

#72 moves from **5 to 8**. The label predated the second defect and the
module-boundary constraint on reusing `workspaceRef`. It stays `status:proposed`.

### Ruling 5 — three concepts, separated structurally

| Concept | Question | Follows affiliation |
|---|---|---|
| Financial beneficiary / seller party | whose money is this? | **yes** — unchanged, ADR-023 §3 |
| Workspace ownership | which workspaces does this user own? | **no** |
| Permission to read finance | who may read these records? | **no** — must use ownership |

Finance reads use **live ownership only**. The rules:

- a business owner may read the owned business's finance;
- a professional owner may read the owned professional's finance;
- a dual owner may read each **separately**;
- staff affiliation grants no finance read access;
- changing affiliation never rewrites historical financial ownership — the
  party is written onto the ledger row and the ledger is append-only;
- **no route accepts a raw owner, user, professional, business or party id.**

### Ruling 6 — approved additive route family

```text
GET /api/v1/me/finance/workspaces
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/summary
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/outstanding-orders
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/settlements
GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/orders/:orderId/ledger
```

Requirements:

- `/workspaces` lists every finance workspace the caller **currently owns**, and
  an empty array — never a `404` — when they own none;
- a `workspaceRef` selects exactly one workspace;
- each workspace's financial data is returned **separately**;
- professional and business totals are **never merged** into one figure;
- every SQL query stays party-scoped at the predicate, never a filter applied to
  already-loaded mixed rows;
- **no direct lookup from a caller-supplied reference** — references are matched
  against recomputed candidates for live-owned parties, never used in a `WHERE`.

### Ruling 7 — the four singular routes stay, corrected

```text
GET /api/v1/me/finance/summary
GET /api/v1/me/finance/outstanding-orders
GET /api/v1/me/finance/settlements
GET /api/v1/me/finance/orders/:orderId/ledger
```

Their party resolution changes from beneficiary to ownership. Behaviour by
caller:

| Owned finance workspaces | Response |
|---|---|
| exactly one | the existing successful shape, byte-for-byte where feasible |
| zero | the existing non-enumerating not-found/not-yours refusal |
| more than one | `409` with the new closed code `finance_workspace_selection_required` |

**The server must never silently select** the first, business-first,
professional-first or affiliation-derived party. That is the defect, and a
compatibility layer that preserved it would preserve the bug.

**Compatibility and deprecation boundary.** The singular routes are retained by
this decision and **are not removed in #72**. The in-repository web client
migrates to the workspace-aware routes in the same pull request, so no user
meets the `409`; it exists for clients this repository cannot see. Retiring the
singular routes is a separate decision, not taken here, and requires evidence
that no client depends on them.

### Ruling 8 — `workspaceRef` is reused exactly, and extracted

#72 reuses Story #69's reference with **no observable change**: the same
`WORKSPACE_REFERENCE_HMAC_SECRET`, the same domain prefix, the same
43-character base64url output, the same length-prefixed HMAC construction, the
same owner/party inputs, the same constant-time ownership resolution. **No new
secret, no changed output, no rotation.** Existing #69 references must remain
byte-identical after the extraction, and a test must prove it.

The primitive moves into a `scope:shared` library or equivalent
domain-neutral boundary, because `@nx/enforce-module-boundaries` restricts
`scope:financial` to `scope:shared` alone — importing `commercial-policy` from
`financial` is a lint error, and duplicating the construction is forbidden.

The shared primitive:

- queries **no** database;
- imports **neither** commercial-policy **nor** financial;
- accepts **already-resolved** owned parties as an argument;
- **never performs authorization itself.**

`commercial-policy` and `financial` each retain responsibility for enumerating
their caller's live-owned parties. The reference is a routing handle that is
matched, never a credential that is believed.

### Ruling 9 — `bc_view_own_finance` is not enforced in #72

The audit established that the capability exists, is granted to the
`professional` and `business` roles in the database, and is enforced on no
route — while `AccountResolverService` normally assigns only the default
`customer` role, and no complete production role-grant lifecycle makes the
capability reliably present in a seller's access token.

**Enforcing it now would lock legitimate sellers out of their own finance.**

For #72 the security boundary is exactly three things:

1. an authenticated caller;
2. live ownership;
3. workspace-scoped SQL predicates.

No documentation, comment, test or response may claim `bc_view_own_finance`
enforcement or live revocation for this surface.

### Ruling 10 — the role lifecycle gap is a separate bug

Recorded as **#75**, in V3.3. It is not implemented as part of #72.

The gap is wider than finance: the seller capabilities exist and are granted to
the `professional` and `business` roles, but account resolution assigns only the
default `customer` role and no self-service path grants either seller role. Any
future story that enforces an existing seller capability would deny legitimate
sellers on the day it ships. A fix must tie role assignment to authoritative
ownership or verification, define revocation and token-invalidation semantics
explicitly, and grant no employer authority from a `business_staff` row.

### Ruling 11 — no migration

#72 requires no schema migration. It must not alter financial ledger tables,
rewrite historical financial parties, create workspace persistence, change
subscriptions or grants, create a new capability, or move or recalculate money.

### Ruling 12 — one refusal

For the workspace-aware routes these are **byte-identical**: malformed, random,
foreign, stale, no-longer-owned and nonexistent `workspaceRef`.

No response may reveal whether the HMAC was valid, whether the party exists,
whether another user owns it, or another workspace's balance, row count or
history.

### Ruling 13 — financial correctness

- balances remain calculated from the existing financial ledger;
- ledger data is **not** copied or rewritten;
- professional and business totals never merge;
- pagination cursors are scoped to the `workspaceRef` and its party, so a cursor
  stolen from one workspace is invalid for another;
- currency and settlement states keep their existing semantics;
- all filtering happens **in SQL**, never after loading mixed rows into memory.

### Ruling 14 — no write of any kind

#72 contains read routes only. It writes no admin audit row, financial ledger
row, settlement row, subscription or grant row, event or notification.

## `V33-DEC-021` — Seller role and capability lifecycle

**Security and contract:** CLOSED by the product owner on 2026-09-04 after review
of the Seller Role and Capability Lifecycle readiness audit. Binding on the
implementation of #75. It moves no money, changes no ledger row, grants no
administrative authority and activates nothing in production.

### Ruling 1 — #75 is an active defect, not a latent one

Story #69 enforces `bc_manage_own_subscription` on three mutations —
initialization, plan selection and cancellation — while production account
creation grants only the default `customer` role and no self-service path grants
`professional` or `business`.

A genuine seller who owns a professional profile or a business is therefore
refused `403` on all three, permanently, until an administrator manually grants
them a role. Because the capability is deliberately non-privileged there is no
live database re-check to consult, and because no production path writes the
role there would be nothing for one to find. The seller also never receives the
base `D-7` workspace, since initialization is its only trigger.

**The absence of a subscription screen in the in-repository web client does not
make this latent.** The routes are mounted unconditionally, behind no rollout
flag, entitlement gate or kill switch. The defect is on the deployed API and is
reachable by any client.

#75's "Impact today — No user-visible breakage" is superseded by this ruling.

### Ruling 2 — the professional trigger is ownership, not verification

The `professional` role is granted **atomically when an authenticated user
successfully creates and owns a professional profile.**

The role represents ownership of a professional workspace. Onboarding and
profile management may legitimately be required *before* verification, so gating
the role on verification would require the role to earn the role.

Verification keeps every job it already has: it governs public visibility and
every verification-dependent operation, and those predicates are unchanged. The
role alone never bypasses a route-level ownership or state check.

The role is **not** granted by:

- submitting verification evidence;
- searching or viewing a professional;
- joining a business as staff or manager;
- any caller-supplied role or owner field.

### Ruling 3 — the business trigger is ownership

The `business` role is granted **atomically when an authenticated user
successfully creates and owns a business.**

It is never derived from `business_staff`, from a manager or staff affiliation,
from a professional affiliation, from a client-supplied role, or from a
client-supplied business or owner id.

### Ruling 4 — roles are additive and `customer` is never removed

Valid combinations are `customer`; `customer` + `professional`; `customer` +
`business`; and `customer` + `professional` + `business`.
`provider.professionals.owner_id` and `business.businesses.owner_id` are
independent unique indexes, so the dual owner is real and both roles are
granted.

**No automatic path may remove the default `customer` role.**
`bc_book_service`, `bc_use_ai_assistant` and `bc_view_own_orders` belong to
`customer` alone, so a grant that replaced roles rather than adding them would
strip every seller of booking, the assistant and their own order history.

### Ruling 5 — a role is never sufficient authorization

Every seller operation retains all of:

- authentication;
- the required capability where the route declares one;
- **live ownership** of the target professional or business;
- any relevant verification, publication or lifecycle predicate.

A role granted without ownership confers no self-scoped authority: the ownership
resolver enumerates no party and the operation finds nothing to act on.

**Ownership checks must not be weakened to make a role test pass.** A test that
requires a loosened predicate is testing the wrong thing.

### Ruling 6 — staff affiliation grants no global role

A `business_staff` row grants no global `business` role. This holds for
`manager` exactly as it holds for `staff` — the same boundary `V33-DEC-020`
Ruling 1 drew for finance, and it must not be reintroduced here by a role grant.

A staff professional keeps their independently owned `professional` role.
Joining or leaving a business grants and revokes nothing.

**Story #44 retains ownership of business-scoped staff roles and permissions.**
#75 introduces no business-scoped capability, no staff-derived role and no new
`business_staff.role` value.

### Ruling 7 — backfill is idempotent and reads ownership only

Existing eligible owners are backfilled. Eligibility comes from authoritative
ownership and nothing else:

| Fact | Grant |
|---|---|
| non-deleted professional row owned by the user | `professional` |
| non-deleted business row owned by the user | `business` |
| ownership of both | **both** |

The backfill must preserve `customer`; must never read `business_staff`; must
never grant `business` to a staff-only user; must not duplicate `user_roles`;
must remain safe under concurrent application activity; must rely on the
existing unique constraints rather than an application-level check; and must
record audit evidence through the established migration/system mechanism —
without inventing a second audit engine.

The exact deleted, suspended and verification predicates are **verified against
the live schema before implementation**. No suspension state is invented: the
audit found `suspended` and `revoked` present in the verification state machine
with no caller, and an unreachable state must not acquire speculative behaviour
here.

### Ruling 8 — ownership and role commit together

New ownership and its matching role assignment commit in **one transaction**,
through a narrow composition-root port that accepts and uses the caller's
transaction manager.

Forbidden: opening another pool connection; emitting an event to grant the role
later; a committed ownership row without its required role; a role row surviving
a failed ownership transaction.

There is no eventual window in which ownership exists and the role does not. If
atomicity proves impossible it is **reported as a finding**, never hidden behind
an event.

### Ruling 9 — grant timing follows the existing token lifecycle

The role row persists immediately. An already-issued access token is not
rewritten. The new role and its capabilities become effective at the caller's
**next successful access-token issuance**, through refresh or login.

Forbidden: claiming immediate capability activation in an existing JWT; adding
database-live checks for every non-privileged capability; invalidating all
sessions; minting tokens from a professional or business domain service.

Clients use the ordinary auth refresh lifecycle before invoking newly
capability-gated seller operations. If a narrow frontend change is required to
trigger that existing refresh flow, it is identified during implementation and
kept minimal.

### Ruling 10 — no new revocation machinery

#75 creates none. The security that already exists is stated rather than
extended:

- **loss or deletion of ownership blocks self-scoped access immediately**,
  through the live ownership predicate, even while a stale token still carries
  the role;
- manual removal of a non-privileged role becomes effective in newly issued
  access tokens, per the existing lifecycle;
- privileged capabilities keep their existing live re-check;
- verification status continues to be checked by the operations that require it.

**No document, comment, test or response may claim live revocation for a
non-privileged capability.** Lifecycle cases that cannot occur in current
production code — professional or business deletion, ownership transfer,
suspension, verification revocation — remain explicit non-goals rather than
speculative implementations.

### Ruling 11 — no role provenance in #75

No provenance column and no assignment-source table.

Live ownership is the real authority, so a manually granted seller role without
ownership grants no access. The defect needs deterministic owner-role creation
and a backfill, not a multi-source authorization model, and #44 will define
future scoped staff authority. Provenance becomes necessary only if multiple
independent grant sources must coexist or carry different revocation semantics.

Recorded as a standing constraint:

- **no code may begin treating the global seller role alone as ownership;**
- any future non-ownership-scoped use of these roles requires a new decision;
- a future provenance model must be additive and migration-backed.

### Ruling 12 — the administrator seed anomaly is recorded, not changed

`administrator` carries `bc_manage_own_profile`, seeded by
`20260824100001_create_roles_and_capabilities.sql` and mirrored in
`CAPABILITIES_BY_ROLE`. It is harmless **only** because ownership remains
mandatory on every route that could use it.

#75 neither expands nor removes administrator capabilities unless the audit
proves an immediate violation. It adds one regression test: an administrator
holding no professional ownership cannot use that capability to read or mutate
another professional's profile.

### Ruling 13 — ADR-023 is amended, not reversed

ADR-023's "`owner` is not a `business_staff` role" section states that V3 never
grants these identity roles dynamically. That statement is superseded to exactly
this extent, using the repository's amendment convention and preserving the
original text:

- beneficiary resolution **may** follow affiliation (ADR-023 §3, unchanged);
- seller workspace authorization follows **ownership**;
- owner role assignment follows **ownership creation**;
- `business_staff` never grants a `business` role.

Nothing else in ADR-023 changes. Its financial-party model, its consent
structure and its one-active-affiliation invariant all stand.

### Ruling 14 — story boundary

**#75 includes:** the professional owner role grant; the business owner role
grant; the existing-owner backfill; transaction-safe composition ports; accurate
token-timing documentation; adversarial role and ownership tests; and any
minimal existing-client refresh adaptation required for reachability.

**#75 excludes:** scoped staff roles; role provenance; business ownership
transfer; new suspension workflows; universal live capability lookup; changes to
`PRIVILEGED_CAPABILITIES`; finance changes; and payment, settlement or external
providers.

### Ruling 15 — story points

#75 moves from **5 to 8**. The label predated the finding that the defect is
active, the two-domain trigger and the backfill. It stays `status:proposed`.

**It is not split.** The professional and business grants share one coherent
ownership-to-role invariant, and splitting them would produce two answers to
"when does a seller become a seller" — the same reasoning `V33-DEC-020` Ruling 3
applied to #72.

### Ruling 16 — the execution gate carries forward

`V33-DEC-020` Ruling 15's gate applies unchanged: targeted fast tests, targeted
real-PostgreSQL tests, the complete fast suite, the complete real-PostgreSQL
suite with zero skipped, typecheck/lint/build, clean isolated migration and
idempotency, role verification, restore rehearsal, production image and
container, route and log controls — and only then a pull request.

**CI must not be the first complete-suite run.** #75 adds one browser check: a
real seller completes profile creation and reaches a working subscription
surface without an administrator touching their account.

meaning "the collected amount", and the ledger currently posts the order total
as the receivable.

**`#41c` may not reinterpret `OrderPaid` without a separate binding decision.**
Its contract meaning today is that the order's whole total was captured, and a
deposit capture does not satisfy that.

### Ruling 8 — `#41d` owns publication and stays gated

`#41d` owns the database-backed administrator publication and selection surface:
immutable policy versions, activation windows, privileged and audited
publication, seller and service selection, and fail-closed lookup when no

It is blocked by the open decisions under #46 — `V33-DEC-011`, `V33-DEC-012`,
and any customer copy required by `V33-DEC-017`. It contains no production

### Ruling 9 — #47's gate is narrow, and was already narrowed

The *External-gate scope correction* of 2026-09-02 already ruled that #47 blocks
real collection and settlement, not deterministic contract, schema and sandbox

**#47 gates real provider collection, settlement and production activation
only. It does not gate `#41a`, and it does not gate deterministic sandbox

### Ruling 10 — what stays outside this closure

Reschedule money treatment, cancellation and no-show outcomes, dispute
