## 3. Ready-to-use Claude Design prompts

Each prompt is self-contained. They share three rules, repeated in every prompt because a designer working from one prompt will not read the others.

### Prompt A — Admin: booking-outcome policy publication

> Design the administrator surface for BeauClick's **booking-outcome policy family** (V3.3 story `#42a`, ADR-051 §1 and §5). Persian, RTL, consistent with screens `40_ADMIN_COMMERCIAL_CATALOGUE` and `44_ADMIN_CONTROL_PLANE` in the existing snapshot.
>
> This surface publishes **ranges and sets a seller will later choose inside** — never a value that binds a booking directly. Three related families live on it:
>
> 1. **Outcome policy versions.** A policy key has versions with a `draft → published → retired` lifecycle, one way, never backwards. A version carries: allowed cancellation cutoff hours (a set, e.g. 6/12/48), allowed late-cancellation retention options (each `none`, a percentage in basis points, a fixed Toman amount, or "full collected"), allowed no-show grace minutes (a set), allowed no-show retention options (same shapes), a free-reschedule count before cutoff, a dispute window in hours, a bodily-harm window in hours, an appeal window in hours, an optional case-file retention in days, and an optional **legal cap** which may only be published against a qualifying Legal-evidence record.
> 2. **Customer policy copy.** Persian (`fa-IR`) text a customer accepts at checkout, versioned with the same lifecycle. Exactly one copy version is active platform-wide at any instant.
> 3. **Legal evidence register.** Records with a key, a subject (`retention_cap` or `policy_copy`), a reference kind, a reference and a summary; one-way lifecycle `recorded → retired`.
>
> **Hard constraints, all enforced by the server — the design must not contradict them:**
> - Every mutation requires a **stated reason**; there is no unexplained change.
> - A published version's terms are immutable. "Editing" a live policy means publishing a new version; show that, do not offer an edit affordance on a published row.
> - Two versions of one key can never be effective at the same instant. Publishing while another is live is refused.
> - The activation instant comes from the server, never from a date picker the administrator fills in. There is no "publish retroactively".
> - A legal cap without qualifying evidence is refused; the refusal names no detail beyond "not qualifying".
> - Nothing is seeded: on a fresh platform every list here is legitimately **empty**, and the empty state is a first-class screen, not an error.
>
> Design: the list of policy keys; a version timeline showing draft/published/retired and the effective window; the version editor (draft only); the publish and retire confirmations, each demanding a reason; the copy family; the evidence register; and the empty states. Show what a refused publication looks like.

### Prompt B — Seller: outcome-policy selection

> Design the **seller's** side of BeauClick's booking-outcome policy (V3.3 story `#42b`/#159, ADR-051 §3). Persian, RTL, consistent with `42_SELLER_COMMERCIAL_AND_OPERATIONS`.
>
> The seller does not write policy. The administrator publishes allowed **ranges and sets**; the seller picks one member of each, per workspace. The server answers `GET /api/v1/me/outcome-policies` with what this seller may choose, and `GET|PUT /api/v1/me/outcome-policy-assignments/:workspaceRef` reads and replaces the current selection.
>
> **Hard constraints:**
> - A seller may own several workspaces (a professional profile and a business). Every workspace is addressed by an **opaque `workspaceRef`**, never by a name or an id the seller could type, and **no workspace is ever pre-selected** — "which workspace?" is always an explicit question.
> - The selection is a **choice inside a published set**, so the UI offers exactly the allowed members and nothing else. A free-text or numeric field here would be wrong.
> - A selection is **superseded, never edited**: choosing again creates a new current selection and the previous one becomes history. Bookings already taken keep the terms they were taken under.
> - If the administrator re-publishes a narrower range that no longer contains the seller's member, new bookings **fail closed** until the seller selects again. Design that state: it is a real, reachable screen, not an error page.
> - A seller with no selection is not broken; they are on the legacy path. Say so plainly rather than implying misconfiguration.
>
> Design: the workspace chooser; the selection screen with its allowed members; the confirmation of what the customer will be shown as a result; the history of past selections; the fail-closed state; and the "not enrolled" state.

### Prompt C — No-show declaration and customer remedy

> Design two connected moments in BeauClick's booking flow (V3.3 story `#42d`/#161, ADR-051 §7–§8). Persian, RTL.
>
> **1. The seller declares a no-show.** `POST /api/v1/bookings/:id/no-show`. Permitted only after `slot start + the grace minutes the booking's terms carry`, and only for a confirmed booking. The declaration is **immutable and evidence-minimal**: one actor, one instant, and an optional statement of at most 2000 characters. There is deliberately **no photo, no location and no health field** — the API rejects them. A declaration moves no money by itself; it opens the customer's objection window.
>
> **2. The customer chooses a remedy.** `POST /api/v1/bookings/:id/remedy`, after a cancellation that was *not* the customer's. Exactly one remedy is offered and it is **already resolved to the default — a full refund**. The customer may override it to a **free reschedule**, but only while the refund is still pending. Once the refund has executed, the choice is closed and repeating the request returns the existing resolution rather than an error.
>
> **Hard constraints:**
> - The default is not a suggestion the customer must confirm — it is already in effect. The screen must not imply that doing nothing loses them money.
> - The override window is bounded by the refund's own state, not by a countdown the client can compute. Show the state, not a timer the server does not honour.
> - A repeat request is a no-op that returns the same answer. Design the second visit, not only the first.
> - The seller's statement is optional, and the UI must not push for one: an evidence-minimal record is a deliberate privacy property.
>
> Design: the seller's declaration entry point and its "too early" state; the confirmation that spells out what a declaration does and does not do; the customer's notification; the remedy screen with the default already applied; the reschedule override; and the closed state after the refund executed.

### Prompt D — Admin: commission policy publication

> Design the administrator surface for BeauClick's **commission policy family** (V3.3 story `#43b-1`/#173, ADR-052 §1). Persian, RTL, consistent with `40_ADMIN_COMMERCIAL_CATALOGUE`.
>
> The platform has **no commission number in its code** — by design, the rate is whatever an administrator publishes here. There are exactly three components: **booking commission**, **acquisition**, and **processing recovery**, each with exactly one policy key.
>
> A version takes one of **four closed shapes**, and the shape decides which fields exist:
> - **zero** — charges nothing. Carries no rate, no amount, no base. It is a *published decision*, and is not the same thing as no policy at all.
> - **percentage** — a rate in basis points (0–10000) **and** a base.
> - **fixed** — a flat Toman amount strictly above zero. No base.
> - **hybrid** — a flat amount (which may be zero) **plus** a rate and a base.
>
> The **base** is either "the amount the platform actually collected" or "the service total". The two differ by exactly what the customer has not paid yet, so the choice is consequential and must never be defaulted or pre-filled.
>
> **Hard constraints:**
> - `draft → published → retired`, one way. A published rule is immutable; publish a new version instead.
> - Two versions of one component can never be effective at once.
> - The publication instant is the server's, to the microsecond. There is no date picker for it, and no retroactive publication.
> - Every mutation requires a stated reason.
> - Nothing is seeded: on a fresh platform all three components are legitimately unpublished, and that is a normal state, not an incident.
> - This surface is administrator-only. What a *seller* is eventually told about commission is a separate, unratified surface — do not design it here.
>
> Design: the three components at a glance with their currently effective rule; the version timeline per component; the shape-aware version editor (the form changes shape with the rule kind, and the four shapes are the only reachable states); publish/retire confirmations with their reason; the refusal when a publication would overlap; and the unpublished empty state.

### Prompt E — Amendment to `46_FINANCE_WORKSPACE`

> Amend the existing screen specification `46_FINANCE_WORKSPACE` (do **not** create a sibling screen) for BeauClick's new per-state funds read (V3.3 `#43a` and #185, ADR-052 §14 and §16). Persian, RTL.
>
> The screen today shows three legacy figures: net receivable, settled, and outstanding. A new route, `GET /api/v1/me/finance/:workspaceRef/funds`, returns **twelve distinct fields** in Toman, and the governing decision requires that **no field mixes seller money with platform money**:
>
> `pending`, `disputed`, `available`, `reserve`, `settled`, `refunded`, `collected`, `platformAdvance`, `recoveredIn` — and, kept visibly apart as platform-side figures — `platformEarned`, `providerFee`, `recoveryOut`.
>
> **Hard constraints:**
> - The twelve are **separate fields by rule**. Do not invent a total that sums across the seller/platform boundary; a single "balance" number would violate the decision the backend enforces.
> - Both regimes coexist: orders collected before the journal existed live in the legacy figures, newer ones in these states. The screen must make it obvious which question each number answers, without implying the two add up.
> - All-zero is the correct, common answer for a workspace with no new-regime collection yet. It is not an empty state and not an error.
> - The reader may be the owner **or** a bookkeeper holding a read-only `finance_read` grant. They see byte-for-byte the same figures — there is no richer owner view — and no identifier of any party appears.
> - The response is never cached (`private, no-store`), so the design must not promise offline or stale-while-revalidate behaviour.
>
> Deliver: the amended section of screen 46, showing how the twelve states are grouped and labelled, how the seller/platform boundary is made visible, the all-zero state, and how the legacy figures coexist without suggesting arithmetic between the two regimes.

## 4. Suggested order

1. **Prompt E** — smallest, and it completes a screen whose implementation is already the highest-value frontend work outstanding.
2. **Prompt C** — customer- and seller-facing, and the only one of the four that a real booking hits today.
3. **Prompt B** — unlocks the seller side of the outcome policy that `#42c`/`#42d` already enforce.
4. **Prompt A** — the largest admin surface (three families, 22 routes).
5. **Prompt D** — admin-only and newest; nothing downstream waits on it until a rate is actually published.

## 5. What does NOT need design yet

- **Dispute and appeal (`#42e`/#162)** — backend not built, and `gate:legal`.
- **Completed-booking objection (`#42f`/#180)** — same.
- **Settlement execution, provider fees, paid credit activation (`#43e`, `#43g`, #99, #47)** — all `gate:external`; designing a screen for a rail that does not exist would fix choices before the constraints are known.
- **The commission snapshot (`#43b-2`/#192)** — it writes a row inside checkout and has no surface at all, by design.

# 4. Outcome — the pack came back, and what the review found

**Claude Design delivered all five prompts on 2026-09-19. Reviewed and accepted 2026-09-20.**

The pack lives on the design branch at
`docs/design/v3.3-gap-pack/` (design/claude-design `958a95c`): five prototypes, five specs
(`47`–`50` plus `46_FINANCE_WORKSPACE_AMENDMENT.md`), Claude Design's own `MANIFEST.md`, and the
reviewer's `REVIEW.md`. The canonical snapshot was not touched.

Every factual claim in the pack was matched against the code it describes rather than read for
plausibility. The twelve funds field names, the 22 admin outcome-policy routes, the 9
commission-policy routes, the three commission components, the four closed shapes, the two bases
and the 1–2000 statement bound are all **exact**. No prototype invents a rate, a cadence or a cap;
all five are self-contained with no external reference.

**Three corrections** were recorded in the specs themselves — the funds boundary has three sides
rather than two, the no-show statement is required for a governed booking, and the seller's
selection carries one required free-text `reason`. `REVIEW.md` states each against the code that
contradicted the spec.

## 4.1 One gap this review opened, and it is ours

**Screen 49 cannot be implemented against today's API**, and that changes the position §2 recorded.

`POST /bookings/:id/no-show` and `POST /bookings/:id/remedy` are the only routes in the family.
Nothing reads back a no-show declaration, its grace snapshot, the objection window, the remedy
resolution or the refund's execution status, and `toBookingShape` carries none of them. Eight of
screen 49's twelve states therefore have no data source, and the "declaration not yet permitted"
state needs an eligibility answer ADR-051 forbids the client from computing.

This is a **backend gap, not a design defect**. It is also **not gated** — no `gate:external`, no
`gate:legal` — which means the claim that the executable backend is exhausted for this milestone
was true only of what was then visible. A read route for the `#42d` family is buildable now, and
screen 49 is blocked until it exists.

**Screens 46, 47, 48 and 50 are implementable today** and frontend work starts there.
